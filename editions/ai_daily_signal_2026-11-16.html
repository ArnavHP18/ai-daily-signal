ULA's Vulcan rocket fails minutes after launch, destroying all 40 Amazon Leo satellites

The first of 38 Amazon launches ends in ULA's first mission failure; the Pentagon's second rocket is likely grounded.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKTwo or more outletsChecked on Ars Technica and The Verge (ULA's and Amazon's statements, time, booster-stage detail) and ULA's own mission page (40 satellites, first of 38 Amazon launches); ULA's failure statement itself was posted on X, which we did not open
United Launch Alliance's Vulcan lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 12:40 pm EDT on 15 Nov carrying 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites, and failed about 10 minutes into flight; all 40 were lost (Ars Technica, The Verge). ULA said the rocket "suffered an anomaly" and "failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit", that it did not yet have data on the cause, and that "today, we did not meet that standard." Ars Technica's sources say the fault was in the booster stage, powered by two Blue Origin BE-4 engines, not the upper stage or the six solid boosters. The Verge reports it as ULA's first mission failure in 20 years. It was the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has bought; Amazon said ULA has been a trusted partner and it expects ULA to learn from the flight.

So what: Vulcan carries about half of the US military's planned national-security launches and most of Amazon's plan to catch Starlink. An indefinite grounding leaves SpaceX, which caught its Starship upper stage a day earlier, even more dominant, and pushes Amazon Leo's rollout back again.

Vulcan's six solid rocket boosters, which had failed on two of its four earlier flights, worked this time. The trouble came later: Ars Technica's sources say the core booster stage, powered by two methane-burning BE-4 engines from Blue Origin, had a problem before the 10-minute mark when ULA says the anomaly occurred. ULA has not said what failed and says customers will see the investigation.

The loss matters well beyond one launch. Vulcan is one of two rockets certified for US national-security missions, and Amazon had booked 38 Vulcan flights to build Leo, its Starlink rival, which ULA's mission page says will start with more than 3,000 satellites. Ars expects the rocket to be grounded for an indefinite period while the cause is found.

#2Banking · settlement · 13 NovIMPACT 7/10

Three central banks show FX trades settling across different systems at once

The Bank of England, the Monetary Authority of Singapore and the Bank of Thailand published the results of Project Meridian FX APAC on 13 Nov. A prototype synchronisation operator built by Partior coordinated movements of central bank money so that both legs of an FX trade settle together (payment-versus-payment), and also ran multilateral net FX settlement, across test systems that included both real-time gross settlement (RTGS) and distributed-ledger platforms. The banks say it worked across different technologies and time zones, and that netting let banks use liquidity more efficiently. The BoE plans a live synchronisation interface in 2028.

So what: FX settlement risk (paying one currency and not receiving the other) is one of the oldest problems in cross-border banking. If synchronisation goes live, treasury and payments teams could settle Asian-currency trades in central bank money without a single shared ledger; watch for the BoE's 2028 interface specification.

#3Security · AI supply chain · 15 NovIMPACT 6/10

Fireworks AI hack spreads to Uber: drivers' licence data stolen

Uber is warning drivers that hackers stole their personal and driver's licence details from a vendor, which TechCrunch identified, and Uber confirmed, as AI inference provider Fireworks AI (via TNW). Uber says Fireworks told it on 9 Oct that internal credentials had been stolen. Notices to state attorneys general list 2,567 people in Massachusetts, 3,430 in South Carolina and 1,873 in Rhode Island, 7,870 so far; California and Illinois residents were notified without a count. Fireworks, valued at $17.5bn in July, has disclosed two October incidents and blames a group that sells stolen access to AI models ("LLMjacking"). It is the same breach Cursor says led to its tampered Claude Code extensions (Developing).

So what: a model-hosting vendor turned out to hold identity documents. Banks using third-party inference should ask what customer data their AI vendors keep, for how long, and whether it sits next to credentials attackers can resell.

#7

OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra helped prove a step toward defining the Standard Model on a lattice

NOTABLEOfficial sourceResearch · physics · 15 Nov
OpenAI opened a science blog on 15 Nov with a post on how GPT-6 Astra, its unreleased next model, "helped turn the idea into a detailed proof" of a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model (OpenAI's feed summary; the page itself refused our request). The physics is in arXiv paper 2610.09093 by physicist Nathaniel Craig (6 Oct): it builds a gauge-invariant measure for overlap fermions on four-dimensional lattices for chiral gauge theories that include the Standard Model, a long-standing obstacle to simulating the theory from first principles. The paper (123 pages) comes with a Lean formalisation and calculation records.

So what: after maths results in August, this is the first time OpenAI has tied Astra to a frontier physics proof. The claim to check is how much the model contributed versus the author; the Lean files make parts of it checkable.

Starling's founder is building Lockwood, a bank designed for AI agents

MONITORTwo or more outletsBanking · agents · 15 Nov
Anne Boden, who founded Starling, is building Lockwood, a bank she says will be "built by agents, to serve customers that want to use agents, and then be defended by agents" (Sunday Times interview, reported by Sifted and Dealroom). Customers would give each AI agent a spending limit, for example a £100 monthly grocery cap for a shopping assistant, and Lockwood would refuse anything outside it. Boden is funding it herself for now and targets a million customers in year one.

So what: agent spending limits are becoming a product, not just a risk control (see ChatGPT Wallet and Wells Fargo's new terms). Incumbent banks will need per-agent mandates of their own.
