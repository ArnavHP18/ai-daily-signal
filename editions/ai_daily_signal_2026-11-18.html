Java4Shell: a flaw in Java's Base64 decoder lets attackers take over servers remotely

Default installs of Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Jenkins, Artifactory and PingFederate can be hijacked without a password on AVX-512 servers; Oracle and OpenJDK patched on Tuesday.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceChecked on Calif's disclosure and the OpenJDK advisory for 17 Nov (opened), plus BleepingComputer, CyberScoop and Aikido's version table; no exploitation in the wild reported yet; NVD had no entry when opened
Security firm Calif disclosed Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938) on 17 Nov: a memory-corruption bug in the hand-written assembly OpenJDK uses to speed up Base64 decoding on chips with AVX-512 VBMI. A string of 64 'A' characters followed by '=' (128 on older Java 17) makes the decoder write 64 bytes into a 50-byte buffer. Calif turned that into memory leaks in the style of Heartbleed and remote code execution without credentials in default setups of Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, JFrog Artifactory, Jenkins and PingFederate, and with a login in Kafka, Solr and Elasticsearch. It affects OpenJDK 17.0.6 and later and builds made from it: Oracle Java SE, Amazon Corretto, Eclipse Temurin, Microsoft, Azul, SAP, BellSoft and Red Hat. ARM servers are not affected. OpenJDK's advisory scores it 8.1 (CVSS 3.1); Calif scores it 9.2 (CVSS 4.0). Oracle shipped fixed builds on 17 Nov (e.g. 17.0.21.1, 21.0.13.1, 25.0.5.1); Aikido says some other distributions had no confirmed fix yet. Stop-gap: start Java with -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics or -XX:UseAVX=2 on x86-64 only. There is no universal exploit: an attacker must find a way to get crafted Base64 into each application. Calif says it found the bug with AI help; the NYT reported it used OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber.

So what: Java runs most bank back ends, login servers (Keycloak, PingFederate) and build pipelines (Jenkins, Artifactory). Treat this like Log4Shell: inventory x86 hosts with AVX-512, patch or set the JVM flag today, and ask vendors for their fixed builds.

OFFICIALCalif: Java4Shell disclosure · OpenJDK vulnerability advisory, 17 NovREPORTINGBleepingComputer · CyberScoopOTHERAikido: affected and fixed versions
Storyline: AI finding and fixing bugs · day 26

Java4Shell is the second big 'AI-assisted' bug in a week after Apache's mod_http2 flaw, and it sits in the most widely deployed enterprise runtime. The fault is not in Java code but in a hardware-specific shortcut: on x86-64 chips with AVX-512 VBMI, OpenJDK decodes Base64 with hand-written assembly, and a malformed final block makes it compute a negative length and overwrite memory past a buffer. Base64 is everywhere, in login tokens, API payloads and headers, which is why default installs of identity and DevOps tools are reachable before login.

Patching is complicated by the many Java builds in use. Oracle's fixed releases shipped on 17 Nov; check each vendor (Corretto, Temurin, Azul, Red Hat, Microsoft, SAP) separately. Where a patch is not yet available, the JVM flags -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics or -XX:UseAVX=2 remove the vulnerable path on x86-64, at some performance cost; they must not be applied to ARM hosts, where the JVM will fail to start.

Nvidia doubles revenue to $115.8bn as its board says Huang will not return

CRITICALOfficial sourceHardware · Nvidia · 17 Nov
WHAT CHANGED Q3 revenue $115.8bn (+103%), Q4 guide $130bn; the board formally says it will not reinstate Huang; US confirms the Megaspeed export investigation.
Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8bn, up 103% from a year earlier and 20% from the second quarter, with net income of $64.9bn and gross margin of 74.9% (Nvidia). It guided to $130bn (±3%) for the fourth quarter, which has an extra week, assuming no data-centre revenue from China. Chair Harvey Jones told analysts "the board does not intend to reinstate" Jensen Huang, hours after Huang said he would be "honoured" to return (BBC). Interim CEO Colette Kress told investors "we've got this"; shares rose almost 4% after hours. Kress said Nvidia will be "disciplined and selective" in supporting neoclouds. Separately, the Justice and Commerce departments confirmed they are investigating Megaspeed, suspected of helping ship Nvidia chips to China (BBC), and Nvidia warned chip buyers of legal risks tied to China access (CNBC).

So what: the business is not slowing, so the risk for investors is governance and export enforcement, not demand; banks with Nvidia-linked data-centre loans (JPMorgan and Citi lent $2.8bn involving Megaspeed, The Information reports) should watch the investigation.

Part of Russia's Crimean bridge collapses, cutting road and rail links to Crimea

NOTABLETwo or more outletsWorld · Ukraine · 17 Nov
WHAT CHANGED An arch has collapsed, cutting Crimea's direct road and rail links; the Kremlin had called the damage minor.
Footage shows an arched section of the Kerch bridge, Russia's road and rail link to occupied Crimea, collapsed into the water after Ukraine's strike, cutting the peninsula's direct road and rail links with Russia until it can be repaired (BBC). Yesterday the Kremlin had called the damage minor. Zelensky described a seven-stage operation that first destroyed air defences, radars and electronic-warfare systems, using several drone types and Flamingo and Neptune cruise missiles. A land corridor through occupied Zaporizhzhia and Kherson remains, though Kyiv has been hitting it too; the WSJ says the strike 'severs part' of the bridge.

So what: losing the bridge raises Russia's cost of supplying Crimea and gives Kyiv leverage before any talks at this week's summits.
