■ Today's Lead · New · Day 5 of the Gemini-4 arc

Gemini 4 Argon arrives, but cyber defenders get it first. Google claims the benchmark lead while its own staff question the coding

On Wednesday Google unveiled Gemini 4 Argon, its long-awaited next-generation model. It is going first to a small group of cybersecurity partners in its Fairwind Program and to the US government. Paid API customers and AI Ultra subscribers come next, "as soon as possible" (Axios, TechCrunch, TNW). Google says it was trained for defensive cyber work and can "autonomously find, validate, and patch critical software vulnerabilities". It also claims state of the art on long-horizon software engineering and knowledge work, and a 1M-token output limit. Sundar Pichai: "Argon has frontier safeguards and we are rolling it out responsibly" (Pichai on X). The same day, Bloomberg reported that some Google employees find it weaker in real use than on benchmarks, especially on some coding work such as front-end design. Google disputes that.

So what: all three frontier labs now gate their strongest model behind a cyber-trust programme before general release. On Google's own numbers, Argon is the strongest model for long, multi-document knowledge work. It is not the clear leader on terminal-heavy coding. Teams choosing a coding model should wait for independent evaluations.

The benchmark picture is mixed. By DataCamp's count, Argon leads 13 of the 19 benchmarks Google published against GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Opus 5.5 (DataCamp; another tally gives 12 of 18, Pillitteri). Argon leads DeepSWE v1.1 at 77.9%, about 3.7 points ahead of Opus 5.5 and 3.8 ahead of Astra. It trails on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (57.4%, against Astra's 58.2% and Opus 5.5's 66.4%) and on FrontierSWE v2 (55.0%, against Astra's 65.5%) (Kingy AI, DataCamp). Its clear wins are in legal, finance, workflow automation and long context. A Vals Index result of 68.9% "using fewer tokens" is also circulating (AlphaSignal).

Price, as reported. Secondary sources quote an introductory $2 input / $10 output per million tokens (cached input $0.10), rising later to $4/$20 on an unspecified date (DEV, AlphaCorp). This briefing did not see a Google price page. If the introductory figure is right, Argon launches at the same list price as GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5.5.

The internal dissent. Bloomberg's sources say Argon is weaker on messy, real-world coding than its scores suggest. A Google employee familiar with model development told Bloomberg there is "large consensus" internally that it is at the frontier, and Google called the underperformance claim inaccurate (Bloomberg via Yahoo). The FrontierSWE and Terminal-Bench gaps above are consistent with the critics on one narrow point: Argon is not the terminal-coding leader.

Why the gated rollout matters. GPT-6 Astra also launched as a limited preview for approved organisations, and Anthropic gates its cyber-capable tiers. Gemini 4 is the third flagship in a row whose first users are security teams. That makes "trusted-tester cyber programme first" the default way frontier models now ship.

Watch next. The date paid API customers and Ultra subscribers get access; an official price page; independent Terminal-Bench and SWE results; and whether Meta's October "Watermelon" model keeps to its window.

IMPACT 7/10#2 POLICY · WORKFORCE · US STATE LAW

California's "No Robo Bosses Act": no firing or discipline by AI alone

On the 30 September deadline Newsom signed SB 947, reversing his earlier veto of similar legislation (CNBC). Employers may not rely solely on automated decision systems to discipline or fire workers. Where AI output is the primary basis, a human reviewer must corroborate the decision with other information, such as manager evaluations, peer reviews and personnel files. It takes effect 1 July 2027 (Sheppard Mullin). It came in a package of AI workplace laws covering surveillance and layoffs (AP via ABC). Newsom also vetoed AB 2575 (AI in health care services), AB 2656 (notice to public employees of AI doing their work) and SB 903 (AI and mental-health professionals) (Governor's office). Separately, he signed an executive order telling state agencies to keep using "artificial intelligence" rather than "super intelligence", the term Trump has ordered federal agencies and diplomats to use (AP, White House).

Any HR system that auto-flags or auto-terminates California workers needs a documented human-corroboration step by July 2027. Vendors selling "autonomous HR agents" now have a compliance spec.

Mercury 2.5: a diffusion LLM at ~1,100 tokens a second, on OpenRouter for three weeks

Older news, new to this briefing. Inception launched Mercury 2.5 on 8 September. It generates text by diffusion, drafting and refining many tokens in parallel rather than one at a time from left to right. Inception claims 1,107 tokens/s on widely available Nvidia GPUs and a 40% intelligence gain over Mercury 2, with a 260K context window, tunable reasoning, native tool use and JSON mode (Business Wire, GIGAZINE). List price is $0.20/$0.75 per million tokens, $0.04/$0.15 during the 80% launch discount. It has been live on OpenRouter and Baseten since launch (OpenRouter). Inception says a larger model is in training.

IMPACT 6/10#4 MODELS · DECISION MODELS · WATCHLIST · DATED 18 SEP

Laya: an open 421M decision model answering in 33ms, the Jev alternative you host yourself

Edition 101 mentioned the Node/ONNX runtime in passing. The model behind it has not had its own card until now. Convai Innovations released Laya on 18 September, three days after Jev, under Apache 2.0. It is a 421M-parameter decision head on ModernBERT-large. It answers typed choice, score and yes/no questions with probabilities and generates no text. Convai reports 32.8ms per answer on a T4 GPU, coverage of 100+ languages, and three checkpoints in one Hugging Face repo (Hugging Face, AI Weekly, El Solitario). A third-party review reports an expected calibration error of 0.081 vs Jev's 0.246 after temperature refit, with the caveat that zero-shot accuracy is weaker (eesel). A local runner, Ollaya, reached the Hacker News front page last week (HN digest).

The decision-model category now has three tiers: a startup API (Jev), a frontier-lab API (OpenAI Decisions, unpriced) and an open model you host yourself (Laya). Calibration, not latency, is where they differ.

Odyssey-3: one world model to drive cars, fly drones, move robot arms and play games

Missed at launch on 15 September and found by today's world-model watchlist search. Odyssey describes Odyssey-3 as an autoregressive diffusion transformer trained to simulate physics, cause and effect, and human behaviour. It is meant as one pretrained base for robot arms, humanoids, vehicles, drones, game agents and video games (Dealroom, daily.dev). In its headline demo, one researcher taught it to drive closed-loop routes in India from 20 hours of driving data (CEO Oliver Cameron). Other demos transfer game policies across GTA V, Red Dead Redemption 2 and Sleeping Dogs. A public release is promised "in the coming weeks". Odyssey raised $310M in June from investors including Amazon, GV, AMD and IQT.

With AMD buying World Labs and AMI Labs still silent, Odyssey is the first world-model company to claim cross-embodiment control. That claim needs an independent test once the model is public.

Amazon signs a $1B+ deal for Synopsys silicon IP behind Trainium, Graviton and Nitro

The technology is application-optimised silicon IP plus Synopsys' EDA, simulation and agentic-AI design tools, for Amazon's whole custom-chip line: Trainium for AI training and inference, Graviton CPUs and Nitro security chips. IMPACT 6/10#8 RESEARCH · BIOSECURITY · NATURE

SynthID Bio: DeepMind watermarks AI-designed proteins without breaking them

A Google DeepMind paper in Nature (30 September) describes SynthID Bio. It weaves a faint statistical signature into both the amino-acid sequence and the 3D shape of AI-designed proteins by slightly biasing which amino acids are chosen, without noticeably impairing function (Nature paper, Nature news). The aim is to let biological databases and synthesis screeners tell machine-made proteins from natural ones, both for biosecurity and to credit the designers. Coverage flags the limit: the stamp can be scrubbed (Science).

This is the first function-preserving provenance mark for designed biology, published in a top journal. Expect DNA-synthesis screening and biosecurity policy to cite it, with the scrubbing caveat attached.

IMPACT 5/10#9 HARDWARE · INTERCONNECT · DEEP BEAT

CScale's optical scale-up interconnect leaves stealth, backed by Nvidia and Intel

The product is an optical interconnect for scale-up networking, the links that tie the accelerators in one AI cluster domain together. It combines photonics, electronics and software, and is designed for system-level reliability rather than easy replacement of failed optics. Founder and CTO Sanjai Kohli previously founded SiRF; CEO Martin Lund ran Cisco's chip engineering group and Broadcom's switch business. Funding is reported as $188M total, of which a $145M Series C was led by Atreides, Valor and Premji Invest, with Nvidia and Intel Capital participating (SiliconANGLE, DCD, TNW).

Nvidia investing in an optical scale-up rival to copper NVLink signals that it expects rack-scale domains to outgrow copper.

IMPACT 5/10#10 HARDWARE · INFERENCE · DATED 28 SEP

Cerebras gets a 100MW order from Gimlet for mixed-chip inference

Gimlet Cloud will deploy 100MW of Cerebras CS-4 wafer-scale systems over one to two years, with the first data centre online later this year and cloud availability in 2027. The technology is a disaggregated inference architecture that sends each phase of inference (prefill, decode, attention) to whichever chip suits it, Cerebras wafers or GPUs. The target is up to 3,000 tokens/s for agentic workloads (DCD, Quartz, Gimlet).

Mixing chips by inference phase is becoming a product category of its own, which weakens the "one GPU for everything" model of inference clouds.

Semifive to build an unnamed US company's LLM inference chip for $52M

The technology is a next-generation LLM inference accelerator using LPDDR6 and PCIe Gen5 for memory bandwidth at lower power, aimed at hyperscalers. Tape-out is planned for H1 2027 and mass production for 2028. Using LPDDR rather than HBM for inference is one way around the HBM shortage Micron just described.

Anthropic's provable-inference milestone falls due with no public word

Anthropic's frontier safety roadmap set 30 September as the date for Phase 1 of provable inference: cryptographically signing model outputs so they can be tied to specific weights, as a defence against tampered models (Anthropic roadmap). Small on its own, but self-set deadlines are what make voluntary safety roadmaps credible. Missing one quietly, during an IPO process, is worth watching.

Micron's record quarter: $54.23B, guidance to $61.5B, and 2027 HBM mostly sold

WHAT CHANGED The print landed. FQ4 revenue was $54.23B against ~$51.07B expected and $50.0B ± $1.0B guidance, up 379% year on year. Revenue, gross margin and EPS all beat the top of guidance. DRAM revenue was $39.8B (73% of sales), and data-centre revenue rose about elevenfold (CNBC, 8-K). FQ1 guidance is $61.5B ± $1.5B (vs ~$57B expected) and EPS $38.15 ± $1.00 (vs ~$35.40). Gross margin guidance of ~86.25% came in just under the ~86.7% consensus, so the stock was roughly flat after hours (MarketBeat, ZeroHedge).
On the call: agreements cover "the vast majority" of calendar-2027 HBM bit supply at significantly higher prices, narrowing HBM's margin gap with conventional DRAM. Micron is shipping HBM4 for Vera Rubin and working with Nvidia on the industry's "first custom HBM implementation" (call transcript). Management called FQ1 the floor for fiscal-2027 margins.

If 2027 HBM is mostly sold, accelerator volumes next year are capped by memory contracts already signed. That is why LPDDR inference designs like Semifive's (New #11) are worth tracking.
