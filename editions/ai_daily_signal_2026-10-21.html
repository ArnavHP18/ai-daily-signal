■ Today's Lead · New · Reuters investigation, Mon 19 Oct · Wednesday deep beat: AI security · Day 14 of the agent-intrusion arc

A single operator used AI agents to break into the websites of more than 100 companies, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins, after giving them instructions shorter than 100 Chinese characters, a Reuters investigation finds

According to Reuters' reporting, as relayed by The Hacker News, Yahoo and others, the agents probed more than 1,200 websites and reached sites belonging to more than 100 companies, mostly e-commerce operations in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan. The operator, described as Chinese-speaking, gave them a brief instruction and the agents then worked for hours with little human direction. Researcher Snorre Fagerland of NorForge found the campaign by scanning the internet for exposed instances of Cairn, a platform that coordinates AI agents. Reports say the tooling included DeepSeek models and Anthropic's Claude Code. One estimate puts the whole campaign at about $5,000, but NorForge had no usage data, so that figure is highly uncertain.

So what: this is the first widely reported case of commodity AI agents running an intrusion campaign across many unrelated companies, and it was found only because the attacker left the orchestrator exposed. If you run customer-facing web properties, assume agent-driven scanning is now cheap and continuous: patch exposed admin panels and plugins this week, and check logs for long, methodical probing sessions. What the attacker took, and from whom, is still unclear.

IMPACT 6/10#2 POLICY · US–CHINA · FEDERAL IT · FEDERAL REGISTER, TUE 20 OCT

A DHS order, issued in July and published in the Federal Register on 20 Oct, bars DeepSeek from US federal civilian procurement and requires agencies to remove DeepSeek products, including third-party products built on DeepSeek

Issued under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 on 24 July after a Federal Acquisition Security Council recommendation, and kept out of view until this month. It covers federal civilian executive-branch systems, not national-security systems. Reporting cites concern that DeepSeek's servers are in China. The 60-day window for exception requests ran from July. Unclear: the order lists no specific products, and does not say how agencies should treat a DeepSeek model running on a US company's servers.

The "built on DeepSeek implementations" clause is the part vendors should read: any product that embeds or fine-tunes DeepSeek weights and sells to civilian agencies now has a removal problem, even if it is hosted in the US.

IMPACT 5/10#3 MODELS · OPEN-SOURCE INFERENCE · DEEPSEEK · TUE 20 OCT

DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, the inference framework behind its production services, under Apache 2.0, with support for NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei's Ascend 950

A companion deepseek-recipe library turns requests into token sequences the same way DeepSeek's official API does. Scope is limited: offline inference only, and only DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. Users must build their own KV-cache system, prefill/decode split and load balancing. DeepInfer started from vLLM but was rebuilt from the bottom up. No outside benchmark of its claimed efficiency yet. Released a day after the DHS order became public.

Matching the official API's tokenisation means self-hosters can reproduce DeepSeek's behaviour exactly, and first-class Ascend 950 support is a signal that Chinese labs are shipping for domestic chips on day one.

IMPACT 5/10#4 MODELS · EMERGING TECH · PHYSICAL AI · VISION-FORCE-ACTION · TUE 20 OCT

Eka Robotics' vision-force-action model, which uses force data from a sensing gripper as well as camera images, draws a $150M Series A at a $1.45B post-money valuation

The product: a manipulation model plus a custom gripper whose force sensors tell the model how firmly it is holding an object, so it can adjust before a slip. Company claims: about 1,500 picks an hour of unfamiliar objects against a ~600 human baseline, and >95% success on objects up to 1 kg. Gripper costs under $1,000, rated for 5M duty cycles (target 20M). Founded by MIT's Pulkit Agrawal and ex-DeepMind Tuomas Haarnoja. a16z and F-Prime led. An investor calls the speed figure a lab result; pilot customers are unnamed.

Most robot foundation models are vision-language-action; adding force as a first-class input is a different model class, aimed at the grasping failures that keep robots out of warehouses. Scored on that, not the round.

IMPACT 4/10#5 MODELS · EMERGING TECH · DECISION / TASK MODELS · LAUNCHED TUE 13 OCT

ScaleDown launches Constrained Decoding Models: small single-task models for classifying, extracting and summarising that can only return allowed outputs, at $0.05/M input with free output, on OpenRouter and in LangChain

Each model is trained for one task and returns a label from a list or spans of the input rather than free text. Company claims: 200x faster and 467x cheaper than frontier APIs on those tasks (no independent test). A Japanese report says the OpenRouter listing launched at a 20% discount ($0.04/M). The langchain-scaledown middleware compresses agent history and tool results and fails open; ScaleDown is in LangSmith's LLM Gateway. Critique (ibl.ai, which sells a rival product): constrained decoding is an established technique, not a new architecture, and the paid model leads the free GLiNER2.5 on ScaleDown's own benchmark by only 0.2 points.

Another entrant in the Jev-style decision-model class, priced just above Jev's $0.042/M and distributed through the gateways and agent frameworks developers already use. Missed by scans since 13 Oct.
