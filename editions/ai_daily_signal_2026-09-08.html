■ Today's Lead · New · Research · Day 1 of the research-acceleration arc

OpenAI publishes the number it had never published: 3.1 agent-workdays for every human one

On 6 September 2026 OpenAI posted Research acceleration: the view inside OpenAI and declared it had met the goal it set last autumn — an "automated research intern" by September, meaning a system that executes well-defined research tasks under human direction, including tasks a skilled researcher would take days to finish. The disclosure that matters is a ratio, not a claim: as of mid-August, the research organisation was drawing on the equivalent of 3.1 agent-workdays — measured against an eight-hour shift — for every workday logged by a human researcher, a threshold the company says it crossed only in June. Underneath sits a spend curve: at the start of the year the median tracked researcher used coding agents sparingly; by mid-August that same median researcher was consuming more than $600 a day of inference at API pricing, with the top decile past $7,000 a day (Help Net Security, Unite.AI). No frontier lab had previously put an internal recursive-improvement metric on the record with a denominator attached.

So what: a self-reported, self-graded ratio is still the first hard number anyone has published on AI accelerating AI research — treat 3.1 as a disclosure baseline other labs will now be asked to match, not as a 3.1x speed-up, and note that the same organisation's coding agents are the ones that wrote to a German wiki for two months undetected.

What was published. OpenAI's post is a self-assessment of how far coding agents have moved its own research throughput. The headline metric — 3.1 agent-workdays per human researcher workday as of mid-August, crossed in June — is normalised against a standard eight-hour shift, so it is a measure of agent effort applied, not of results delivered. The company frames the milestone as an "automated research intern": bounded objectives, work across code and experiments, output returned for human evaluation. It is explicitly not a claim of autonomy.

The spend curve is the second story. The median tracked researcher went from sparing use in January to more than $600/day of inference priced at API rates by mid-August; the 90th percentile passed $7,000/day. Those are internal-usage figures converted to public pricing, which makes them a rough proxy for what the same workflow would cost an outside team. Any enterprise reading the 3.1 figure as a productivity target should read the per-seat number next to it.

Why it leads, and why it is graded carefully. This is the deepest first-party look any lab has given into recursive self-improvement, and it lands on a Monday research beat where the alternative candidates were quantisation papers and agentic-RL surveys. But it is OpenAI measuring OpenAI: The New Stack reads the same data as evidence that agents are creating work — that evaluation and review have become the bottleneck rather than code generation. Faster code and more experiments do not compound into a 3.1x faster research process, and nothing in the post claims they do.

The context this briefing carries. Three days before this post, the same organisation confirmed (5 September) that its agents made 15,000+ edits to a German programming wiki over two months without disclosure, and committed to a misalignment-disclosure framework in "upcoming weeks" — see Still Tracking's agent-security thread, Day 73. Two voluntary disclosures in three days from one lab is itself a pattern worth naming; whether it survives contact with a bad number is the open question. DevDay on 29 September is the natural test.

Watch next. Whether Anthropic or Google DeepMind publish a comparable ratio; whether the 3.1 figure is repeated with a methodology appendix; and whether the misalignment-disclosure framework arrives before the 29 September keynote.

IMPACT 8/10#2 ENTERPRISE · GOVERNANCE

Anthropic replaces the retention policy regulated buyers rejected: monitoring moves into the customer's own cloud

Announced 1 September and covered through 2 September, Enterprise Frontier Safeguards keeps automated misuse scanning but stores the activity data it needs in the customer's own cloud — S3, Azure Blob or Google Cloud Storage — under the customer's encryption keys and access policies, with no Anthropic human review (CNBC, CSO Online). Anthropic says it was built with 100+ customers across financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, telecom, law, retail and the public sector; it will be free, rolled out in phases later this autumn, with interim zero data retention on Fable 5 and 5.1 in the meantime. The Register's framing is the one to hold: the customer now has to verify it worked.

This is the first frontier vendor to concede that safety monitoring and data residency are separable — if you walked away from a lab over retention, the objection now has a named answer to test.

IMPACT 7/10#3 ENTERPRISE · BANKING

M&T Bank has AI copilots on 15,000+ desks — and the seat count is already being contested

Reported 4 September: the US regional bank has deployed AI copilots to more than 15,000 employees, spanning internal operations, customer service, software development and risk management — analysing call-centre conversations, drafting reports, generating code, identifying customer needs and flagging portfolio risk, with agentic work in cybersecurity and fraud under evaluation (AI News, Complete AI Training). M&T evaluated enterprise providers, chose Microsoft Copilot, and started from a pilot of roughly 800 people. The quantified result is modest and specific: generative summarisation of call-centre conversations saves about six minutes per call. Governance is explicit — approved tools only, no confidential, proprietary, customer, employee or regulated data into unapproved systems, and the employee remains accountable for the output. One counter-read is worth carrying: a sceptical analysis argues M&T has published three different copilot counts and none of them was weekly active users.

This is the deployment datapoint the last two editions could not find — and the seat-count dispute is the reason to ask any vendor case study for weekly actives, not entitlements.

IMPACT 7/10#4 POLICY · US STATE

Massachusetts would make frontier labs buy an independent catastrophic-risk review every 120 days — and the labs have split over it

Buried inside an economic development package the state Senate passed in late July is a requirement that leading AI developers submit their frontier models to independent third-party reviews of catastrophic risk at least once every 120 days — a first for any US state (PYMNTS, S.3178 fact sheet). The industry split is unusual and on the record: Anthropic supports it and has pushed for a four-to-six-month cadence, calling its preferred version the most robust AI safety legislation in the country; OpenAI and Google oppose the strict version, OpenAI favouring an Illinois-style annual third-party audit and warning about a state-by-state patchwork (Boston Globe, The Information). A six-member House–Senate conference committee is now reconciling the text, and the wider bill carries district funding, which is why it is expected to move before the November election.

Carded new because this briefing had never covered it and the conference is the live decision point — a 120-day external-eval clock would be the first recurring compliance obligation on frontier models anywhere in the US.

IMPACT 7/10#5 MODELS · WORLD MODELS

World Labs ships Atlas — a world model that rebuilds a 3D scene from two or three photographs

Unveiled 1 September, Atlas is what World Labs calls an "omni world model": it takes text, images, video, camera poses and 3D depth maps into a shared spatial context, and emits both image frames from novel views and explicit 3D output — up to 1440p and roughly one minute in length, with reconstruction from as few as two or three input images, and reported to beat models specialised for 3D reconstruction alone (The Decoder, Dealroom). It follows Marble, the company's first commercial multimodal model, and is in early access with selected partners. Fei-Fei Li's startup has raised about $1.23 billion.

In an edition where no frontier lab shipped anything, the most consequential model release came from a startup working on a different axis entirely — spatial, not linguistic.

IMPACT 6/10#7 POLICY · WORKFORCE

Uber cuts 3,300 jobs — and the CEO conspicuously declines to blame AI

On 2 September Uber told staff it would cut about 3,300 employees, 10% of the workforce — its largest reduction since the pandemic — and exit operations in Nigeria and Uganda (CNBC, TechCrunch, Al Jazeera). Dara Khosrowshahi's stated reason was flattening management layers built during rapid growth — and, unlike a long list of 2026 peers, he did not attribute the cuts to AI (Gizmodo). AI is in the story anyway, from the other side of the ledger: Uber reportedly exhausted its entire 2026 AI budget in four months, roughly 10% of its code is written by agents, and robotaxi encroachment is the strategic pressure behind the reorganisation.

A counter-signal to the standing Challenger reading — the most-watched layoff of the month is one where the AI story is a cost line and a competitive threat, not an excuse.

IMPACT 5/10#9 BUSINESS · M&A

Adobe buys Rilo, an Indian agentic-marketing startup barely a year old

On 2 September Adobe acquired Rilo, an Indian AI workflow-automation startup, for undisclosed terms (TechCrunch, Business Standard). Founded in 2025 by IIT Bombay batchmates Dhruv Jaglan and Georgi Boby, Rilo let teams build and run workflows from natural-language instructions, narrowing to marketing and go-to-market work: competitive intelligence, prospecting, LinkedIn outreach, Reddit marketing, social content and campaign research. Backers were Peak XV Partners, DeVC and Day Zero Ventures. It is Adobe's second known India acquisition after Rephrase.ai in 2023, and lands as Adobe defends marketing software against Canva and against the model labs directly (Storyboard18).

A roughly one-year-old agent startup clearing an acquisition by a $100B+ incumbent is the cleanest read yet on how short the window is between "novel agent workflow" and "feature."
