Anthropic cuts its small-model price by up to 90% with Claude Haiku 5.5

On Wednesday 7 October Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for requests up to 100,000 tokens, against $1 and $5 for Haiku 4.5. That is a 90% cut on short prompts and 50% above 100K tokens. Context grows to 1 million tokens (VentureBeat · Decrypt · Techmeme / The New Stack). The same afternoon OpenAI brought GPT-6 to every ChatGPT tier (New #2), with GPT-6 Luna reaching free users from today.

So what: the cheapest tier of the two biggest API vendors now costs the same, $0.10 per million input tokens. Any team running classification, extraction, routing or sub-agent calls on Haiku 4.5 should re-price this week; Anthropic's own estimate is about 75% average savings. It also completes the Claude 5.5 family a week before Anthropic's reported pre-IPO investor day (14 Oct, on the radar).

IMPACT 8/10#1 MODELS · SMALL-MODEL PRICING · DATED WED 7 OCT

Anthropic releases Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, up to 90% below Haiku 4.5, with a 1M-token context, matching OpenAI's cheapest GPT-6 tier

Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5 on 7 October, the last of the Claude 5.5 family after Opus and Sonnet. For requests up to 100,000 tokens it costs $0.10 per million input and $0.50 per million output; above that, $0.50 and $2.50. Haiku 4.5 cost $1 and $5, so short prompts are 90% cheaper and long ones 50%. Anthropic estimates about 75% average savings, since the updated tokenizer is slightly more token-heavy. Context rises to 1M tokens from 200K, and Sonnet 5.5 cache reads drop to $0.10 per million. It ships as claude-haiku-5-5 on the Claude Platform, AWS, Google Cloud and Azure (VentureBeat · Decrypt · Yahoo Finance · StreetInsider · Techmeme / The New Stack). VentureBeat notes the price matches OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna; Reuters, as summarised by Techmeme, framed it as an expansion of the lineup before Anthropic's planned IPO.

Act this week if you pay per token for small-model calls: a like-for-like swap from Haiku 4.5 is roughly a 75% cost cut, and a 1M context makes long-document extraction viable at this tier. With Anthropic and OpenAI both at $0.10 per million input tokens, the fight at the cheap end now turns on quality and latency. That also squeezes decision-model startups such as Jev ($0.042/M input) on price. No independent benchmarks yet.

IMPACT 7/10#2 MODELS · CONSUMER ROLLOUT · DATED WED 7 OCT

OpenAI brings GPT-6 to all ChatGPT users: paid tiers from 7 October, GPT-6 Luna for Free and Go from 8 October, with an optional 'Intelligent UI' of charts, buttons and forms

On 7 October OpenAI began rolling GPT-6 into the Chat side of ChatGPT for Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users. Free and Go users get the lighter GPT-6 Luna from 8 October. OpenAI says the change applies to Chat only; the models in Work and Codex are unchanged. When a search is needed, GPT-6 Instant starts answering 44% sooner on average than GPT-5.6 Instant and is better at judging when to search. A new, optional Intelligent UI puts charts, buttons and forms inside answers. GPT-6 had been paid-only since September; ChatGPT's free users, the largest AI user base, now get it too. Interactive answers will change how search and publishing traffic reach people, so SEO and content teams should look at what the Intelligent UI shows instead of a link. Luna is also the model behind OpenAI's Decisions API, so its cheapest model now has a huge consumer footprint as well.

IMPACT 7/10#3 ENTERPRISE · AGENT STANDARDS · AGENTIC COMMERCE · DATED TUE 6 OCT

Meta, Walmart, Shopify, Stripe, Sierra and others launch the Personal Agent Protocol so businesses can tell which AI agent acts for which person; OpenAI and Anthropic are not in it yet

Announced on 6 October, the Personal Agent Protocol is an open standard for how personal AI agents identify themselves to, and deal with, businesses. Founding partners named by CNBC are Meta, Sierra, Genesys, Instinct, Rocket, Shopify and Walmart, with Stripe listed in its own announcement. Bret Taylor, Sierra co-founder and OpenAI chair, leads it. He called the current situation "kind of chaos until such a standard exists" and said "companies will know when it's a personal agent versus an actual person." Sierra plans a v0.1 specification later this month. OpenAI and Anthropic are not participants, though Taylor expects them to join (CNBC · Quartz · Yahoo Tech · Daily Caller · Techmeme). Background: Amazon began blocking Meta's Muse agent in September, saying it had not authorised Muse to access accounts, scrape data or transact, and that the agent did not identify itself (The Next Web · Adweek).

Agent identity is turning into a group fight: Meta's group (Walmart, Shopify) on one side, Amazon's block on the other, with OpenAI and Anthropic's agents outside both for now. Retailers and banks building agent-facing checkout should read the v0.1 spec when it lands this month. It would sit alongside, not replace, the ACP and UCP checkout protocols. Edition 109 had this only as background to Muse; it was missed on the day.

IMPACT 6/10#4 MODELS · OPEN WEIGHTS · DATED MON 5 OCT

Reflection AI unveils Beam, its first open-weight model: a 501B-parameter MoE with 23B active, pitched as the Western answer to GLM and Qwen, with Apache 2.0 weights promised for October

Nvidia-backed Reflection AI announced Beam on 5 October: a text-only sparse mixture-of-experts with 501B total and 23B active parameters, pretrained on 23.8T tokens and then trained with large-scale reinforcement learning, with a company-reported 1M-token context. Reflection claims 80.9 on SWE-Bench Verified and 80.1 on Terminal-Bench v2.1, and says it matches Z.ai's GLM-5.2 on reasoning with 3–4x less inference compute. All of these are self-reported, and one analysis notes the compute comparison leaves out several serving costs. The API is in beta behind a waitlist; weights, a technical report and fine-tuning tools are promised under Apache 2.0 later in October (The Next Web · RuntimeWire · Crypto Briefing · Ecosistema Startup). CEO Misha Laskin told Semafor, as reported, that "the only way to own intelligence is, by definition, if it's open"; co-founder Ioannis Antonoglou hinted that future, more capable models may not be released openly.

If the weights ship as promised, this is the largest US open-weight model aimed at sovereign and on-premise buyers that until now defaulted to Chinese checkpoints (Qwen 3.8, GLM-5.2, Kimi K3). Until the weights and the technical report appear, treat the numbers as marketing. Weights due date is on the radar. Two days old: new to this briefing, not inside 24h.

IMPACT 5/10#5 ENTERPRISE · PERSONAL AGENTS · EMERGING TECH · SURFACED 22 SEP – 6 OCT

Open-source clones of Meta's Muse multiply: CopilotKit's OpenMuse (MIT) and nanoMuse (GPL-3.0, with a 6 October arXiv paper) both rebuild the always-on personal agent with its own browser, shell and files

Within a month of Meta's Muse launch (8 Sep), two open re-implementations have appeared. OpenMuse, which CopilotKit announced on 22 September, is an MIT-licensed personal agent with a persistent Chromium browser, an optional Linux terminal and file handling. It runs on iOS, Android and web from one Expo/React Native codebase and has a durable task engine with mandatory review before external writes. It is alpha, and every mode needs a free CopilotKit Intelligence key, so it is not fully self-hosted (AlphaSignal · andrew.ooo review · Railway template). nanoMuse (GPL-3.0) runs the agent locally on Android, with a Linux file system, shell, browser, MCP, skills and scheduled tasks, using a model you supply. Desktop and web versions have shipped, and iOS and glasses are planned (nanoMuse wiki (primary)). Its paper, "nanoMuse: An Open-Source Personal Agent for Every Device You Own", was posted on 6 October (arXiv 2610.08699 · DailyArXiv digest, 8 Oct).

A run of clones is the developer-attention signal the emerging-tech beat looks for. As with Dots (OpenDots), the open community is copying the 'agent with its own computer' design within weeks. For companies, that means Muse-style agents will hit their sites from many builders, not only Meta, which is the problem the Personal Agent Protocol (New #3) is meant to solve. Self-reported features; star counts not checked today.

IMPACT 5/10#8 POLICY · ELECTIONS · SOCIETY · DATED 2–3 OCT (LATE CATCH-UP)

US midterm campaigns start sending legal threats over AI deepfake ads, while 31 state laws mostly require only a disclosure label

In a Wisconsin House race, Democrat Rebecca Cooke's lawyers sent Rep. Derrick Van Orden a cease-and-desist over AI videos putting words in her mouth. He replied with another AI video, a duck in a DSA T-shirt saying "cease and desist". Congress has set no federal standard (Axios, 2 Oct). The National Conference of State Legislatures counts 31 states with election deepfake laws. Most only require a label, so the fake ad can stay up; Louisiana's law exempts federal candidates (Fast Company · Kansas Reflector (opinion)). It follows Ken Paxton's realistic AI ad of James Talarico in Texas (Axios, 9 Sep).

With no federal rule, private lawsuits are becoming the main brake on deepfake political ads, four weeks before the vote. Platforms and ad-tech vendors will get takedown demands they have no clear rules to judge.
