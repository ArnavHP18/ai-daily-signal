US voters back data-centre limits almost everywhere they were asked, and elect moratorium governors

In Tuesday's midterms, local bans and caps passed in dozens of towns, and Texas and Georgia picked governors who want a pause.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceChecked on Trumbull County's official results and the Data Centers on the Ballot tracker

Data-centre measures were on ballots in dozens of US towns and counties on Tuesday, and voters sided with restrictions almost everywhere. The Data Centers on the Ballot tracker, which reads county election pages, had the restrict side winning or ahead in 51 of 59 measures it follows as of Wednesday evening (23 final, 22 with all precincts in, 6 still counting); the allow side led in one, and seven had no results. In Hubbard, Ohio, Trumbull County's summary report shows a citizen-initiated ban on any new data centre passing 2,622 to 802. Ohio alone voted on 21 of the roughly 41 measures nationwide, and at least 14 municipalities approved bans or restrictions, Fortune reports: Trenton backed a ban 80% to 20%, aimed at an 18-building AWS campus, and Sunbury and Pataskala approved charter bans on large sites. At state level, Democrats Gina Hinojosa (Texas), who defeated Greg Abbott, and Keisha Lance Bottoms (Georgia) both campaigned for data-centre moratoriums (Wired, The Register).

So what: Siting risk for AI compute is now political, and it has reached the two biggest US build-out states. A pause in Texas and Georgia could touch hundreds of planned projects, and strategists expect tax breaks to go and permits to get harder. This week, anyone with US capacity plans should map which sites sit in towns that just voted, or in states with incoming moratorium governors. Every count is unofficial until counties certify them.

Who won the argument. Almost a quarter of voters told NBC News that data centres were 'crucial' to their vote, and nearly two-thirds called them a major or minor factor (Wired). Before the vote, Data Center Watch found that 43% of 74 leading candidates for governor backed a ban or moratorium and 51% wanted more regulation; in eight states, including Texas, Ohio and Michigan, both main candidates backed some kind of pause. Hinojosa's campaign said she would freeze data-centre permits until the legislature writes rules and would end the industry's tax breaks.

The money. AI-industry political groups lost several of the races they spent most on: American Mission and Building the Future, linked to the Leading the Future network backed by OpenAI president Greg Brockman and Andreessen Horowitz, spent $4.8M backing South Carolina Senate candidate Darline Graham, who lost (TechCrunch). The Anthropic-backed Public First network had mixed results. A day after the vote, OpenAI's global affairs chief Chris Lehane published an essay urging politicians to address voters' AI worries before 2028.

Limits of the vote. Many measures cover future projects, not sites already under construction. Tom's Hardware notes that of more than 300 local bans enacted so far, only three developments totalling 2.3GW have been delayed, and labs are already chasing smaller 20-30MW sites. Results in some towns were still being counted on Wednesday.

#2Models · open models · 4 NovIMPACT 8/10

A chip-design lab releases Ripple-65B, a small dense open model claiming the US coding lead

Ricursive Intelligence says it trained the model from scratch for $32M and beats US open models up to 15 times its size on an agentic coding test.

Ricursive Intelligence, a lab that co-designs chips, software and models, released Ripple-65B Preview, a general-purpose version of the model behind its AI chip-design platform. It is a dense Transformer (no mixture-of-experts), pretrained on 12 trillion tokens with up to a 1M-token context. Ricursive reports 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1, ahead of Reflection Beam 501B (80.1), Meta Muse Spark Open 816B (76.4), Thinking Machines Inkling 975B (63.8) and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B (56.4). It puts the full R&D cost at $32M, about a tenth of the previous open-weight leader by its estimate, and says reinforcement learning reached that score in 1.4M rollouts, about 70 times fewer than Reflection Beam. The Hugging Face card lists Apache 2.0 and about 64.8B parameters, with NVFP4 and DFlash2 variants; community GGUF builds appeared the same day. OpenRouter lists it at $0.20/$0.60 per million input/output tokens, and Ricursive says it runs on a DGX Spark.

The US open-weight coding bar was held by models eight to fifteen times larger. A 65B dense model that fits a workstation, with a permissive licence, makes self-hosted coding agents cheaper still. The scores are Ricursive's own and compare only US models, not Chinese ones such as Kimi K3.1 Pro (Developing D1); wait for independent Terminal-Bench runs.

#3Hardware · chip controls · 4 NovIMPACT 7/10

Oracle reportedly drops a $7B deal to rent 100,000 AI chips to Tencent

The FT says US officials pressed Oracle, while officials in Guangdong urged Tencent to support Chinese chips.

Oracle has cancelled a multibillion-dollar chip-leasing contract with Tencent after US officials approached it with concerns, the Financial Times reported, according to Reuters. Tencent also had visits from senior officials in Guangdong, its home province, who asked it to support domestic Chinese chips. The FT reported last month that the five-year lease, worth about $7B and Tencent's largest overseas deal, would have given it about 100,000 advanced AI chips in Oracle data centres in Southeast Asia, chips it cannot buy at home because of US and Chinese curbs. Reuters could not verify the report; Oracle, Tencent and the White House did not respond. It follows Nvidia telling cloud customers to take less business from Chinese companies (yesterday's #10).

Renting US chips offshore was the main way Chinese labs reached top-end compute, and that route is closing from both capitals at once. Clouds in Southeast Asia with Chinese tenants should expect the same questions; Chinese labs will lean harder on domestic accelerators and on squeezing more from each chip.

#4

China's $70B order for Nvidia's export-legal card will be about 1% filled for now

NOTABLEOne secondary sourceHardware · chips for China · 4 Nov

ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent and other Chinese companies will receive only "some tens of thousands, or roughly 1%" of the RTX Pro 5500 workstation GPUs they ordered over the next few months, Bloomberg reports, per Tom's Hardware. Orders have reportedly grown to about $70B at roughly $11,000 a card, from $35B for 3 million-plus cards a fortnight ago. The card has the RTX 5090's CUDA cores with 84GB of GDDR7 and no export limits, so it can ship to China. A first batch of several thousand is expected by the end of 2026 and about 700,000 by the end of 2027. Nvidia declined to comment on shipments but criticised "outdated U.S. export controls".

Even the legal route into China is supply-bound, so Chinese labs will be short of compute through 2027 whatever Washington decides. Expect more efficiency-first models and domestic chips from China, and higher grey-market prices.

#5

Tencent ships Hy4 with computer use and promises open weights within days

NOTABLEOfficial sourceModels · open models · 4 Nov

Tencent released the full Hy4, a 770B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 49B active and a 1M-token context, and says it adds computer use for the first time, combining visual understanding, reasoning and tool calls (SiliconANGLE). It reports gains over August's Hy4 Preview in instruction following, long context, long-horizon agent tasks and deep research, and in game development, with integrations for Unreal Engine, Unity and Godot (+19.1% on its GameDev benchmark; baselines not published). Tencent also says its Hyra 2.0 autonomous research-agent harness optimised pre-training infrastructure for smaller models of the same design. OpenRouter lists tencent/hy4 with text and image input at about $0.42/$1.25 per million tokens. Tencent says it will publish the weights and system card "in coming days"; Hugging Face has only the preview so far.

Another Chinese frontier-scale model is heading for open weights, this time with computer use, the capability enterprise agents most need. Until the weights and independent tests arrive, the gains are Tencent's own claims.

#6

OpenAI buys Pierre, the Git storage start-up already built into Codex

NOTABLETwo or more outletsEnterprise · coding agents · 4 Nov

OpenAI says it will acquire Pierre Computer Company; terms were not disclosed. Pierre runs Code Storage, a Git-based cloud service for the code AI agents generate (SiliconANGLE says it helped Lovable cut code-retrieval latency by about 70%), and open-source libraries including Trees (file navigation), Diffs (change views) and Highlights (live syntax highlighting). Trees and Diffs already power Codex's interface. OpenAI says the goal is Git workflows that keep up when many agents write code in parallel; it already runs Pando, its own version-control backend for people and agents. The libraries stay open source and the deal needs regulatory approval (The Decoder).

Version control built for agents is turning into a platform contest with GitHub. Teams running many agents at once should watch where their repositories, and the history of what agents changed, end up living.

Meta buys Conductor, the app for running teams of coding agents, and winds it down

NOTABLEOfficial sourceEnterprise · coding agents · 4 Nov

Conductor, launched in July 2025, lets developers run several coding agents, from labs such as Anthropic and OpenAI, in separate parallel workspaces. CEO Charlie Holtz says the team joins Meta Superintelligence Labs to work with Alexandr Wang and Nat Friedman and promises "something new" early next year; Meta confirmed the deal but gave no terms (TechCrunch). Two of the big labs bought agent-workflow tools on the same day (see #6). Conductor Cloud users have about a week to move workspaces to local or export their data.

#9

Apple trims engineers' daily AI allowance from $300 to $200, with deeper cuts planned

NOTABLEOne secondary sourceEnterprise · AI costs · 4 Nov

Apple lowered the daily cap on AI model spending for some engineers from $300 to $200 last month and plans further cuts that could take some teams to $100, Bloomberg reports (via TNW). Many staff outside engineering already have a $100 cap, and Apple has considered $25 for some teams; staff can seek exemptions. Employees can use Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT and Google's Gemini, and some teams are being told to switch to cheaper or on-device models. Apple declined to comment. Its annual report shows R&D up 32% in fiscal 2026 on AI and infrastructure. TNW lists other caps: Microsoft division budgets, Tesla $200 a week, Uber $1,500 a month per coding tool.

Unlimited AI tokens inside big companies are giving way to budgets, and that favours cheaper and self-hosted models over premium ones. AI vendors selling to enterprises should expect harder questions about cost per task.

Canva cuts 1,271 jobs, 22% of staff, as AI costs and chatbots slow its growth

Design-software company Canva is cutting 22% of its staff, 1,271 people. App-layer software is being squeezed from two sides: inference bills on one, general chatbots doing the same job on the other. Watch for the same pattern at other design and productivity companies.

#17

A Codex jailbreak kit quietly turns the agent into an ad channel

MONITOROne secondary sourceEnterprise · AI security · 4 Nov

Check Point Research describes Haiou ("seagull"), a jailbreak bundle for OpenAI's Codex that edits the agent's configuration and global instructions so that greetings such as "hi" or "are you there?" trigger an advert for the author's site, an opaque OpenAI-compatible LLM gateway. Check Point calls it "prompt adware": the free jailbreak acts as a sales funnel for a paid gateway. It also reframes cyber requests as Capture-the-Flag games and installs skills for penetration testing, cracking and reverse engineering (The Hacker News).

Agent configuration files are a new place for persistent tampering, and routing traffic through an unknown gateway exposes everything you send. Check for unexpected edits to agent instruction and skills files on developer machines.

#18

Descope's Envoy lets shops admit AI agents under their own identity

MONITOROne secondary sourceEnterprise · agent identity · 4 Nov

Identity company Descope launched Envoy, which lets consumer websites and apps accept personal AI agents acting for customers, each with an identity separate from the person's. Today the choice is to block agents, as Amazon did with Meta's Muse in September, or let them log in with the customer's own password, which hides that software was involved. Envoy implements the Personal Agent Protocol ("Poppy"), a 0.1 draft Meta and Sierra published in October: the business publishes endpoints for agents, verifies which agent is calling and issues a session cryptographically bound to it (SiliconANGLE).

Agent identity standards are moving from drafts to shipping products. Retailers and banks can now ask vendors for Poppy support instead of a block-or-allow choice.

Nvidia's RTX Spark laptops run local models five times slower on Linux

MONITOROne secondary sourceHardware · AI PCs · 4 Nov

Testing an MSI Prestige laptop with Nvidia's RTX Spark chip, MindStudio found Linux works but the GPU is locked to about 780MHz on wall power, against about 2.2GHz on Windows. Moonshot posts Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights: a 2.8-trillion-parameter open model

NOTABLEOfficial sourceModels · open models · 2-4 Nov
WHAT CHANGED The weights are now public on Hugging Face (about 2.78T parameters, custom licence, ungated); the card adds the switch to Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention

Moonshot kept its promise to release Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights by 4 November; the post reached the Hacker News front page on Wednesday. The Hugging Face card calls it "an open-weight, natively multimodal agentic model" and Moonshot's "most capable 3T-class model to date", post-trained from Kimi K3 with large-scale reinforcement learning across hundreds of domains, with a 1M-token context. The largest open model to date is now downloadable, though running it needs a large GPU cluster. Read the custom licence before commercial use.

Anthropic delays one Claude watermark and adds three models that are easy to evade

MONITOROne secondary sourceModels · content labels · 4 Nov
WHAT CHANGED Opus 4.6 slips to 16 November and Opus 4.5, Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5 are added; on all four the mark applies only at default sampling settings

A second email to API customers, reported by The Decoder, says Claude Opus 4.6's text watermark, due on 3 November, now starts on 16 November, with no reason given. Opus 4.5, Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5 get it the same day; Amazon Bedrock may take a few extra days. On all four the watermark applies only when temperature, top_p and top_k are left at their defaults, so changing any one of them avoids it. Anthropic ties the retrofit to the EU AI Act's December marking deadline; models released since 2 August carry the mark from day one, and a detection API is in private preview.

A watermark that a single sampling setting switches off is a compliance label, not a reliable way to detect AI text. Anyone planning to use it to police AI writing should not rely on it for these models.
