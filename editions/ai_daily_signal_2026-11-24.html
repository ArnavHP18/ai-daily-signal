WHAT CHANGED Nvidia's board unanimously reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, citing input from senior US government officials; Trump says he 'made the deal'; shares jump 31.5%

Interim CEO Colette Kress stays CFO; Harvey Jones remains chair. Democrats accuse the administration of trading national security for market gains; Huang is due to testify to Congress in early December. Shares closed up 31.5% at $165.05.

So what: the leadership crisis is over but the open questions are not: the SEC accounting probe, the delayed 10-Q and Megaspeed all remain, now with the White House visibly involved in a private board's decision.

OpenRouter drops three AI providers after decoy prompts leaked to attackers

Model marketplace OpenRouter removed three inference providers, Together AI, Phala and Novita, on 23 Nov and revoked zero-data-retention (ZDR) eligibility for 125 endpoints as a precaution, BleepingComputer reports. The trigger was a five-month test by industrial-security firm Dragos, which sent about two million prompts containing unique links and decoy credentials through widely used AI services. Dragos says data from at least three providers was exposed: its seeded prompts were later used to try to get into its test systems and canary AWS accounts. It also found resellers able to view and keep customer prompts with standard logging, and providers not honouring privacy settings. OpenRouter says its own systems were not breached. Atlas Cloud withdrew its models pending security testing; Together AI said an AI coding agent had exposed data.

So what: a 'zero retention' label on a model router is only as good as the hosts behind it; any bank sending customer or code data through third-party inference should map which providers actually serve its requests and assume secrets in prompts can leak.

#4

RBI proposes a 'stop switch' and widens when banks must pay for digital fraud

NOTABLEOfficial sourceBanking · India · 23 Nov

The Reserve Bank of India published draft amendments on 23 Nov to its responsible-business-conduct rules for every kind of bank, from commercial and small finance banks to co-operatives. Customers would get customer-induced controls, a so-called stop switch: the option to disable digital payment channels, set transaction limits and block future debits. A Trusted Person Mechanism would add an extra approval for transfers above a threshold the customer sets (ET BFSI). The RBI also widens what counts as bank negligence in fraudulent electronic transactions, which decides who bears the loss: not offering these controls, not applying required authentication, not sending alerts, delaying complaints, system malfunctions, security breaches and collusion by staff or third parties. It builds on June's customer-liability rules. Comments are due by 14 Dec. The same day the RBI kept SBI (0.80% extra CET1), HDFC Bank (0.40%) and ICICI Bank (0.20%) as systemically important banks.

So what: Indian banks would need to build channel kill-switches and a delegated-approval flow, and would carry more fraud losses where they fall short; product, fraud and legal teams should respond before 14 Dec.

#5

China's Z.ai ships GLM-5.4, a 750B model that runs half its layers twice

NOTABLETwo or more outletsModels · new architectures · 23 Nov

Z.ai (Zhipu) launched GLM-5.4 on 23 Nov, its first loop transformer: instead of using each layer once, the 750B-parameter model runs a block of shared layers (27 to 54) twice, making the network deeper without adding parameters. Z.ai retrained the GLM-5 base into a loop model, saying loop models trained from scratch did worse; per-token gates control how much each pass changes the result, and both passes share the KV cache so a 1M-token context stays affordable (The Decoder). Z.ai claims it performs like models of 2-3 trillion parameters and runs at 70-200 tokens a second. In a matched 30B test, looping lifted AIME25 from 19.2 to 30.4 with about 10% more training compute. It is on Z.ai's Coding Plan (not yet the regular API); weights are promised. Simon Willison found it comparable in quality to OpenAI and Anthropic models on his informal tests. It follows Thinking Machines' looped Inkling 2 Mini (20 Nov).

So what: two looped models in four days, one at frontier scale, suggest 'more compute per token' is becoming a standard way to get bigger-model quality from smaller weights; if the weights ship, expect it to undercut US closed models on price.

OpenAI and Cisco open-source Shirudo to shield unpatched Linux servers with AI

NOTABLEOfficial sourceSecurity · AI defence · 23 Nov

OpenAI and Cisco launched Shirudo on 23 Nov, an open-source runtime that protects Linux systems while patches are tested and rolled out. It uses eBPF, small programs that run inside the operating system, to block the specific code path an exploit needs without taking the service offline. Agents can investigate a vulnerability, write a targeted 'shield' and test it against exploits and real traffic; security teams decide what is enforced and can watch a shield's effect before turning it on. So what: banks often wait weeks to patch core Linux systems; an open, auditable stop-gap layer is worth piloting, but treat agent-written kernel rules as code that needs review.
