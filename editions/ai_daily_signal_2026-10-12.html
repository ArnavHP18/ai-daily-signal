■ Today's Lead · New on the Wire · Broke Sun 11 Oct · Day 6 of the world-models arc

Amazon is in talks to buy Decart, the maker of a real-time video model and world-model simulators, for about $7 billion

The Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday, citing people familiar with the matter, that Amazon is in talks to acquire Israeli startup Decart in a deal that could be valued at around $7 billion. A deal could come soon if talks hold and no other bidder emerges. Scored on the product, not the price: Decart makes Lucy, a model that edits live video in real time from text prompts, and Oasis, real-time world models that generate simulated environments for training robots and self-driving systems. Decart already optimises Lucy on AWS Trainium and has planned distribution through Amazon Bedrock. No company has commented (Reuters, syndicated · Seeking Alpha · Finimize · Startup Fortune).

So what: real-time generative video and learned simulators are now worth hyperscaler-acquisition prices. Amazon would get a world-model team already tuned to its own chips, in a week when Odyssey-3 and H-JEPA showed the world-model class moving fast. This is Decart's third reported suitor: Anthropic explored a ~$6B deal in August (this briefing covered it on 18 Aug) and walked away after due diligence; Nvidia, an investor, also held talks.

The products. Lucy (now 2.5) transforms live video at 30 fps and 1080p from text prompts and reference images; Decart says it responds in under 30 ms, a figure not independently audited. Oasis is the world-model line: one report says Oasis 3 generates multi-camera driving environments in real time through an API. Decart also builds DOS, an inference stack targeting Nvidia GPUs, Google TPUs and Trainium. When Anthropic's interest surfaced in August, that chip-portability software was the reported draw (TNW · The Decoder).

The money. Decart raised $300M in May at nearly $4B (Radical Ventures lead; Nvidia joined), so ~$7B is close to a doubling in five months. It says it has raised more than $450M in total.

The auction. Nvidia held direct talks first. Anthropic then exchanged advanced drafts at about $7B (mostly in stock) before walking away after due diligence in September (Bloomberg, as relayed). SpaceX and Nebius were also linked in August; Musk denied SpaceX was negotiating. Amazon was already a strategic customer.

Not yet known. Whether talks close, the price, whether Lucy and Oasis stay available to other clouds, and how the deal fits next to Amazon's Nova models.

IMPACT 6/10#2 RESEARCH · INTERPRETABILITY · AGENT MONITORING · DEEP BEAT · DATED 8–10 OCT

Research beat: probes on a model's internal activations catch agent sabotage and unspoken deception better than a frontier text monitor, and Goodfire is already selling the idea as a cheap runtime monitor

"Caught in the Act: Probes Effectively Detect Sabotage and Catch Unverbalized Deception" (arXiv 2610.12445; Hollinsworth, Spies, Diriba, Gleave, Cundy). The authors build a large deception training set and a probe that pools signals across many layers and tokens. It reaches 98.8% AUC on SHADE-Arena, beating a text-monitoring baseline built on Opus 5.5, and the probes get better as the model scales. They also test "introspective" deception that cannot be judged from the transcript alone (arXiv · arXiv digest, 10 Oct). On 8 Oct, Goodfire launched "inside-out" monitors for Baseten customers. Probes read activations at every agent step, and a separate model is called only on flagged steps. On Kimi K3, Goodfire reports catching 94% of malicious hacking sessions across ~1,500 runs for about $51, against ~$233 for a cheap model checking every step. It also flagged 8.7% of benign sessions (a 5.5% false-positive rate in other reports). These are company tests (Startup Fortune · RuntimeWire · Mezha).

The week Anthropic pulled its evals off the live internet, white-box monitoring went from lab result to a product. It only works where you hold the weights, so open models (Kimi K3) go first; closed-model users still rely on vendors' monitors.

IMPACT 5/10#3 POLICY · INDIA · MEITY · DATED THU 8 OCT

India's IT ministry will publish a consultation paper on AI regulation within a month, focused on safety and deepfakes, with a "techno-legal" model that puts primary responsibility on technology companies

Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced it at the India launch of the World Bank's World Development Report 2026. Safety is the priority; deepfakes, skilling, cybersecurity, user harm and AI's psychological effects are also in scope. He called deepfakes a "nuisance which can cause huge problems in society". The approach pairs rules with technical safeguards and industry involvement. It builds on the principle-based AI Governance Guidelines (Nov 2025), which chose not to propose a dedicated AI law. Binding labelling of AI-generated content has applied under the amended IT Rules since 20 Feb 2026 (ThePrint · exchange4media · Deccan Herald).

A possible turn from "existing laws first" toward AI-specific rules in a market every global lab is chasing. That "shift" is one outlet's framing; Vaishnaw also said AI cannot be regulated by law alone. Expect the paper around early-to-mid November (on the radar).

IMPACT 5/10#4 ENTERPRISE · AGENT TOOLING · EMERGING TECH · DEVELOPER ATTENTION · DATED 7–10 OCT

rea, an open-source toolkit that lets coding agents reverse engineer apps "from app behavior down to native binaries", hits #1 on GitHub trending

morluto/rea ("Reverse Engineer Anything") gives agents such as Claude Code tools for native binaries, JavaScript and Electron apps, .NET assemblies and websites. Coverage says it reached #1 on GitHub trending with 10,000+ stars days after a 7 Oct release. Star counts since then conflict widely (~10K to ~80K+ across trackers). Separately, AI-assisted decompilation tools were used to port Halo (and, using leaked source, GTA V) to the browser in days; one outlet credits rea among them, a single-source claim (GitHub · Startup Fortune · Startup Fortune (ports) · HoangYell).

Emerging-tech rule: measurable developer adoption makes it Notable. Agents are making reverse engineering cheap. That helps security audits and interoperability, and erodes the protection closed binaries gave to software and game IP. Expect DMCA fights.
