FAA halts all US departures as air traffic control communications fail nationwide

The third nationwide ground stop in FAA history; DHS sees no attack, and early reporting points to a bad network update.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceChecked on the FAA's own statement; cause, counts and quotes from ABC News, Ars Technica and Business Insider
The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for all US airports on Friday afternoon, citing communication issues affecting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers, the 22 centres that handle planes above 18,000 feet. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin called it a telecommunications outage with no indication of a "nefarious" act. Multiple sources told CBS News an update to the private network that links the air traffic control system made it reboot and crash. By the evening FlightAware counted more than 4,000 cancellations and 10,000+ delays (ABC); some flights were later allowed to depart as centres recovered. Ars Technica says it is only the third nationwide ground stop in FAA history. The contractor behind the FAA's new AI congestion tool, and Microsoft, both said they were not the cause (Business Insider).

So what: a single network change grounded the world's busiest airspace for hours, a day after a Microsoft 365 outage traced to a network capacity upgrade. Banks and insurers should expect claims, travel-cover and business-interruption questions, and every operations team should re-read its own change-control rules for shared networks.

ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI's agents pay with your card, without seeing it

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its dots agents choose and complete purchases. It starts with a limited number of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18+ with a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. The agent pays with a payment token rather than the card number, users review the items, total and card first, and every purchase is approved with Face ID or a fingerprint, keeping existing card rewards and protections. OpenAI plans spending limits and rules by category or time window so agents can be pre-approved; CNBC reports OpenAI is not taking a share of transaction revenue at launch. OpenAI warns some sites and fraud systems will still block agent purchases.

So what: this is the card networks' tokenised agent-payment rails going live inside the biggest consumer AI app. Issuers need to decide how their fraud models treat agent-initiated, biometrically approved transactions, who is liable when an agent buys the wrong thing, and how disputes will work.

#13

Claude helps surface 663 theorems hidden in Kurt Gödel's notebooks

NOTABLETwo or more outletsResearch · mathematics · 13 Nov
Since 2017 Jan von Plato at the University of Helsinki has studied 16 notebooks, 1,009 pages, that Kurt Gödel wrote in the early 1940s in an obsolete German shorthand, at about a page a week. In September he gave his transcriptions to Claude, which produced a digest within days showing Gödel had proved hundreds of unknown theorems. Asked to extract every theorem Gödel proved, sketched or had an idea for, Claude found 663. Checked in the Coq proof assistant, about 400 were verified as stated, about 200 needed repair and about 50 were false, the Guardian reports. Claude did not read the shorthand; von Plato did.

So what: a good example of AI as a research accelerant on archives too large for a human: the model did the reading and cataloguing, a proof assistant checked the results, and a human expert framed the work. The same pattern fits legacy code and document archives in banks.

WHAT CHANGED Iran's Guards claim a strike on a US radar in Bahrain after a strike on Sirik; CENTCOM says it shot down two drones and nothing was hit.
Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it hit a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, home of the US Fifth Fleet, in response to an "enemy" strike on a telecommunications tower in Sirik, Hormozgan province. CENTCOM said two one-way attack drones were launched toward Bahrain and both were shot down, with no injuries or damage. The Guard also claimed a tanker hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz, which is unverified. The IEA said the same day that the oil market has returned to surplus (separate card).

So what: the first Iranian claim against a US target in Bahrain in months. Even denied, it keeps a risk premium on Gulf shipping and insurance just as crude prices ease.

Paraguay widens charges against ex-president Peña over Ueno Bank's collapse

MONITORTwo or more outletsBanking · Paraguay · 13 Nov
WHAT CHANGED Prosecutors seek wider charges against ousted president Peña and confirm Ueno assets abroad; 1.8M depositors partly repaid.
Prosecutors confirmed Ueno Bank-linked assets abroad and are seeking wider charges against impeached president Santiago Peña, who is in pre-trial detention and accused of using his office to benefit the digital bank, AFP reports. The central bank says 1.8 million depositors have had money back from the guarantee fund, though Ueno claimed 2.9 million customers and much of its money is still missing; 27 executives face charges.

So what: a digital bank that held deposits for nearly half the country, and government funds, shows what concentration and political ties can do; regulators elsewhere will cite it.
