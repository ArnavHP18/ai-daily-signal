The number printed: $96.2 billion — and NVIDIA handed the margin straight to the memory makers

Six editions of this radar pointed at one date, and it landed after last edition closed. NVIDIA's Q2 FY27, reported after the US close on 26 August, came in at $96.2B in revenue — up 106% year over year and 18% sequentially, against the company's own $91.0B ±2% guide and a consensus that compilers had put around $91.9–92.3B. The stock closed the regular session down ~1.6% at $209.66 and traded up ~4% to about $218 after hours.

So what: the demand question is now answered for four quarters — nobody buying accelerators should still be modelling a 2026 air pocket. The open question moved one layer down the bill of materials: a full point of NVIDIA gross margin, guided away in public, is the first hard number attached to the memory supercycle, and it is the number your own 2027 compute budget should be indexed to.

IMPACT 7/10#2 HARDWARE · SILICON

NVIDIA opens up the Vera CPU: 88 Olympus cores on one monolithic die, designed for agents

At Hot Chips 2026 NVIDIA detailed the CPU half of Vera Rubin, and the design choices are the story. Vera is an Arm server CPU with 88 cores on NVIDIA's own new Olympus core — the first time NVIDIA has designed the core itself rather than licensing an Arm design. Two hardware threads per core gives 176 threads; TDP is configurable 250W–450W; 164MB L3 plus 2MB L2 per core; a SOCAMM2 LPDDR5X memory system up to 1.5TB at 9600 MT/s and ~1.2 TB/s bandwidth; cores tied together by a second-generation Scalable Coherency Fabric. Crucially it is a monolithic compute die, deliberately breaking from the chiplet designs of its x86 competitors. NVIDIA positions it explicitly as the host for agentic AI — Python runtimes, code compilation, data preparation, agent orchestration, browser processes (NVIDIA Technical Blog, Tom's Hardware, ServeTheHome). Correction to a widely-circulated summary: Vera is monolithic, not "six chiplets on an interposer."

The agent boom has a CPU-shaped bottleneck — orchestration, tool calls and interpreters are single-thread-latency work, not matrix work — and NVIDIA has now built silicon aimed squarely at it. If your agent stack is host-CPU bound today, this is the part of Vera Rubin that changes your cost per run.

IMPACT 7/10#4 RESEARCH · SECURITY

Mandiant publishes its agentic vulnerability-discovery harness — 100+ true-positive criticals in two days

Published 18 August and surfaced in security press through the week of 24 August: Mandiant released the architecture and early results of its Agentic Vulnerability Discovery Harness. The headline result is a single incident-response engagement in which the harness found more than 100 true-positive critical vulnerabilities in two days. Across ten months of use the reported totals are tens of millions of lines of code processed, thousands of analysis pipelines run, tens of thousands of findings generated, 12 assigned CVEs and roughly another dozen findings in active disclosure (eSecurity Planet). It sits directly opposite this arc's other half: the same week's reporting carries Veracode's finding that AI-generated code has been stuck at a 56% security pass rate across 100+ models over four years, with ~44% of generation tasks producing an OWASP Top 10 vulnerability (Veracode/BusinessWire, 28 July). Agents are now measurably better at finding vulnerabilities than at not writing them.

Two asymmetries compound: agentic discovery is scaling fast and asymmetrically favours whoever runs it at volume, while agent-written code security has flatlined for four years. If you ship AI-generated code, assume someone else's harness reads it before your reviewers do.

Promoted from Tracking: the >15% server price rise now has a disclosed number behind it

WHAT CHANGED Escalated out of Tracking. Since 20 August the reported >15% price increase on Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell systems had rested on a single originating trade report. NVIDIA's Q3 gross-margin guide — ~74%, down from 75%, with memory and wafer costs named — is the first figure the company itself has put on that pressure. The rumour is now a disclosed cost line.
The underlying supply picture has not eased. HBM consumed 23% of global DRAM wafers in 2026, up from 8% in 2024, and each gigabyte of HBM takes roughly three times the wafer capacity of standard DRAM. Conventional DRAM contract prices rose 58–63% quarter-on-quarter in Q2 2026, after a 90–95% surge in Q1; Samsung and SK hynix lifted 2026 HBM3E supply prices by close to 20% before the year began; and SK hynix has moved to long-term contracts that remove the industry-standard price cap, letting spot increases pass straight through (TrendForce, Tom's Hardware). Today's AWS announcement adds a twist: NVIDIA is now co-developing custom high-bandwidth memory (NVHBM) with Annapurna Labs — memory is becoming something the accelerator vendor designs, not just buys.

One point of gross margin at NVIDIA's scale is the cheapest possible early warning — if the vendor with the most pricing power in the industry can only hold 74%, assume your own 2027 hardware quotes move by more than that.
