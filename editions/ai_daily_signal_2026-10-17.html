■ Today's Lead · New · Fri 16 Oct · Day 1 of the china-ai-export-controls arc

China proposes export controls on 23 AI technologies, including large models, agents and computing clusters, and starts registering exporters now

On 16 October China's Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology published a draft (Announcement No. 48 of 2026) that would add 40 technologies to the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited and Restricted from Export. 23 of them are AI: large models, agents, computing clusters and information processing. The draft splits the list into 14 outright prohibitions and 26 items that would need a licence. It is a proposal, with comments open until 15 November, but an interim registration requirement for exporters of the covered technologies took effect the same day. Bloomberg reported it the day it was issued.

So what: China's open-weight models (Qwen, GLM, DeepSeek, Kimi, MiMo) are the default open base for much of the world, including for many US and Indian builders. Until now the question of whether Beijing would restrict them was a July FT report about consultations. Now there is a draft text. The published summaries say API, website, app and SaaS access is exempt, so the thing to watch is weights and on-premises deployment abroad. Analysts disagree on whether open-source release of a covered model counts as an export.

Which models. Secondary reports give the model thresholds differently. One says a model's core technology is covered if it meets any of three tests: training compute of 10^26 FLOPs, 2 trillion or more parameters, or capabilities judged "globally leading". Another gives only the parameter and "world-leading" tests. One analysis says the computing-cluster items mainly target clusters of more than 10,000 accelerators. None of these figures has been checked against the announcement text here.

What stays open. Summaries say calling a Chinese model through an API, website, app or SaaS platform is not an export, and that technology already public through open-source releases or papers is exempt. A commentary reads it more narrowly: a restricted model installed on servers in another country would be an export, and prohibited-tier technology could not go out through overseas open-source channels. The second reading would bite on future open-weight releases, not those already published.

Background. In July the FT reported that the Commerce Ministry was consulting Alibaba, ByteDance and Zhipu (Z.ai) on limiting overseas transfer of training data and whether foreign users should be allowed to download the weights of the most advanced models. The draft also reportedly covers chip designs and foreign acquisitions of strategic AI firms.

Watch for. The official text and its thresholds; whether Qwen, GLM or Kimi release cadences or licences change before 15 November; how Hugging Face and US/Indian cloud hosts of Chinese weights respond; and Washington's reaction, since this mirrors US export-control design.

IMPACT 6/10#2 MODELS · EMERGING TECH · VISUAL REASONING · FRI 16 OCT

Elorian releases Glimpse, its first model: it re-examines images and video during reasoning, zooming into regions and re-scanning frames, instead of turning an image into tokens once

A research preview: API access now, chat by waitlist. Price $0.60 per M input / $3 per M output. Elorian says a small team trained it on a few hundred GPUs and that it beats GPT-6 Astra on scientific chart reasoning and short-form video captioning; those results are the company's own and untested by outsiders. Elorian was founded by Andrew Dai (about 14 years at Google Brain and DeepMind) and Yinfei Yang (ex-Apple) and raised a $55M seed in April (Striker, Menlo, Altimeter).

A new approach rather than a version bump: today's frontier models mostly describe an image once and then reason in text. If 'looking again' holds up in independent tests, it matters for charts, engineering drawings and video. New watchlist entry.

IMPACT 5/10#5 POLICY · AGENT CONTROLS · MICROSOFT · POSTED SAT 10 OCT

Satya Nadella calls for an 'emergency brake' on AI models and says companies should treat advanced models as potential insider threats

In a post on X, Microsoft's CEO asked for: a person with authority who can pause or stop a model mid-task; safety controls, permissions and activity logs kept in a separate layer the model cannot alter; tamper-proof, human-readable records of meaningful actions; no model grading its own work; independent audits; and more transparency about security failures. 'We must assume a model is compromised and contain it from the start,' he wrote, as quoted via The Verge. Outlets differ on the exact wording of the logging proposal.

The CEO of the largest enterprise AI vendor is now describing the same controls US bank supervisors are asking about and the White House notification rule implies (both in Tracking). It reads like a spec for agent platforms, including Microsoft's own.

IMPACT 4/10#7 RESEARCH · AGENT BENCHMARKS · VALS AI · ~9–11 OCT

Vals AI finds coding-agent teams cost 1.8–5.1x as much as a single agent and rarely score better

On Vibe Code Bench, Vals ran GPT-6 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5 alone and as teams, at medium and maximum effort. Only Sol at medium effort showed a statistically significant gain from teaming. Opus at maximum effort as a team scored highest (93.2%) but had the largest cost multiple. Most of the extra spend was cached input, because each sub-agent holds its own copy of the spec and context. Teams sometimes finished faster.

A rare controlled test of a design choice that many agent products default to. Before paying for orchestration, measure whether it beats one well-prompted agent.
