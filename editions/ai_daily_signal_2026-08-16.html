Anthropic's quarter lands above $11.5B — a more than 14x year-on-year jump, with the first positive adjusted operating income, disclosed straight into IPO prep

Anthropic's preliminary second-quarter revenue exceeded $11.5B, up from $787M in the same quarter a year earlier and from $4.73B in Q1 2026, and the company also posted positive adjusted operating income for the quarter. The figures come from internal documents seen by Bloomberg News and reported on 15 August by CNBC, Fortune and Yahoo Finance. They are explicitly preliminary and could still change. They also overshoot the company's own guidance: investor materials circulated earlier had projected roughly $10.9B of Q2 revenue and about $559M of operating income, with compute costs falling from about 71 cents per revenue dollar in Q1 to about 56 cents in Q2 (Let's Data Science, summarising the earlier projection reporting). The timing is the point: this briefing has carried an IPO-wave thread for weeks in which OpenAI's confidential S-1 has not yet flipped public. Anthropic has now put a hard revenue number and a profitability claim into the market first.

So what: the first frontier lab to show a quarter where revenue growth outran compute cost resets the benchmark every AI vendor will be priced against — if you are negotiating a 2027 Claude contract or modelling an AI supplier's durability, re-baseline on $11.5B a quarter and a positive operating line, not on the $10.9B/$559M projection that has been circulating.

IMPACT 8/10#2 MODELS · OPEN-WEIGHT

Z.ai's GLM-5.3 leads open-source agent benchmarks on post-training alone — and ships without its weights

Released 14 August. GLM-5.3 reuses the same roughly 743B-parameter base as GLM-5.2 and takes its entire gain from post-training scaling: Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 28.3 against 4.6 for its predecessor — the highest of any open-source model on that benchmark — Agents' Last Exam 28.5, CyberGym 84.5% with exploitation scores more than doubling, and a claimed 50% improvement on Z.ai's internal coding-agent benchmark (SiliconANGLE, ExplainX). The structural news is the release model: it shipped without the open weights that made the GLM line matter, with Z.ai saying API access and weights arrive in stages after a safety evaluation, roughly two weeks out (Fello AI). Benchmark figures are vendor-run.

An open-weight leader that gates its weights behind a safety review is the Western frontier-lab playbook arriving in the open ecosystem — if your fallback plan assumes "top open model = downloadable on day one," that assumption expired on 14 August.

IMPACT 8/10#3 POLICY · CHINA

Apple becomes the first foreign company cleared to offer its own generative model in China

Reuters reported on 14 August, citing three sources, that Apple has trained a China-specific large language model with Alibaba's technical support, and that the Cyberspace Administration of China registered Apple's generative AI service last month. Every generative model offered publicly in China must sit on that register, which has been dominated by Baidu, Alibaba, ByteDance and DeepSeek — and which OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude have never cleared (MacRumors, Quartz, Seeking Alpha). It marks a shift from Apple's earlier plan of leaning wholly on a partner's model; Alibaba has said Qwen would power Apple Intelligence text and image features on iOS, iPadOS, macOS and visionOS for Chinese users. Rollout is expected "in the coming months" — no date.

The register just proved passable for a foreign firm, which reframes China market access as a compliance-and-partnership problem rather than a closed door — but the price of entry, on this worked example, is a domestic technical partner and a China-specific model.

IMPACT 7/10#6 RESEARCH · CRYPTOGRAPHY

Today's research beat: Google pushes HEIR, a compiler that turns a trained model into one that runs on encrypted inputs

Google's security blog surfaced this week with "How Google is Making Private AI Practical with Homomorphic Encryption", positioning HEIR — Homomorphic Encryption Intermediate Representation — as an open-source MLIR-based compiler toolchain that converts pretrained models to run inference on encrypted inputs, so the serving host never sees the plaintext. Developers annotate which Python types are secret and the compiler handles the rest; there is partial StableHLO support for trained inference models, a demonstrated TensorFlow Lite path producing a private inference in 16 seconds for a three-layer network, and a companion library, Jaxite, for running FHE on GPUs and TPUs (google/heir, heir.dev, Google Developers Blog). Date caveat: the repository and the underlying HEIR compiler paper long predate this week — what is new is the productisation push and its distribution, not the project.

Sixteen seconds for a three-layer net is not production inference, but it is the first credible developer on-ramp to encrypted serving — worth a spike now if you have a regulated workload you cannot send to a cloud in plaintext.

INTERPOL: AI is now behind 55% of reported cybercrime across Africa, with losses up 2.5x since 2024

INTERPOL's African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026, built on survey responses from 36 African member countries, finds 55% of reported cybercrimes on the continent are now AI-enabled, and that financial losses have risen from $192M to $484M since 2024 on the back of AI-assisted scams, credential theft and automated social engineering (INTERPOL, JURIST). Business email compromise has become materially more convincing, with Africa-based operators targeting victims in Europe and North America across jurisdictions; a partner recorded more than 600,000 sextortion detections in the review period. Date honestly: published around 4–5 August — carded now as this briefing's first coverage of it, not as breaking news.

This is the first multi-country law-enforcement baseline putting a share number on AI-enabled crime — cite it when you need a non-vendor figure for why fraud controls have to assume synthetic voice, video and email as default.
