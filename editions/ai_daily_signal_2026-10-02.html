■ Today's Lead · New · Day 8 of the decision-model arc

Amazon releases its own Jev: an open 2B decision model that picks from a list in about 100ms

On Thursday AWS released Strands Decider 2B, an open-source decision model under Apache 2.0. Like TypeSafe's Jev, it does not write text. It picks one of a set of answers the developer has defined in advance and says how confident it is (TechCrunch, SiliconANGLE, VentureBeat). It is built on a Qwen3.5-2B backbone with a roughly 1M-parameter "pointer head" that scores the options directly instead of generating tokens. AWS reports about 72% accuracy on the public JevBench set, with a 106ms median and 296ms p95 on a single RTX 3090. It is small enough to run on a laptop. It started as a side project by Amazon distinguished engineer Marc Brooker, which briefly topped the JevBench leaderboard before Strands Labs turned it into a product.

So what: this is the first hyperscaler to ship an open model in the category this briefing has watched since Jev launched on 15 September. Teams that route, classify or gate agent steps can now try a free, self-hosted decision model from a vendor they already buy from. The paid APIs (Jev, OpenAI's still-unpriced Decisions API) will have to compete on calibration and accuracy, not on access.

Where it sits on the leaderboard. The category now has a public benchmark. JevBench v1.3.0 scores 52 systems on 534 decisions (220 of them hard) across four equally weighted axes: accuracy, calibration, speed and cost. TypeSafe's own Jev 1.13.0 leads at 74.4, ahead of SemIf (a Qwen3.5-4B build) at 73.1 and djev at 73.0 (aggregator summary, JevBench page). Amazon's 72% is an accuracy figure on an earlier public set (v19), so it cannot be placed directly against the composite scores. This briefing has not seen a v1.3.0 entry for Decider.

The open field is filling up. Besides Laya (Convai, 421M) and Decider (Amazon, 2B), LibertAI's Deem 9B is an Apache-2.0 decision model on Qwen3.5-9B. It reads the decision from a single prefill pass with no decoding, abstains when unsure, and reports 65.8 on JevBench's hard tasks (68.9 with its extended-reasoning mode) (model card). That is the vendor's own figure.

What is still missing. No Google or Anthropic decision model; still no price for OpenAI's Decisions API (re-searched today, nothing found); and no independent calibration test of Decider. Calibration matters most here, because a decision model is only useful if "90% sure" really means 90%.

Watch next. Decider's JevBench v1.3.0 score; whether AWS puts it behind a Bedrock endpoint; and whether TypeSafe answers with a price cut or an open checkpoint.

IMPACT 8/10#2 POLICY · LAB SELF-GOVERNANCE · DATED 29 SEP

The White House Accord on Super Intelligence: six labs promise to police themselves

Older news, new to this briefing. On 29 September Trump posted a one-page "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities", signed with Sundar Pichai (Google), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), Greg Brockman (OpenAI), Elon Musk (xAI) and Jensen Huang (Nvidia) (CNN, NPR). The signatories pledge robust internal controls on models in training and development, covering cyber, bio and chemical threats and systems that "hack or access technical systems in unintended ways". They also pledge an internal team to check those controls work, an independent external auditor or evaluator, and an independent board committee that receives reports from both (UPI, Forbes). There are no penalties and no regulator. Trump called it "morally binding". Sam Altman stayed at DevDay and sent Brockman. Critics point out that each company picks its own auditor, and the accord says nothing about accreditation, access or whether results are published. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries called an unenforceable agreement "not the right response at this moment" (Yahoo News, CNBC analysis). The signed document misspells "United States" (TechCrunch).

This is now the federal answer to frontier-AI oversight: self-chosen auditors and board committees, with no statute behind them. Watch which auditors the labs name and whether any of them publish findings. Each of the six now has a public promise it can be held to, and the agent-escape incidents tracked here are the obvious first test.

IMPACT 7/10#3 HARDWARE · EXPORT CONTROLS · COMPUTE LEASING

Tencent reportedly leases ~100,000 AI chips from Oracle for $7B, outside China

Per a report first credited to the Financial Times, Tencent has signed a five-year lease worth about $7B for roughly 100,000 advanced AI chips spread across several Oracle data centres in Southeast Asia. That works out to about $14,000 per chip per year. Roughly 30% is said to be paid upfront (TrendForce, Benzinga, Quartz). The chips never enter China. US export rules ban selling top accelerators to Chinese buyers but still let them rent capacity abroad. The upfront payment fits the large prepayments that turned Tencent's Q2 free cash flow negative, though neither company has confirmed the deal (Financial News). Oracle shares rose on the report (NAI500).

This is the biggest known use of the cloud-access loophole, roughly seven times Tencent's earlier ~15,000-chip rental through Japan's Datasection. Expect pressure in Congress to close the gap. Oracle's backlog now carries some China policy risk.

IMPACT 6/10#5 ENTERPRISE · BANKING · DEEP BEAT

Barclays puts Claude Code in front of half its developers by year-end

Barclays is widening its use of Claude to modernise legacy systems and cut operating costs (Anthropic, Bloomberg). The bank expects 50% of its software developers on Claude Code by the end of 2026 and a majority of engineers in 2027. Results already reported: a RAG-based Colleague Knowledge Assistant used by 16,000 Barclays UK staff, with 1M+ searches run on behalf of 20M+ retail customers, and Global Markets teams using Claude to sort and route about 120,000 client emails a day. Co-COO Craig Bright: "Software engineering and cyber security are both being reshaped by increasingly capable AI systems." The rollout is part of a multi-year efficiency programme targeting about £2B in savings, of which AI is one part.

The first numbers here are about volume, not coding output, so treat the 50% developer target as the thing to check. Other UK banks will be measured against it.

Armadin's always-on "attacker swarm" raises $255.5M at a $2.5B+ valuation

The product is an agentic attacker swarm: specialised agents on custom models that run continuously, chaining vulnerabilities together the way a nation-state intruder would, in place of periodic penetration tests. Founder Kevin Mandia (Mandiant, sold to Google for $5.4B) has now raised $255.5M at more than $2.5B (TechCrunch, SiliconANGLE). That is up from a $189.9M seed and Series A in March led by Accel (TechCrunch, March).

Attackers already use agent swarms, as the payload datasets in Still Tracking show. Defenders are now buying the same technique as a subscription, so red-team budgets will move from annual engagements to continuous services.

Volantis A-1: optical links to break the inference memory wall ($88M Series A)

The product is A-1, an inference system that replaces part of the electrical path between AI chips and memory with optical links. Volantis says it is designed to run 20T+ parameter models at up to 10,000 tokens/s per user, with first systems to customers in 2027. The $88M Series A was co-led by Lachy Groom and Abstract Ventures, with John Doerr and angels including Dwarkesh Patel, Naveen Rao and Sholto Douglas (SiliconANGLE, PR Newswire). With HBM sold out into 2027, memory bandwidth is the constraint worth attacking. But the specs are company targets and nothing is independently tested (FourWeekMBA).

IMPACT 5/10#8 RESEARCH · COMPUTE · PEER-REVIEWED

Google's peer-reviewed orbital data-centre paper: Starship must fly ~1,800 times first

Google released a peer-reviewed version of its Project Suncatcher white paper, to be published in Joule. It sets out how expensive launch would have to become for orbital compute to compete with ground data centres. Google estimates Starship would need about 1,800 launches over ten years (~180 a year, 200 tonnes each) to bring prices near $200/kg by 2035 (TechCrunch). The design is clusters of 81 satellites flying in close formation. Note: TechCrunch's URL and some syndications say 1,600 while its headline says 1,800 (Yahoo).

This is the clearest public condition yet for "AI in space": until launch costs hit about $200/kg, orbital compute is an R&D project, not capacity you can plan around.

IMPACT 5/10#9 MODELS · NEW APPROACH · DATED 21 SEP

Apple's LensVLM-9B reads long documents as compressed images and zooms in only where needed

Found by today's Hugging Face trending check, so it is new here but not new this week. LensVLM-9B appeared on Apple's Hugging Face profile on 21 September, with code on GitHub the next day. It renders long text into images at 5x, 10x or 15x compression, reads those, and uses learned tools to expand only the pages relevant to a question (Hugging Face, Pillitteri). It is a fine-tune of Alibaba's Qwen3.5-9B under Apple's ML research licence, which does not allow commercial use, and it now sits among the top 30 trending models.

Compressing text visually is a way to fit long context into fewer tokens. It is notable that Apple builds on Qwen rather than its own base model. Research use only for now.

IMPACT 5/10#10 MODELS · AI-BUILT MODELS · DATED 27 SEP

NaiveAI's Naive-N0.5-Flash: a 309B open model the company says AI agents largely built

Beijing start-up NaiveAI released Naive-N0.5-Flash on 27 September. It is an open-weight 309B mixture-of-experts model with 15.5B active parameters, a native 1M-token context and its own runtime, NaiveRT. It uses only sliding-window and DeepSeek-style sparse attention, with no full-attention layers (LLM Gateway timeline). NaiveAI says models wrote code, ran experiments, monitored results and proposed changes during development. Humans set the objectives and evaluation standards and made the key calls (RuntimeWire).

"AI built this model" is the company's own claim, and no independent benchmark has been found. The attention design without full-attention layers is the part that can be checked.
