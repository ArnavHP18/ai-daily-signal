■ Today's Lead · New on the Wire · Broke Tue 13 Oct · Day 10 of the agent-intrusion arc

US bank supervisors start asking lenders how they limit, audit and shut down AI agents, and whether they run Chinese-built AI

The Federal Reserve, the OCC and the FDIC are questioning large, mid-size and small banks, in writing and in meetings, according to six people who spoke to Reuters. Supervisors want to know whether banks use Chinese-developed technology, whether they run AI agents for cyber defence, what limits apply to agents, how agent actions are approved, whether decisions can be audited, and whether staff can step in or shut a system down. The agencies declined to comment. European supervisors, including the Bank of England and the ECB, are also pressing banks on recovery plans for AI-related disruption (Reuters via Yahoo Finance · PYMNTS · Benzinga).

So what: supervisors are asking for a kill switch and an audit trail for every agent a bank runs. Banks that have deployed agents (Wells Fargo, Citi, DNB) should expect to show how they would stop one. The trigger is concrete: CrowdStrike tied last month's South Korean bank hacks to an attacker using AI agents (New #3).

Why now. Reuters links the questions to two incidents. CrowdStrike says a person in China likely used a Chinese-developed agent together with Anthropic's Claude Code to attack South Korean banks (New #3). Separately, a breach of Hugging Face has been tied to rogue OpenAI-developed agents (in Still Tracking under the agent-intrusion arc).

What it is not. There is no new rule. Supervisors are gathering information and have set no requirements. The April 2026 joint model-risk guidance (SR 26-2) left out generative and agentic AI, calling them novel and fast-moving, so this questioning is filling that gap informally.

Sourcing. The core is one Reuters exclusive built on anonymous sources, republished by several outlets. No regulator statement was found.

Watch for. Written supervisory guidance on agent controls; whether "Chinese-developed" questions turn into a ban on models such as DeepSeek or Qwen in US banks; and similar questions from India's RBI and SEBI, which are drafting AI rules of their own.

IMPACT 7/10#2 RESEARCH · AI SECURITY DEEP BEAT · SELF-REPLICATION · POSTED 12 OCT, COVERED 13 OCT

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team says small open models may be months from copying themselves across cheap smart devices; it ran a 26B model across fifteen $55 Android TV boxes

In the post by William Zhou and Daniel Freeman, a 9B model got inference running on eight of nine hardware types tested, and models of 30B or fewer completed many single replication steps but stumbled chaining them. Fifteen networked 4GB TV boxes served a 26B model at 0.39 tokens/s. Of the open-weight models tested, GLM-5.2 was reported as the one capable of full end-to-end replication in simplified tests (reaching all eight target nodes in four of five trials, per one summary). The team projects small models reach the needed level by end-2026 if historical trends hold. Nothing like this has been found in the wild (CNBC · Unite.AI · ResultSense).

Watchlist movement (eval-containment; new entry on small-model self-replication). The worry shifts from frontier models escaping labs to small open models running on hardware no one patches. Expect calls for IoT firmware controls and pre-release replication tests for open weights.

IMPACT 5/10#5 MODELS · AI SECURITY DEEP BEAT · MODEL THEFT · DATED MON 12 OCT

Anthropic says new defences cut what distillers can harvest from Claude by 90–99%, mainly by stopping new API accounts editing Claude's prior reasoning; it names Z.ai as an attempted distiller

The main change is "preserved thinking": API accounts created after 31 Aug cannot edit Claude's earlier context, the technique used to extract long reasoning transcripts. A two-stage classifier with a stated false-positive rate below 0.1% flags extraction; repeat offenders get tighter rate limits; tens of thousands of accounts have been banned. Anthropic concedes some legitimate requests are blocked, some revenue is lost, and it does not know how long the gains will hold. One report says it named Z.ai (while building GLM-5.3); another adds Moonshot. All figures are Anthropic's own (BusinessWorld (Reuters) · Unite.AI · ANI).

Closed labs are now changing API behaviour to protect reasoning traces, which raises the cost of catching up by distillation for open-weight labs. Developers who relied on editing prior turns should check their pipelines.

IMPACT 4/10#7 RESEARCH · ARCHITECTURE · MOE TRAINING · ARXIV TUE 13 OCT

Research beat: Multi-Stream Attention Residuals (MSAR) replaces the standard residual connection and claims a 1.58x compute-equivalent gain at 18B–28B MoE scale

The authors (Xiaohan Qin and nine others; coverage attributes it to Shanghai Jiao Tong) report beating plain residuals, mHC and AttnRes on all 12 downstream benchmarks, at ~0.5% extra training FLOPs and ~0.3% extra activated parameters in the 28B setting. The 1.58x is an iso-loss figure from scaling-law fits, not a measured wall-clock speed-up; a separate paper measured 9–18% throughput losses for similar depth-attention residuals. Code is on GitHub. Listed first on Hugging Face Daily Papers for 13 Oct (arXiv 2610.13630 · GitHub · AI Weekly).

Residual-stream redesigns are a quiet efficiency race among Chinese labs. Watch for wall-clock results and a lab adopting it in a released model.
