US proposes tariffs on 14 economies as inflation hits 3.8% and tech stocks swing

India faces up to 25%, Vietnam 35%, the EU 15%; hearings in January, so nothing takes effect this year

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceChecked on the USTR release and BEA's PCE and GDP releases (opened); closing prices from Yahoo Finance data

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer ruled on 25 Nov that 14 economies' industrial policies create 'structural excess capacity' and are actionable under Section 301, and proposed tariffs on all their goods, net of existing duties and with product exemptions: up to 35% on Vietnam (20% with a trade deal), 25% on India, 20% on China (stacking on existing levies) and Singapore, 18% on Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand, and 15% on the EU, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland. Mexico and Norway were not ruled on. Comments are due 5 Jan and hearings start 25 Jan 2027. The same morning BEA reported October PCE inflation at 3.8% (core 3.3%) and revised Q3 growth up to 4.3%.

Markets lurched: the Nasdaq 100 fell nearly 5% intraday and briefly sat 20% below its October record (bear-market territory) before buyers returned. It closed down 0.6%; the S&P 500 lost 0.3% and the Dow gained 0.3%. Nvidia closed 3.9% lower at $149.30. US markets are shut for Thanksgiving and open for a short session on Friday.

So what: Greer calls it a 'top-up' that replaces earlier tariffs, but with inflation near twice the Fed's target a December hike looks more likely; exporters in India and South-East Asia, and the banks financing their trade, have until 5 Jan to make their case.

#3Research · AI mathematics · 24-25 NovIMPACT 8/10

OpenAI, Anthropic and Google post a burst of AI proofs of decades-old problems

OpenAI added nine manuscripts to its public math repository on 24 Nov, produced by an unreleased internal model with selected Lean formalisations. They claim the matrix multiplication exponent ω is exactly 9/4 (a matching lower bound to October's upper bound), that VP and VNP differ (algebraic analogue of P vs NP), near-tight bounds for the Fourier transform and integer multiplication, a strongly polynomial algorithm for linear programming (Smale's ninth problem) and a disproof of the polynomial Hirsch conjecture. Anthropic posted its own VP ≠ VNP proof the same day, Latent Space reports. Google Research's Cogentic agent, running on Gemini 4, posted six papers on 25 Nov with no human help beyond the prompt, including unconditional integer factorisation in time LN[1/3, 105] and faster discrete logarithms in small-characteristic fields. Scott Aaronson calls it 'a whole new continent of mathematical truth'; experts have not yet reviewed most results.

So what for banks: the factoring result proves a bound of the same family as today's best method, but its constant (105) makes it far slower in practice, so RSA and elliptic-curve keys are not affected. Rumours that a lab can factor in polynomial time are unverified. The pace still argues for keeping post-quantum (and 'post-AI') migration plans on schedule.
