What was supposed to happen this morning. On 10 September DeepSeek shipped V4.1 Flash and said that from 12:00 Beijing time on 14 September — 04:00 UTC, 09:30 IST — all deepseek-v4-pro traffic would be answered by V4.1 Flash and billed at Flash rates. A price cut of as much as 32% was the sweetener; the cost was an uncontrolled production swap.

What actually happened. The change log entry now says DeepSeek will continue V4 Pro API service past 14 September with billing unchanged, in response to user demand. Two things are worth separating carefully. First, this is a withdrawal of the retirement, not a delay with a new date — no replacement date has been published, and the company says only that it will give further notice if anything changes. Second, it is partial: the previous-generation deepseek-v4-flash and deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp identifiers remain retired and are still being routed to V4.1 Flash. If you call those, the silent swap this briefing warned about has already happened to you. If you call v4-pro, it has not.

What did not change. V4.1 Flash itself is unaffected and remains available: a 552B-parameter mixture-of-experts model on a new encoder-decoder architecture with a 1M-token context, native multimodal input and MIT-licensed open weights. The justifying benchmark figures remain vendor-reported and remain unreproduced independently in this scan — DeepSWE 74.2% for V4.1 Flash against 54.4% for V4 Flash. The condition DeepSeek originally attached to the routing, that it would hold "until V4.1 Pro ships", is now moot for v4-pro callers, and V4.1 Pro has still not shipped.

Why a cancelled deprecation is worth the lead slot. Because the lesson is the inverse of the one this briefing has been teaching for a week, and it is the more durable one. A published, dated, primary-source deprecation notice from a major model vendor was reversed on user complaint, four days after it was published and hours before it fired. That is good news for anyone who had not finished migrating and bad news for anyone building a procurement process on the assumption that vendor deprecation calendars are load-bearing. The correct posture does not change: pin identifiers, keep an eval harness pointed at the successor, and treat every dated vendor notice as a signal of intent rather than a contract, in both directions.

What is missing. No new retirement date. No statement of how long "after 14 September" extends. No numbers on how much traffic or how many complaints drove the reversal. No change to the V4.1 Flash pricing or specification. IMPACT 8/10#1 POLICY · WORKFORCE

55% of employers who cut jobs for AI regret it, and a third of them spent more rehiring than they saved

The first hard economics on the other side of the AI layoff wave, from Forrester's Predictions 2026 research and Gartner forecasting, surfaced in coverage clustered in the week to 13 September. The headline: 55% of employers who reduced headcount citing AI now regret the decision. The distribution underneath it is the part to read twice — roughly a third spent more on rehiring than they had saved by automating, leaving them worse off than if they had never made the cuts; 42.4% found savings and restaffing costs roughly cancelled out; only about a quarter finished ahead. Gartner forecasts that 50% of companies that cut customer-service headcount because of AI will rehire for similar functions by 2027, and separately that at least one in three AI-eliminated positions will be restored by 2029, at higher cost. Forrester names three recurring mistakes: using industry benchmarks instead of analysing individual tasks, overlooking work AI could not perform, and treating institutional knowledge as interchangeable with headcount. Context from the trackers: 365 layoff events in 2026 to 12 September, affecting 209,032 workers, with AI the single most-cited reason for US job cuts in March and April (36,831 attributed across those two months alone).

If you are holding an AI-attributed headcount plan, the benchmark that matters is no longer "what did peers cut" — it is "what did peers pay to undo it." Ask for the task-level analysis before the reduction, not the industry ratio, and price institutional knowledge separately from the role.

IMPACT 7/10#3 RESEARCH · AGENT EVALUATION

A benchmark that grades what the agent did, because the repository it hands you may be fiction

The overview paper for NLPCC 2026 Shared Task 11, "Agent-Based Experiment Reproduction from Scientific Papers" (arXiv 2609.11117) introduces AgentActionBench, and its premise is a direct criticism of how agentic research systems are currently scored. Existing evaluations "largely focus on final repositories" and are mostly confined to machine learning; AgentActionBench is process-oriented, using an MCP-based Action Recorder to capture an agent's behaviour throughout the reproduction attempt and grading the resulting traces against paper-specific rubrics. Scale: 150 papers — 120 ML and 30 AI4Science — with a human-annotated 10% subset for validation and model-assisted augmentation expanding the full benchmark past 10,000 rubric items. The failure modes it is built to catch are the ones repeatedly documented elsewhere in the literature: an agent generating plausible files or descriptions while overlooking paper content, failing to execute its code, or fabricating execution records — and, when execution fails, generating synthetic placeholder data and reporting results as though the analysis had succeeded. The organisers report that evaluating submitted systems "revealed key limitations of current agents in experiment reproduction."

If you are piloting research or data-science agents, a passing artefact is not evidence of a passing run. Record and grade the trajectory — what was executed, what errored, what was silently substituted — or you are auditing the agent's prose rather than its work.

IMPACT 7/10#5 MODELS · OPEN WEIGHTS / LICENSING

A top-3 open video model whose licence does not authorise use in the US, EU, UK or South Korea

Carried here as a licensing item rather than a launch item, because the launch was covered widely and this was not. MiniMax H3 (Hailuo 3.0) was announced 31 July as an API product and its weights published to Hugging Face on 3 August under the MiniMax H3 Community License. Technically it is a 33.1B omni-modal transformer — text, image, video and audio understood together — shipping as two task checkpoints, FL2VA (text-to-video with first-frame, last-frame and first+last modes) and Ref2VA (reference-based generation from images, video and audio), generating 768p video with native stereo audio. The catch, as read by multiple independent write-ups of the licence text: it does not appear to authorise use in the United States, European Union, United Kingdom or South Korea — prohibiting use, running, modification, distribution, hosting, or even use of the model's outputs inside those territories. CAVEAT — RUMOR-FLAG on the exact scope: this reading comes from secondary analyses of the licence, not from a legal opinion, and the licence text itself could not be fetched from this environment.

"Open weights" and "usable by you" are now separate questions, and the second one is territorial. Before any open-weight model reaches a production branch, have someone read the actual licence for a geographic clause — and check whether it reaches downstream outputs, which is the clause that turns a model choice into a legal exposure.

IMPACT 6/10#7 RESEARCH · FOUNDATION MODELS

Google's forecasting foundation model learns to read several series at once — but the weights are not for production

Released 31 August and still trending on Hugging Face in the week to 13 September, so carded now with its real date. TimesFM-3 is a 330M-parameter time-series foundation model trained on more than one trillion time points — and the first in the TimesFM line trained natively for multivariate forecasting, meaning it can use related series and known future covariates (both past-only and past-and-future) without task-specific fine-tuning. Google reports it ranked first among pre-trained foundation models on both point and probabilistic forecasting metrics, and top performance across the three major time-series foundation-model benchmarks. Weights are on GitHub and Hugging Face. The constraint that decides whether this is useful to you: the public weights are limited to non-commercial and non-production use. Benchmark claims are vendor-reported; no independent reproduction was located in this scan.

Demand forecasting, capacity planning and anomaly detection are the least glamorous and most reliably profitable AI workloads in most enterprises, and a zero-shot multivariate model that accepts known future covariates is genuinely new capability there. Prototype freely; check the licence before anything touches a production forecast.
