Cloudflare open-sources Clef: decision models that can see, with a Jev-compatible API

On 1 October, the same day as Amazon's Decider, Cloudflare released two open-weight decision models, Clef (27B, built on Qwen3.8-27B) and Clef-flash (9B, built on Qwen3.5-9B). Like Jev, they do not write text. Each reads an input and a schema of typed questions, then returns a probability for every allowed answer in a single forward pass (Cloudflare, The Register, MarkTechPost). Two things set them apart from Jev and Decider. They keep Qwen's vision encoder, so the input can be text, JSON, images or video. And the API is Jev-compatible, so existing Jev code can switch by changing the endpoint. Weights are on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, and both models run on Workers AI (Hugging Face, changelog). Edition 104 missed this release; it is carded today with its real date.

So what: the decision-model category now has two big infrastructure companies shipping free, open models within the same day. Cloudflare's "drop-in for Jev" pitch goes straight at TypeSafe's paid API. Image and video input opens decisions Jev cannot handle, such as content moderation, screenshot-driven agent steps and visual inspection.

Cloudflare's numbers (not independently checked). Cloudflare says Clef leads 7 of 10 decision benchmarks. It reports median decision latency of about 209ms for Clef and 39ms for Clef-flash, against about 524ms for hosted Jev; it describes Clef-flash as up to 13x faster than Jev. These are vendor figures, and this briefing has not found a JevBench entry for Clef (Cloudflare, Developers Digest).

How it is trained. Inference is a prefill-only pass through Qwen, after which every valid choice in the schema is scored in parallel. Cloudflare post-trained with what it calls Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD), which gives partial credit to nearby choices on ordered scales. Calibration matters most for a decision model: "90% sure" has to mean 90% for a confidence threshold to be safe.

The business model. The weights are free. Cloudflare will fine-tune Clef on customer data with its forward-deployed engineers, and says it is building a self-serve platform to capture data, fine-tune and redeploy on Workers AI. That is the hosting-plus-tuning play AWS has not yet announced for Decider.

Watch next. Independent calibration tests of Clef, Decider and Jev on the same tasks; whether TypeSafe cuts its price or opens a checkpoint; OpenAI pricing its Decisions API (re-searched today, still unpriced); and whether Google or Anthropic enter.

China-aligned hackers posed as a former White House AI official and an Anthropic employee to phish AI policy experts

Proofpoint attributes the campaigns to TA419, a China-aligned group it says supports Beijing's intelligence interests. From 8 July 2026 it impersonated a former senior leader of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, then a former State Department economist. In February it posed as a senior Anthropic employee in an email titled "Request for Feedback on Military Integration of Claude". Targets were AI-policy researchers at think tanks, universities and law firms, approached with invitations to advise on AI policy and export controls, then sent links built to steal Microsoft logins (CNN, Nextgov/FCW, Help Net Security).

AI policy, and export controls in particular, is now an intelligence target in its own right. Anyone who advises on it should treat unsolicited "please review our draft" emails from lab or government names as suspect and check them out of band.

IMPACT 7/10#3 BUSINESS · COMPUTE FINANCE · REPORTED (FT)

Amazon in talks to move ~$8B of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips off its balance sheet and lease them back

The Financial Times reported that Amazon has held talks to put about $8B of Nvidia Grace Blackwell accelerators into a special-purpose vehicle. The SPV would raise money from outside investors, mostly as debt, and Amazon would lease the chips back for its own use. The hardware sits in more than a dozen US data centres across five states, including Nevada and Virginia. Amazon declined to comment and the talks may change (SiliconANGLE, Yahoo Finance, Benzinga).

A hyperscaler that can fund itself is choosing to treat GPUs as financed assets, like aircraft. Watch for the lease term against the chips' useful life: if it is longer than Blackwell stays competitive, investors carry the obsolescence risk.

IMPACT 6/10#4 HARDWARE · ORBITAL COMPUTE · PRIMARY

Google's first TPUs are in orbit: the Suncatcher prototype launched and is working

A day after this briefing carded Google's peer-reviewed Suncatcher economics paper, the hardware flew. The Planet-built MVP satellite launched on SpaceX's Transporter-18 from Vandenberg on 1 October with about 130 other payloads. It carries four Trillium TPUs (equivalent to a Cloud TPU v6e-4 slice). Google confirmed contact and says it is operating as expected. Because it is hard to shed heat in a vacuum, the TPUs will run Gemini inference in short bursts of about 15 minutes while engineers measure radiation errors, power draw and heat rejection (Google, TNW, Mixed).

This is the first real data on how commercial AI accelerators survive in space. Read it with yesterday's paper: the chips may work, but the business case still depends on launch falling to about $200/kg.

IMPACT 5/10#6 RESEARCH · NEW APPROACH · NATURE · DATED 30 SEP

Ataraxos (Nature): superhuman at Stratego on about $8,000 of compute, using methods that carry over to other hidden-information games

A team from MIT, CMU, NYU and Stanford published Ataraxos in Nature on 30 September. It uses self-play reinforcement learning plus search at decision time, built for games with large amounts of hidden information. It beat Pim Niemeijer, the most decorated Stratego player, 15–1–4 over 20 games and went 39–2 against top players at the world championship. Training took roughly 16 H100s for a week (~$8,000), against a reported $3–4.5M for DeepMind's DeepNash. The same methods reached superhuman play in Barrage Stratego, Hanabi and Dou dizhu (Nature, MIT News).

The new part is not "AI wins a game". It is that planning under hidden information, which is how negotiation, security and markets actually work, became hundreds of times cheaper and transferable between games.

IMPACT 3/10#11 RESEARCH · NETWORKING · DATED 1 OCT

Ousterhout pushes Homa, a TCP replacement for AI data centres, as his "life's mission"

Stanford professor emeritus John Ousterhout is campaigning for Homa, a data-centre transport protocol. Homa sends whole messages instead of byte streams, lets the receiver control congestion, and runs alongside TCP so applications can move over gradually (The Register). Earlier work reported 10–100x lower tail latency for short messages than TCP (arXiv). A Linux implementation is going through upstream review (LWN).

Tail latency on short RPCs is what agent fan-out and inference serving keep hitting. This is a long-horizon item; the trigger to watch is Homa landing in mainline Linux.
