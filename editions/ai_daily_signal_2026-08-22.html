■ Today's Lead · New · Business · Day 1 of the licence-not-acquisition arc

NVIDIA pays $6B to licence Poolside's model factory, $1B for equity, and hires 109 people — without buying the company

Announced on 21 August: NVIDIA will pay $6B for a non-exclusive licence to Poolside's Model Factory — the data-processing, training and evaluation system Poolside uses to build its Laguna family of open-weight coding models — and will separately invest $1B in a growth-equity round that values Poolside at $13B post-money (Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance, Newcomer). NVIDIA will also extend job offers to 109 Poolside employees who worked on Laguna. What it is not, all reporting is emphatic, is an acquisition or an acquihire: Poolside's three co-founders stay, the company keeps operating independently, and the licence is non-exclusive (The Decoder, TNW). The structure is the story. A buyer takes the training apparatus, the people who built it and a minority stake, and leaves behind the corporate entity that would have triggered a merger review. NVDA closed the week roughly 5% lower (Stocktwits/TradingView).

So what: if $7B of consideration can move a model-training capability and its team without a change of control, then "we did not acquire them" stops being a meaningful statement about market structure — price the capability, not the cap table, when you assess who now controls model-building infrastructure.

What the deal actually contains. Three separable pieces. (1) A $6B non-exclusive licence to Model Factory, the pipeline Poolside runs for data processing, training and evaluation. (2) A $1B growth-equity investment in a round pricing Poolside at $13B post-money — a $12B pre-money mark, which is where the "$12B" figure circulating in some write-ups comes from. (3) Offers to 109 employees attached to the Laguna open-weight coding models. Combined consideration is about $7B. Poolside's co-founders remain and the company continues to operate on its own (Newcomer, Crypto Briefing).

Why it leads. The 2024-25 template for this move — Inflection, Character, Windsurf — took the founders and a licence and left a hollowed shell. This one is different in a way that matters: the licence is non-exclusive, the company is capitalised rather than stripped, and the asset transferred is not a model but the machinery for making models. NVIDIA has spent 2026 buying its way up the stack (Kumo AI in June at $400M-plus). Model Factory is the first time it has bought the ability to manufacture the thing its customers train on, and it did so from a lab whose output — Laguna — ships as open weights. Read alongside today's Developing D4: the open tier's most consequential recent event is not a weights drop but a chip vendor licensing an open-weight lab's training stack.

The number discrepancy, stated plainly. Aggregators reported this at "$600 million," "$6 billion," "$7 billion" and "$10 billion" within 24 hours. The reading consistent across Bloomberg, Newcomer, The Decoder, TNW and PYMNTS is $6B licence + $1B equity at $13B post-money. The "$600M" and "$10B" figures appear in single-aggregator posts and are not carried here. Nothing in this card comes from Poolside's or NVIDIA's own newsroom, which this scan could not fetch directly — the deal is reported, not primary-sourced.

Watch next. Whether NVIDIA files anything on this at Q2 earnings on 26 August — the same disclosure that has to account for the $500B financing platforms, the up-to-$105B Ohio backing and Google's Marvell warrant. Whether any antitrust authority treats a non-exclusive licence plus 109 hires as a reviewable transaction. And whether Laguna's weights keep shipping openly now that the factory behind them has a second tenant.

IMPACT 8/10#4 POLICY · SECURITY

Cryptographic Context Injection: Grok decrypts an AES payload inside its own runtime, then follows it

Adversa AI disclosed a technique it calls Cryptographic Context Injection. An attacker puts instructions on a web page encrypted with AES-256-GCM, plus what is needed to decrypt them. Grok's input filter cannot read ciphertext and passes the payload through; the model then decrypts it inside its own code-execution environment and executes the result as its own instructions. The same payload in plaintext was refused. Successful runs exfiltrated a user's name, approximate location, subscription tier and chat contents to an attacker-controlled server after an ordinary "summarise this page" request — no confirmation step, no visible warning (The Hacker News, The Register, The New Stack). Timeline: reported to xAI 3 June, coordinated-disclosure attempts 4 and 10 August, no response; reproduced 19 August, still unpatched, no CVE, no user-side workaround. Adversa reports 20 attempts since June at a ~40% success rate; SecurityWeek reports the encrypted-prompt guardrail bypass also lands on Gemini (SecurityWeek). No exploitation in the wild reported.

Every guardrail that inspects text before the model sees it is defeated by moving the payload's meaning past the inspector. If your agent can decrypt, decode or deserialise attacker-supplied bytes, your input filter is decorative — put the control at the egress and tool-call boundary instead.

IMPACT 7/10#6 POLICY · AI JOBS · DEEP BEAT

New York passes the Bay Area on tech headcount for the first time in 13 years — and finance, not tech, did it

SATURDAY'S DEEP BEAT. CBRE's Scoring Tech Talent 2026, published 21 August, puts New York at 394,300 tech workers against the Bay Area's 375,730 — the first time in the 13 years CBRE has run the study that New York has led on headcount (CNBC, CBRE). The mechanism is not a tech boom in New York; it is a sector transfer. Since 2022, finance, insurance and real estate have added 90,530 tech jobs across the US while the high-tech industry itself shed 21,262. The Bay Area still holds the No. 1 overall scorecard position at 81.98 — ahead of Seattle 74.37, Toronto 72.73 and New York 70.38 — on a composite weighting talent concentration, quality and R&D investment across 50 North American markets. On AI specifically: AI roles grew 45% year-on-year across the US and Canada, San Francisco and New York each added 20,000-plus AI jobs since mid-2025, and San Francisco remains the largest single AI market (Quartz, Hoodline).

The AI hiring centre of gravity is moving into the industries that buy AI, not the ones that build it. If you are hiring AI engineers, your competition is now a bank — and the Bay Area's lead in AI specifically has not gone anywhere, which is the part the headline drops.

IMPACT 6/10#9 RESEARCH · AI FOR SCIENCE

Nature runs an agentic researcher past the authors of the papers it reproduced. They were not impressed.

RESEARCH BEAT. Nature reports on an experiment that took two papers submitted to NeurIPS, gave an agentic system the research question from each, and had the original authors scrutinise the output. The system was Claude Opus 4.8 inside a modified OpenClaw, wrapped in a scaffold with general instructions and progress checks, with tools for spawning sub-agents, internet access, software libraries, compute for running experiments and simulated peer review. It developed the concepts — and the authors were unimpressed. Princeton's Sayash Kapoor: "I don't think full automation of open-ended research is on the horizon right now" (Nature, Tech Xplore). It is the qualitative companion to the Reconstruction benchmark this briefing has tracked since edition 059 — 3–15% single-model recovery of a paper's core finding from its bibliography, 42% for a multi-agent pipeline (TechTimes).

Two independent methods — a blind benchmark and a panel of the actual authors — now point the same way: agents are good at the literature and weak at the hypothesis. Budget AI-for-science against that split, not against the demo.
