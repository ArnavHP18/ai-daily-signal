AMD's data-centre business doubled — and the market shrugged

On 4 August AMD reported a record quarter: revenue of $11.54B, up 50% year on year, with data-centre revenue up 107% to $6.7B — now 58% of the entire company — on EPYC processors and Instinct accelerators. Non-GAAP EPS came in at $1.66 on net income of $2.8B, and the third quarter is guided to $12.7B–$13.3B, roughly 41% growth (AMD investor relations, CNBC, DCD). Lisa Su said the company enters the second half "with momentum as EPYC demand accelerates" and the Helios rack-scale platform begins to ramp. The stock fell on the print — the miss was against expectation, not against the business — and gaming revenue dropped 31%, with Su conceding prices have "weighed on" consumer demand (Tom's Hardware). Yesterday's edition recorded a genuine gap — no August hardware or compute event had surfaced at all. This is that event, and it arrived two days before the scan found it.

So what: the significance is not the beat, it is the concentration. A company that sold gaming and client silicon for forty years now derives 58% of revenue from AI data centres, guided up again, while its consumer line contracts by nearly a third. That is the second-source confirmation the compute-buildout thesis needed: NVIDIA's ~81% accelerator share was always compatible with "one company's bubble," but a challenger doubling into the same demand is not. If you are modelling accelerator supply for 2027, AMD's Helios ramp is now a real second lane rather than a roadmap slide — and if you are modelling cost, note that the two new denominators today both point the other way: nearly half of US voters polled want new data-centre construction paused, and the capacity being financed ($100B at Paducah) is being sited on land the public already has opinions about.

IMPACT 7/10#2 ENTERPRISE · GOVERNANCE

Anthropic ships inference hooks: an allow/deny checkpoint in front of every Claude Enterprise prompt

Announced 5 August in beta. When an organisation enables inference hooks, every inference request routes over a signed WebSocket to that organisation's own security server for an allow-or-deny verdict before the model sees the prompt — and the same check runs on tool-call responses, so what an MCP server, skill or plugin returns is inspected before it reaches the model. Coverage spans Claude Enterprise chat, Claude Code and Cowork in one enforcement layer rather than per-product integrations (Anthropic, platform docs). The webhook protocol has a published schema and is designed to plug into existing DLP from Netskope, Palo Alto Networks, Proofpoint and Zscaler, or a custom server; shadow mode, role-based exclusions and percentage rollouts allow phasing (The Next Web, Unite.AI). Limits are explicit: verdicts are binary — allow or deny, no rewrite or redact — and the only hook event at launch fires pre-inference; response-side enforcement is planned, not shipped.

This is the tool-call inspection point the agent-security arc has been asking for all month — the same surface the TrapDoor campaign and the npm worm abuse. Run it in shadow mode first: a binary deny on a false positive breaks a developer's session with no redaction fallback.

IMPACT 7/10#3 HARDWARE · SITING

$100B and 1.2GW on a Cold War uranium site: Brookfield and NextEra take the DOE's Paducah plant

Announced 29 July: Brookfield and NextEra Energy plan to convert part of the Department of Energy's former Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant in western Kentucky — built in the 1950s to enrich uranium for weapons, closed in 2013 — into one of the largest AI data-centre campuses in the US, targeting more than 1.2GW of compute with an investment that "could total roughly $100 billion over time" (Data Center Knowledge, Bloomberg). The power plan is the story: up to 2GW of dedicated natural gas generation and up to 2.6GW of battery storage built adjacent by NextEra, with up to 1.8GW of utility-delivered capacity by 2032 alongside Big Rivers Electric, Jackson Purchase Energy Cooperative and Paducah Power System (Department of Energy). It remains subject to definitive agreements and regulatory approval.

Read this next to the FERC large-load docket (17 August) and the voter poll below: hyperscale siting is migrating to federal brownfield land with self-supplied generation precisely because the interconnection queue and the local politics are the binding constraints.

IMPACT 7/10#4 POLICY · SOCIETY

The consent problem: nearly half of US voters polled back a data-centre construction pause

In a survey of 6,872 registered US voters by Milltown Partners, reported by TIME, nearly half supported a temporary ban on new data-centre construction against only 16% opposed — with the buildings functioning, in TIME's framing, as a stand-in for a broader sense that AI is being built around people rather than with them. The surrounding sentiment data points the same way: 40% expect AI to have a negative effect on society over the next twenty years against 16% expecting a positive one; 63% say AI is advancing too quickly; 71% say wider AI use will make their personal information less secure; and 67% have little or no confidence in the US government to regulate it (SmarterX). On the labour side, 71% of professionals expect AI to eliminate more jobs than it creates over three years, against 13% expecting net creation. The campus-level version showed up in May, when pro-AI commencement speeches drew sustained booing at Arizona, Central Florida and Middle Tennessee State (AI CERTs).

The compute buildout has been modelled as a capital and supply-chain problem. This is the first number in this briefing suggesting the binding constraint in 2027 could be a zoning board. If you are siting capacity, budget for a consent campaign the way you budget for interconnection.

IMPACT 7/10#5 MODELS · OPEN-WEIGHT

Poolside's Laguna S 2.1: 118B open weights that top SWE-Bench Multilingual and fit one DGX Spark

Released 21 July and missed by this briefing's earlier scans. Laguna S 2.1 is a 118B-parameter mixture-of-experts with 8B active per token, built for agentic coding, with up to 1M tokens of context. On SWE-Bench Multilingual it scores 78.5%, topping the published table outright, and on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-Bench Pro it matches or exceeds models several times its size — DeepSeek-V4-Flash, NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra and Thinking Machines' Inkling among them (Poolside, via GlobeNewswire, MarkTechPost). Weights ship on Hugging Face under an OpenMDW-1.1 licence with BF16 plus FP8, INT4, NVFP4, GGUF and MLX quantisations, small enough to run on a single NVIDIA DGX Spark. Pre-training began 22 May 2026 on 4,096 NVIDIA H200s (The Decoder).

The open coding frontier this briefing has been tracking as a Chinese story now has a Western entrant with published weights and a real licence. If you deferred an open-weight fallback because self-hosting a 700B model was unaffordable, 118B/8B on one Spark changes that arithmetic.

IMPACT 6/10#7 MODELS · QUANTISATION

Bonsai 27B puts a 27B-class model in 3.9GB — and claims 90–95% of full precision

Released 14 July under Apache 2.0 by PrismML: Bonsai 27B is an extreme quantisation of Alibaba's Qwen3.6-27B using a 1.58-bit ternary representation — each weight is −1, 0 or 1. Two builds ship: Ternary at 1.71 effective bits/weight and 5.9GB (laptop-class) and 1-bit at 1.125 bits/weight and 3.9GB (phone-class), both reported to retain 90–95% of the full-precision baseline across 15 benchmarks spanning maths, coding, agentic tool use and vision. Quantisation runs end-to-end across embeddings, attention, MLPs and the LM head; the vision tower is handled separately at 4-bit. GGUF weights are on Hugging Face and the model is served on Together AI (MarkTechPost).

If the 90–95% retention holds under your own evals, on-device inference stops being a demo. Apache 2.0 means you can quantise further and ship it inside a product without licence friction.

IMPACT 5/10#10 RESEARCH · BENCHMARKS

WorldExam asks whether a video model knows how the world reacts, not just how it looks

Thursday's research leg. arXiv:2608.02603, submitted 3 August 2026 by sixteen authors, proposes WorldExam: a hierarchical diagnostic benchmark for controllable video-generation models used as world models. It grades across four ascending levels — Visual Quality, Control Adherence, Spatial Consistency and World Reactivity — with 1,474 cases across eight dedicated tasks, and supports unified evaluation of camera-, action- and language-driven paradigms in one harness. The top level is the interesting one: reactivity tests whether a model can infer how the world should respond and generate consequences that were never described in the input (project page).

Video-generation quality has been scored on appearance for three years. Separating "looks right" from "behaves right" is the measurement that world-model claims have been missing — borrow the four-level split for your own eval harness even if you never run the benchmark.

IMPACT 9/10D1 ENTERPRISE · SUPPLY CHAIN

The keyv worm gets a name, a lineage and a blockchain for command-and-control

WHAT CHANGED Attribution and mechanism, both new since yesterday. The campaign is attributed to the TeamPCP threat group and identified as a descendant of the "Mini" Shai-Hulud malware family — which settles yesterday's contested naming. More consequentially, the worm resolves its command-and-control domains dynamically from an Ethereum smart contract, so seizing or sinkholing domains does not sever control. Yesterday's package spread also firms upward: twelve organisations, not nine.
The scale numbers are now specific. keyv alone carries roughly 127 million npm downloads a week; the same maintainer's flat-cache (565M/month), file-entry-cache (557M/month) and cacheable (29M/month) were swept into the same compromise (Wiz, SafeDep). The first poisoned publish, [email protected], went out at 09:35 UTC on 4 August carrying a credential stealer, the worm and a persistence mechanism designed to detonate during incident response. Self-propagation extended the blast radius to 868+ additional packages across 1,381 versions and more than 2 billion monthly installs, with named downstream organisations including Deliveroo, Picsart, Qlik, Ornikar and HubSync (Datadog Security Labs, Upwind). The malware harvests cloud credentials, GitHub tokens, SSH keys and CI/CD secrets from workstations and build environments (Orca).

The blockchain C2 is the durable lesson: the standard response playbook — identify the domain, get it taken down — is structurally defeated by a contract that cannot be seized. Combine that with yesterday's finding that rotating the stolen token triggers the handler, and the remediation order stands: isolate the host, strip the agent-config persistence, then rotate. Today's inference-hooks release (New #2) is the same problem approached from the vendor side.
