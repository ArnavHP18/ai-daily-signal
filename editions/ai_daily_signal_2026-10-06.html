The White House gives superintelligence its own task force, and puts the spy chief in charge

On Sunday 4 October President Trump created a Super Intelligence Force chaired by DNI Jay Clayton, with FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Emil Michael (Under Secretary of War for Research and Engineering) and OPM's Scott Kupor as vice chairs. It reports to Trump and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and owes a report on AI's risks and opportunities within 120 days (ABC News · CBS News · TechCrunch). A day later, the four biggest US labs testified under oath to the New York City Council (New #2).

So what: superintelligence is now a named federal portfolio with an intelligence chief at the top and the FTC's chair inside it, while the FTC investigates the same labs. The 120-day report, due around 1 February, is the first federal document likely to say what the government thinks it should do about superintelligence, beyond voluntary pledges.

IMPACT 7/10#1 POLICY · US FEDERAL · DATED SUN 4 OCT

Trump creates a 'Super Intelligence Force' led by DNI Jay Clayton, with the FTC chair as a vice chair and a 120-day deadline to report on AI's risks and opportunities

On Sunday 4 October President Trump announced a federal Super Intelligence Force (SIF) to coordinate the government's work on keeping the US ahead in "Super Intelligence". It is chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton; the vice chairs are FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Under Secretary of War for Research and Engineering Emil Michael and OPM Director Scott Kupor. It reports to the President and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, will lead engagement with consumers, public-interest and religious groups, critical-infrastructure providers and tech companies, and must deliver a report on AI's risks and opportunities within 120 days (ABC News · CBS News · The Hill · TechCrunch · Fox News). Edition 107 saw the name in one aggregator and did not card it; it is confirmed by five outlets on re-search.

The FTC chair sitting on the force matters most: the FTC's AI-safety probe of Anthropic, OpenAI and METR (Still Tracking) now runs beside a White House body he helps lead. Putting the intelligence chief in charge frames superintelligence as a national-security file, not a consumer one. Expect the 120-day report (due about 1 February) to shape whether federal policy moves past the voluntary White House accord.

IMPACT 6/10#2 POLICY · US CITY · HEARING MON 5 OCT

Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta testify under oath to the New York City Council as former lab researchers warn of losing control

At a rare Committee of the Whole hearing on Monday 5 October, the New York City Council took sworn testimony on AI risks. The companies sent policy and safety staff, all appearing remotely: Logan Graham, head of Anthropic's Frontier Red Team; OpenAI's Morgan Dwyer, head of policy development and operations; Meta's Shane Cahill; and Google's Alice Friend. Former researchers Jacob Coxon (OpenAI, Anthropic), Alex Turner (Google DeepMind) and Daniel Kokotajlo (AI Futures Project) also testified. Coxon called the industry's approach "extremely reckless": "we are racing to build and grow our own adversary here at home, which is misaligned AI" (CNBC · Bloomberg · Quartz · CBS New York · Fortune). amNY's account is that the companies gave few clear answers to the key safety questions (amNY). Speaker Menin had subpoenaed SpaceXAI on 28 September to compel testimony (NYC Council (SpaceXAI subpoena, 28 Sep)); whether it appeared was not clear in the coverage found.

A city council has no power over frontier labs, but sworn testimony creates a record that state attorneys general and the FTC can quote. The labs sent policy and red-team staff, not CEOs. Watch whether the Council drafts a local bill on AI used by city agencies or contractors, the one lever it actually has.

IMPACT 5/10#3 MODELS · DECISION MODELS · EMERGING TECH · DATED 24 SEP · HF TRENDING 5 OCT

Fastino's GLiNER2.5-Decide: a 340M open-weight encoder decision model that runs on CPUs and claims to beat a Jev model on its own routing suite

Fastino Labs released GLiNER2.5-Decide on 24 September: a 340M-parameter, Apache 2.0 decision model built on a DeBERTa-v3-large encoder. It does not generate tokens. Given text and a set of typed questions and rules, an encoder scores the allowed answers and a constrained decoder enforces the declared rules, returning answers with probabilities, confidence scores and feasibility metadata. Fastino reports 60.1% average accuracy on its own 17-dataset "Fast Decisions" suite against 57.5% for JevK5, and 167 ms on a 48-core CPU; it runs air-gapped and supports full or LoRA fine-tuning (Fastino blog (primary) · MarkTechPost · DataNorth). It is on Hugging Face's trending list on 5 October with about 53,600 downloads (Hugging Face trending, 5 Oct). Missed for 12 days: the Jev watchlist query did not include Fastino or GLiNER; it is carded today with its real date.

Decision models no longer need a GPU or a decoder LLM: an encoder of the BERT family, small enough for a CPU, now competes with Jev on routing and guardrails. For teams with on-premise or air-gapped rules, this is the cheapest decision model to test. The head-to-head with Jev is Fastino's own benchmark; treat it as a claim.

IMPACT 5/10#4 MODELS · DECISION MODELS · EMERGING TECH · DATED 23 SEP · HF TRENDING 5 OCT

CLM-v0.1-8B: an open contrastive model that makes decisions by matching a state against candidate actions, built as two small heads on a frozen Qwen3-8B

Contrastive-LM released CLM-v0.1-8B on 23 September under Apache 2.0. Rather than generating an answer, it embeds the current state and each candidate action with two trained projection heads (~20M parameters each) on a frozen Qwen3-8B encoder, and returns a choice, scores or a ranking with relative probabilities. Because state and action embeddings are computed separately, repeated candidates can be cached, which suits agent loops. The project says it trained on about 60 million question-answer pairs, 30 million synthetic hard negatives and one million agentic trajectories (Contrastive-LM on Hugging Face (primary) · Contrastive-LM/CLM (GitHub) · Gradually.ai · Wavect). It is on Hugging Face's trending list on 5 October with about 715 likes (Hugging Face trending, 5 Oct). Missed for 13 days for the same reason as GLiNER2.5-Decide.

This is a third design for decision models, after Jev's single-pass classifier and Fastino's encoder: treat a decision as a lookup between a state and its options. Cached action embeddings make it cheap to re-score the same tools many times, which is exactly what agent routers do. It is the one to try as a verifier or reranker beside a generating model.

IMPACT 5/10#5 MODELS · OPEN WEIGHTS · EUROPE · TUESDAY DEEP BEAT · DATED 3 OCT

Aleph Alpha releases Kolibri-1: a 78B-parameter English-German reasoning model with 3.46B active parameters, a 1M-token context and Apache 2.0 weights

Heidelberg-based Aleph Alpha released Kolibri-1 on 3 October: a mixture-of-experts reasoning model with 78.1B total and 3.46B active parameters per token, a 1,048,576-token context window, FP8 weights on Hugging Face and an Apache 2.0 licence. It was trained from scratch on 20T tokens (about 62.5% English, 23.9% German, 13.6% code) on infrastructure in Germany and Finland, supports four reasoning levels (none to high) and tool calling, and has a June 2026 knowledge cutoff. The minimum stated hardware is a single H200, B200 or B300, or two A100/H100 80GB (Aleph Alpha blog (primary) · Kolibri-1 model card · El Solitario · innFactory). No independent benchmark results were found yet.

Europe's 'sovereign' model pitch now comes with open weights, not just a hosted API, and with very low active-parameter cost for its size. It is the first big release since Aleph Alpha agreed its combination with Cohere (Still Tracking). For German-language public-sector and regulated buyers, it is a self-hostable option to test against Qwen and Mistral before any procurement.

IMPACT 4/10#6 BUSINESS · WORLD MODELS FOR PHYSICS · $10M SEED · DATED 30 SEP

Zenithon's world models for extreme physics, for fusion reactors, rockets and chip manufacturing, get $10M

UK start-up Zenithon is building world models for extreme physics: learned models of how complex physical systems behave that predict the outcome of a design far faster than a conventional simulator, targeting fusion reactors, rockets and semiconductor manufacturing. It claims its models can explore a million design points in the time a conventional simulator handles one, while learning from real experiments, and plans a new model every three months. The $10M round (30 September) is backed by BACKED, Lunar, Seraphim, MMC and SOSV; co-founders are Alex Higginbottom and Abetharan Antony, and advisers include Dan Brunner, co-founder and former CTO of Commonwealth Fusion Systems (Tech.eu · AI Weekly · Startup Researcher). The speed-up is the company's own claim.

'World model' usually means video and robots; this is the same idea aimed at engineering simulation, where a 1,000,000x speed-up would change how many designs a fusion or chip team can try. Scored on the technology, not the round: a new model class with independent coverage. Watch for a first published comparison against a standard solver.

IMPACT 4/10#7 ENTERPRISE · PAYMENTS · AGENTIC COMMERCE · DATED 30 SEP

Mastercard starts testing a score for how likely a card payment was made by an AI agent, the first of new Agent Pay trust services

On 30 September Mastercard expanded Agent Pay, its agentic-payments programme, with trust and intelligence services that combine identity, intent, behaviour and fraud signals so banks and merchants can understand AI-initiated transactions. The first, now testing in the US, gives issuers a probability score that a transaction was initiated by an AI agent, so they can approve legitimate agent purchases with less friction. The services form the intelligence layer of Mastercard's Agent Pay Trust Framework, and Mastercard is working with Cloudflare and Skyfire on agent visibility (Mastercard (primary) · The Paypers).

Card networks are building the plumbing that the bank warnings about agentic commerce (Still Tracking) asked for: a way to tell an agent's purchase from fraud. Merchants testing agent checkout in Gemini, Muse or ChatGPT will meet this score at authorisation time.

IMPACT 3/10#10 RESEARCH · OPTIMISERS · ARXIV 2 OCT LISTINGS

Research beat: TACO fine-tunes 30B-class models on one 80GB H100, and SoftServe makes quasi-Newton optimisers scale to deep learning

Two optimiser papers from the arXiv listings of 2 October (flagged in a daily digest, then read in their abstracts). TACO (arXiv 2610.02199 (TACO)) keeps first-order gradients but makes the optimiser's stored state almost free: on OPT-13B it cuts persistent optimiser state 174x against AdamW8bit (27.7 GB to 0.16 GB) and peak training memory 2.9x (80.6 GB to 27.5 GB), enough for full-parameter fine-tuning of 30–32B models on a single 80GB H100. SoftServe (arXiv 2610.02182 (SoftServe)) is a family of quasi-Newton optimisers that keep curvature estimates positive-definite and use GPU-friendly Newton-Schulz iterations instead of matrix decompositions; the authors report lower losses than Adam, Muon and SOAP on badly conditioned problems, including a 136M-parameter physics-informed diffusion model (TACO abstract (deeplearn.org) · SoftServe abstract (deeplearn.org) · arXiv digest, 3 Oct). Both are preprints with author-reported results.

TACO is the practical one: if it holds, full fine-tuning of a 30B open model moves from a multi-GPU node to one card, which matters for anyone adapting Qwen- or Kolibri-class models in-house. SoftServe matters for scientific ML, where badly conditioned losses are common.

IMPACT 6/10D1 POLICY · LAB SAFETY · PEOPLE · DATED 3 OCT

OpenAI's safety exits, confirmed: David Robinson, who led safety reports on 12 frontier launches, quits with an Atlantic essay saying the company's 'culture is broken'

WHAT CHANGED Edition 106 demoted this as single-outlet. Robinson has now published his own account in The Atlantic (3 Oct), covered by TechCrunch and Calcalist, and the three researchers dismissed on 1 Oct have been named in later coverage.
David Robinson, among OpenAI's longest-tenured employees at three and a half years, oversaw the safety reports for 12 OpenAI frontier launches and was a key architect of its Preparedness Framework. In an essay in The Atlantic titled "I Quit OpenAI Because Its Culture Is Broken", he argues AI companies are not being "nearly careful enough", that the failures are cultural so new rules alone will not fix them, and that OpenAI's trial-and-error 'iterative deployment' must end: "The time for trial and error is over" (The Atlantic (Robinson essay) · TechCrunch · Calcalist). The essay drew 456 points on Hacker News (Hacker News AI digest, 5 Oct). Separately, the three safety researchers OpenAI dismissed on 1 October for allegedly sharing confidential material with an outside safety group have been named as Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni (eSecurity Planet · Decrypt).

The person who wrote OpenAI's system cards is now a public critic, in the same week the FTC is asking labs for safety records and a White House force with the FTC chair on it was created. OpenAI's next model launch, whenever its training pause ends, will be read against his essay.
