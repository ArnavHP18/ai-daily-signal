Alibaba ships the first Max-class open weights — eleven days late, one model short

The thread this briefing has carried since 4 August closed on 13 August: Qwen3.8-Max's open weights went live as Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B on Hugging Face and ModelScope, with an FP8 quantisation alongside it — a 2.4-trillion-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts model with roughly 95B active per token, and the first Max-tier Qwen release ever made downloadable (OrcaRouter, byteiota, Neomanex). Two things came off in the shipping. The checkpoint is text-only and drops the 1M-token context the priced API version carries, and it lands under a new revenue-share licence rather than Apache 2.0. And the second half of the promise did not arrive: Qwen3.8-27B — the small model pitched as the realistic local and on-premise path — still has no repository, no model card and no licence file, with the ModelScope countdown now naming 15 August, 00:00 JST (OrcaRouter countdown). It shipped into the same 24 hours as DeepSeek's V4 Pro going GA (New #1).

So what: a Max-class model you can actually download changes what "open ceiling" means — but read the licence before the benchmark. A revenue-share term and a dropped context window are the two places where an open-weight release stops being a drop-in substitute for the API you were paying for.

What shipped. Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B is downloadable from Hugging Face and ModelScope as of 13 August, with an FP8 quantised variant published alongside. Alibaba previewed Qwen3.8-Max on 19 July, launched the priced API on 3 August, and committed the same week to publishing open weights for both Max and a smaller Qwen3.8-27B "within about a week" — a window this briefing put on the deadline radar as closing 16 August.

What did not ship, and what came off. The 27B is absent: no repository, no model card, no licence file and no benchmark of its own. The Max checkpoint that did land is text-only and does not carry the 1M-token context advertised on the API model, and it is governed by a revenue-share licence rather than a standard permissive one. None of those three caveats was in the original commitment as reported.

Why it is the lead and not New #1. Under this briefing's rule the hero is the biggest new item unless a carried thread has a decisive event. This is a decisive event: eleven days of "announced, not shipped" ended with an actual artefact, and it is a genuine first — no Max-tier Qwen has ever been downloadable. DeepSeek's V4 Pro GA is the bigger raw story and takes New #1; the two together are the same fact seen twice, which is that in one 24-hour window two Chinese labs put frontier-tier weights and frontier-tier pricing on the table.

The caveats. Every source here is a secondary tracker or explainer site. Hugging Face, ModelScope and Alibaba's own Qwen blog were not read directly in this scan, so the parameter split (2.4T total / ~95B active), the FP8 variant, the licence type and the dropped context window are all reported rather than verified against the model card. Treat the licence characterisation in particular as unconfirmed until you read the file yourself — it is the term most likely to be mis-summarised by a tracker, and it is the term that decides whether you can use this commercially.

Watch next. Whether the 27B lands on the newly named 15 August ModelScope date or slips again; whether the licence text is a true revenue-share or a size-gated commercial clause; whether independent benchmark runs reproduce the API model's numbers on a text-only, short-context checkpoint; and whether the open ceiling this briefing tracks — currently Kimi K3's 2.8T weights of 16 July — is now effectively contested by a model with a live commercial API behind it.

IMPACT 8/10#1 MODELS · DEEPSEEK

DeepSeek's V4 Pro leaves preview in silence — 1.6T MoE at $0.87 per million output tokens

DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813 became the general-availability build behind the deepseek-v4-pro endpoint, appearing on OpenRouter on 12 August and in DeepSeek's own API documentation on 13 August (OpenRouter, Unite.AI). There was no blog post, no changelog and no press release — only a brief note on DeepSeek's website calling out "significantly enhanced agent capabilities", which was removed by the following afternoon. The model is a mixture-of-experts at 1.6T total parameters, 49B active per token, a 1,048,576-token context and up to 384,000 output tokens, priced at $0.43 / $0.87 per million in/out (Artificial Analysis, Wccftech). The claimed jumps against the preview are large: DeepSWE 12.8 → 62.7, CyberGym 52.7 → 83.3, Terminal-Bench 2.1 72.1 → 87.9. Coverage splits on whether it is a win: SCMP frames it as struggling on general benchmarks while excelling at cybersecurity (SCMP), and one outlet reports a coming API price rise with no date, figure or affected tier named. Every headline number is DeepSeek's own; independent replication has not landed (TechTimes).

A frontier-tier agentic model priced an order of magnitude under the Western majors, shipped without an announcement, is a pricing event before it is a capability event. Re-run your own eval suite before you believe the deltas — and note that a CyberGym leap to 83.3 is a security-posture question, not just a leaderboard one.

Vantage explores a ~$100B listing — the largest data-centre IPO ever, if it happens

Reuters reported on 13 August that Vantage Data Centers — backed by Silver Lake and DigitalBridge — is exploring an IPO at roughly $100B or a sale, potentially raising around $10B, as soon as next year (Reuters via Yahoo, BNN Bloomberg, Quartz). Three data-centre operators queueing for public markets at once is the buildout looking for an exit, not just for capacity. If it clears, the cost of AI capacity stops being set by private infrastructure funds and starts being set quarterly, in public, by people who can sell.

IMPACT 6/10#8 ENTERPRISE · ADOPTION DATA

Today's deep beat: the first real telemetry on how many agents a company actually runs — 5 to 13 in 14 months

Salesforce published its Agentic Enterprise Index on 10 August, built from aggregated Agentforce usage rather than a survey — which is what makes it worth carding (Salesforce, Enterprise Times). Across businesses running agents continuously from February 2025 to April 2026: the average number of activated agents went from 5 to 13; time-to-first-agent fell 53%, with agents created within about two days of provisioning; average skills per agent rose 2 → 6, reaching 9 in retail at peak; employee agent usage tripled while customer escalation rates held steady at scale; and retailers using agents saw 4x higher online sales growth (BusinessWorld). One honest wrinkle: Salesforce's own headline says agent deployments "more than double", while 5→13 is 2.6x and outlets ran it as "nearly tripled" — same data, two framings.

Read this against the enterprise-agent pilot-gap thread already in Tracking. Both can be true: agents per customer are compounding inside firms that got one working, while the population that never got one working is not in this dataset at all. It is vendor telemetry on survivors, not a market-wide adoption rate.

IMPACT 6/10#9 RESEARCH · AI FOR SCIENCE

An extra day of cyclone warning, in Nature — and the weights are on GitHub under Apache 2.0

Today's research beat, dated honestly: a Nature paper published 6 August reports that Google DeepMind's WeatherNext Cyclones forecasts tropical-cyclone track, intensity and wind structure at least a day earlier than leading operational systems on average, evaluated on storms from 2023–2025 — a gain the team compares to more than a decade of conventional progress, with three-day forecasts matching what prior models delivered at two days (Google DeepMind, the decoder). It was trained on ~20TB of global atmospheric data plus NOAA's IBTrACS archive — about 5,000 cyclones over 45 years. The US National Hurricane Center used it operationally during the 2025 Atlantic season, including on Hurricane Melissa's rapid intensification and Jamaica landfall (TechTimes). Code and weights are open-sourced under Apache 2.0 across three variants — Cyclones, WeatherNext 2 and a 2-mini that runs in a free Colab notebook (GitHub).

This is the cleanest counter-example to "AI-for-science is demos": a peer-reviewed lead-time gain, validated by the agency that issues the warnings, with the weights released. The Apache licence is the part to notice — a national met office in a low-income country can run this on Monday.
