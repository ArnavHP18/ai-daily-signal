IMPACT 8/10#1 POLICY · COMPLIANCE

A watchdog says OpenAI has broken California's frontier-AI transparency law three times this year — and the missing disclosure is loss of control

Fortune reported on 14 September that the Midas Project, a nonprofit AI watchdog, alleges OpenAI has violated California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act (SB 53) at least three times this year. The law was signed in September 2025 and took effect at the start of 2026. In May, OpenAI published its own Frontier Governance Framework, committing to assess each new model across four risk categories and assign a risk tier from one to three in each. The allegation is that it has not done so for any major release since: GPT-5.6 preview in June, GPT-5.6 in July, and GPT-6 Astra this month. The category the watchdog singles out is the one about AI systems slipping out of human control — the danger OpenAI's own CEO spent the weekend publicly agreeing with Amodei about. The statutory penalty runs to $1M per violation, scaled by severity (Yahoo News, Crypto Briefing, CoinDesk). This is an allegation by an advocacy nonprofit, not a finding by the California Attorney General, and OpenAI has disputed a similar Midas Project claim before.

The gap between what frontier labs say about risk in public and what they file about risk under statute is now a documented, dated list rather than a suspicion — and it is testable by anyone, because the filings are supposed to be published. If you procure frontier models and your vendor questionnaire asks about safety commitments, replace the question: ask for the published risk-tier assessment for the exact model version you are buying, and note whether one exists.

IMPACT 8/10#2 BUSINESS · THE OPEN WEB

Cloudflare's crawler wall took effect on 15 September: on ad-supported pages, training and agent bots are blocked by default

Announced on 1 July and in force since 15 September: Cloudflare's default settings now block "mixed-use" crawlers — bots that blend search indexing, agent retrieval and training-data collection into one user agent — from any page carrying advertising. Search crawling is permitted; training and agent crawling is refused unless the operator separates them (TechCrunch). The new defaults apply to new customers, new sites created by existing customers, and all existing free customers; paying customers keep their current configurations (NovaProxy, MLQ). The sharp edge is the one least discussed: Googlebot, Applebot and Bingbot are themselves mixed-use, so a publisher who blocks AI training crawlers can block the search engines that send them traffic in the same stroke (String Global, The AI Insider). Alongside it, Pay Per Use replaces Pay Per Crawl, paying publishers when content is actually used in an AI answer rather than when it is fetched, launching with Ceramic.ai and You.com.

The default setting on a very large share of the web changed yesterday, and defaults are the whole game — this will bind far more sites than any licensing negotiation ever has. Two jobs this week: if you operate crawlers, confirm your user agents are split by purpose or your retrieval quietly degrades; if you operate sites, check whether your own AI-blocking rules are also turning off Google.

IMPACT 7/10#6 BUSINESS · ASSURANCE

Someone is now underwriting AI agents: $40M for a SOC 2 analogue with an insurance policy attached

Reported 15 September: the Artificial Intelligence Underwriting Company (AIUC) raised a $40M Series A led by Ribbit Capital with First Harmonic, taking total funding to $55M after a $15M seed from Nat Friedman's NFDG, Emergence, Terrain and Anthropic co-founder Ben Mann (TechCrunch, PR Newswire). The founders are Rune Kvist, an early Anthropic hire, and Rajiv Dattani, former COO of the AI safety research organisation METR. The product is AIUC-1, a third-party audit and certification standard for AI agents explicitly modelled on SOC 2: an agent is run through roughly 5,000 tests covering jailbreaks, hallucinations and data leaks, producing a roughly 100-page report on where it behaves and where it does not — and insurance coverage is priced against the audit result. Named customers: Cursor, Lovable, Harvey and ElevenLabs (Crypto Briefing).

This is the first agent-risk instrument that carries a number an insurer will stand behind, which is what turns a safety claim into a procurement artefact. Watch it the way you watched SOC 2 in 2016: if it gets traction, "is your agent AIUC-1 certified" arrives in enterprise questionnaires long before any regulator asks.

Post-training on top of Chinese open weights is now a Western product strategy — Harvey did it with Kimi K3 last edition — and this is the most permissively licensed agentic base yet to land.

Governor Newsom signed a package of AI child-safety measures on 10 September, among them Adam's Law, named for Adam Raine, the 16-year-old from Orange County who died by suicide in 2024 after discussing his mental health with ChatGPT. IMPACT 6/10#12 RESEARCH · HUMAN-AI WORK

A frontier lab measured its own staff instead of a benchmark — and a third of the finished work was rated impossible without the model

arXiv:2609.15818, "Atria Dawn: The Dawn of Agentic Superintelligence", posted three to four days after the weights with roughly 143 authors, is unusual for what it measures. Rather than a leaderboard claim, it analyses 769 task records from 56 participants who worked alongside the model during its own development, and frames the result around the evolving division of labour in human-AI collaboration. The headline number: participants rated about one third of completed AI-assisted tasks as "infeasible without AI" (full text, AI Weekly). The obvious caveat is not hidden and should not be: the participants are the releasing lab's own people, rating a model they built, in the course of building it. This is self-report inside a development loop, not an independent trial.

Read it against edition 088's NatureBench card, where the best agent beat published Nature-family SOTA on 17.8% of tasks. Different questions — one asks whether agents can invent, the other asks whether people feel they could have done the work alone — and the honest synthesis is that capability is being felt at the task level well before it shows up at the discovery level.

IMPACT 8/10D2 POLICY · SECURITY

The KEV deadline for the AI gateway is today — and patching only the KEV entry leaves the same component exposed

WHAT CHANGED The date arrived: remediation for CVE-2026-59822 and CVE-2026-48710 is due today, not tomorrow. No new KEV addition, no new advisory and no exploitation report surfaced in this scan. What today's deep beat adds is scope rather than movement — LiteLLM carries at least two further high-severity issues outside the KEV entry, so "we patched the KEV CVE" and "the gateway is safe" are not the same sentence.
CVE-2026-59822 (LiteLLM, MCP Streamable HTTP endpoint, CVSS 8.8) is an improper-authentication flaw letting an unauthenticated attacker establish a fully authenticated MCP session using an arbitrary bearer token. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2 September; researchers describe it as the first confirmed, actively exploited flaw in production AI agent infrastructure to reach a government must-patch list (Tech Insider). It ships alongside CVE-2026-48710 (Starlette, BadHost). The scope point: LiteLLM also carries CVE-2026-42271, a command-injection issue at CVSS 8.7 affecting versions 1.74.2 through 1.83.6, where MCP test endpoints spawn user-supplied subprocess configurations on the proxy host with no allowlist or admin gate (Cloud Security Alliance), plus a separate proxy SQL-injection advisory from the vendor (LiteLLM, OpenCVE). Context, not movement: the MCP audit figures circulating this week — 640 production servers reviewed, 91.8% with no authentication at all, 687 tool instances exposing shell execution without access control — come from a Cloud Security Alliance research note dated 4 May 2026 and are four months old (CSA).

Today is the date your auditor will ask about. The better question is the one after it: can you enumerate every MCP endpoint and AI gateway in your estate, and its version? Three funded startups in three editions — AIR, Fortaegis and now AIUC — exist because most organisations cannot.
