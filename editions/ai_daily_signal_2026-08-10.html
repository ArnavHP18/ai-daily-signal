Hassabis gives up the Google DeepMind CEO title — and yesterday's unresolved claim resolves the other way

This briefing declined to card a Google reorganisation yesterday because August-dated coverage could not be told apart from the April 2024 Research-into-DeepMind consolidation. That call now settles, and it settles against the consolidation reading: the August event is a succession at the top. On 5 August, Demis Hassabis gave up the Google DeepMind CEO title to become Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet, shifting to AGI strategy and scientific applications; Koray Kavukcuoglu — DeepMind's CTO and Alphabet's chief AI architect — takes day-to-day operations as SVP, reporting to Sundar Pichai rather than holding a stand-alone CEO title, and owns Gemini model development, frontier research and the Gemini app and developer teams (Bloomberg, Fortune, TIME). So what: the person who owns your Gemini roadmap changed, and the reporting line moved up to Pichai — that is a product-cadence signal, not a personnel footnote. If Gemini is a single point of failure in your stack, this is the quarter to price a second model in, and to stop treating the missing flagship as a scheduling slip.

IMPACT 7/10#5 RESEARCH · AI FOR SCIENCE

Monday's research beat: AI makes individual scientists 3× more prolific and science 4.6% narrower

Date honesty first: this was published in Nature on 14 January 2026 and is seven months old. It has never been carried here, the research beat produced no dated August entry, and the finding is the most load-bearing thing this scan surfaced on AI-for-science — so it is carded as new to these pages and explicitly not badged NEW.24H. Hao and colleagues trained a language model to identify AI-augmented work across 41.3 million English-language natural-science papers published 1980–2025 in biology, chemistry, physics, medicine, materials science and geology, comparing roughly 311,000 AI-using papers against the rest. Adopters publish 3.02× more papers, receive 4.84× more citations, and become project leaders 1.37 years earlier. Collectively, though, AI adoption shrinks the volume of topics studied by 4.63% and cuts engagement between scientists by 22% — the tools automate crowded established fields rather than open new ones (Nature, Science/AAAS, IEEE Spectrum).

Every AI-for-science pitch this briefing has carried — Robin, the automated-research systems, Discovery Loop as of today — is measured on individual throughput. This is the first large-N evidence that the throughput gain and a contraction in what gets studied are the same effect. If you fund research, the metric to add is topic dispersion, not output.

IMPACT 6/10#8 POLICY · CHINA

China's companion-AI rules start biting: 12 fines, 4.2M RMB in the first weeks

The Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic Interactive Services — co-issued 10 April by the CAC with the NDRC, MIIT, Ministry of Public Security and SAMR — took effect 15 July, covering services that simulate personality and provide ongoing emotional interaction, with content requirements, safety assessments, anti-addiction measures and heightened protection for minors and elderly users (Latham & Watkins, Just Security, IAPP). AGG-SOURCED FIGURE Two regulatory trackers now report that in the first three weeks of enforcement the CAC issued 12 fines totalling 4.2M RMB (Cubbbix tracker, Ethicore) — no CAC enforcement notice was reachable to confirm it. Ceilings are modest: CNY100,000 for simple violations, CNY200,000 where harm to life, health or safety follows. Shanghai's CAC had already stripped 14,000+ non-compliant AI agents in a 26 June pre-deadline sweep, and Doubao and Qwen shut companion features on the effective date (Caixin).

The fines are small enough to be a message rather than a deterrent, but this is the world's first enforcement record for a behaviour-and-dependency rule rather than a content rule — the template other regulators will copy if it holds. Treat the 12/4.2M figure as tracker-sourced until a CAC notice surfaces.
