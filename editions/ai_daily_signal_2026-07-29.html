AMD stops buying compute and starts renting the building it sits in

AMD has taken more than 529 megawatts of US data-centre capacity from Core Scientific under a set of 15-year leases that Core Scientific says could generate more than $14 billion in base contracted revenue, with an option for AMD to reserve up to a further 1,925MW by 28 December 2028 — taking the partnership toward roughly 2.5 gigawatts (CoinDesk, Quartz). AMD directly leased 377MW at Pecos and Hunt County, Texas and Muskogee, Oklahoma; an unnamed cloud provider signed for a further 152MW in Alabama and Georgia with AMD's support. AMD also received warrants over up to 30 million Core Scientific shares at $23.47, of which roughly 6.5 million vest on the initial leases. First customer deployments are targeted for 2027. The counterparty is a bitcoin miner winding its mining operation down, having just ended its Block arrangement — the pivot from hashing to AI leasing, completed.

So what: the scarce asset is no longer the accelerator, it is powered land with a substation and fibre already attached — and the company that sells you accelerators is now bidding for it against you. If your 2027–28 capacity plan assumes you can lease megawatts on demand, re-price it this quarter.

What was signed. Core Scientific announced leases covering more than 529MW of AI data-centre capacity across the United States, on 15-year terms, which it puts at more than $14B of base contracted revenue over their life. The split is 377MW leased directly by AMD (Pecos and Hunt County in Texas; Muskogee, Oklahoma) and 152MW leased by an unnamed cloud provider with AMD support (Alabama, Georgia). The reservation option runs to 28 December 2028 for up to 1,925MW more, which is where the ~2.5GW headline comes from. Initial customer deployments are slated for 2027, not this year (CoinDesk, Quartz).

Why it leads. Every large compute story of the last fortnight has been about who guarantees whose money — Nvidia's discussed backstop for OpenAI's Ohio campus, Anthropic's multi-gigawatt AMD commitment, the megacap capex prints landing today. This one is different in kind: AMD is not buying servers and not renting cloud. It is contracting for the physical layer directly, on utility timescales, in a market where interconnection queues and not chip supply are the binding constraint. A chip vendor that controls powered sites can guarantee its customers' deployments in a way it cannot guarantee their GPU allocations — and it competes with its own customers for the same substations.

The other side of the trade. Core Scientific is a bitcoin miner exiting mining. The AMD leases land as it winds down operations and after it ended its arrangement with Block; its AI-hosting revenue has roughly doubled. The warrant package — up to 30 million shares at $23.47, ~6.5 million vesting on the initial leases — makes AMD a prospective equity holder in its own landlord, which is a structure worth reading twice.

The caveats. The $14B is base contracted revenue over 15 years, not a payment made or a capex number, and the ~2.5GW figure is an option AMD has not exercised. The 152MW cloud counterparty is unnamed. Nothing here delivers a watt before 2027, and interconnection risk is exactly the thing FERC's Section 206 show-cause process (radar, Aug 17) is currently re-writing the rules for.

Watch next. Whether Nvidia responds with direct site leases of its own; whether the unnamed cloud provider surfaces; whether AMD exercises any part of the 1,925MW option before the December 2028 deadline; and how the megacap capex guidance landing tonight treats leased-versus-owned capacity.

IMPACT 8/10#2 ENTERPRISE · AGENT INFRA

MCP's 2026-07-28 spec ships final — the protocol goes stateless and sessions disappear

The Model Context Protocol published its 2026-07-28 specification on schedule, moving from a bidirectional stateful protocol to a request/response model so servers can run on serverless and edge infrastructure (MCP blog). The breaking changes are real: the initialize handshake and the Mcp-Session-Id header are removed entirely, letting any request land on any instance; tasks/list is dropped; Tasks is rebuilt around handles rather than session-bound objects; and six SEPs formally position MCP servers as OAuth 2.1 resource servers. Apps and Tasks now ship under a versioned extensions framework. The spec was locked as a release candidate on 21 May and validated for ten weeks before publication. Anthropic shipped Claude support the same day (Anthropic); The Register trailed the break a week out.

If you run an MCP server, this is a migration with a deprecation list attached, not a version bump — read the breaking changes before your next deploy, and treat the OAuth 2.1 resource-server framing as the security work it actually is.

Nvidia launches the Open Secure AI Alliance — and OpenAI, Google and Anthropic are not in it

Nvidia launched the Open Secure AI Alliance on Jul 27, built on the argument that cyber defenders need frontier models they can inspect, modify and run on their own infrastructure. Founding names reported include Microsoft, IBM, Dell, Red Hat, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Cloudflare, Hugging Face, Databricks, GitHub, Salesforce, SAP and the Linux Foundation, building on the Foundation's Akrites project and OpenSSF (SecurityWeek, The Hacker News). The first technical contribution is NOOA (NVIDIA-labs OO Agents), an Apache-2.0 framework for making agent behaviour testable, traceable and auditable. The membership count differs by outlet — 37, "40+" and 52 all appear — and the three largest closed-model labs are absent from every version of the list.

The defensive tooling is consolidating around open, self-hostable models while the frontier labs sit outside it — if your security roadmap assumes your model vendor will supply the defence, note that they did not join the body building it.

IMPACT 7/10#4 POLICY · PRIVACY

Shared Claude chats and Artifacts turned up in Google — for want of a noindex tag

Over the weekend of Jul 25–26, Reddit users found that a site:claude.ai/share query surfaced a long list of shared Claude conversations and Artifacts; the story was carried Jul 27 by Axios, TechCrunch and Fortune. Conversations are private by default and these were pages users chose to make shareable — but the shared pages lacked the noindex metadata that would keep them out of search. Reporting describes indexed pages containing health records, internal company documents, legal discussions and personal contact details. Anthropic's statement is that it does not share chat directories or sitemaps with search engines and that the links are not guessable. The links no longer surface via the Reddit technique, so the indexing itself appears fixed.

"Shared" and "public" are the same thing to a crawler unless someone sets a header — audit every share feature you ship, and tell your staff that a share link is a publication.

IMPACT 7/10#5 ENTERPRISE · SECURITY (DEEP BEAT)

Microsoft's agentic scanner drove a record 570 patches — 316% above last July

July's Patch Tuesday fixed a record 570 vulnerabilities — against 137 in July 2025, a 316% increase — and Microsoft attributes the jump to its own AI (TechCrunch, Krebs on Security). MDASH — the Multi-Model Agentic Scanning Harness — now runs in production across the full Windows codebase hunting exploitable flaws continuously. The batch includes 59 rated Critical (48 RCE, nine privilege-escalation, one bypass, one spoofing) and three zero-days, two of them actively exploited (Kaspersky).

AI-assisted discovery is now outrunning patch-deployment capacity — if your patch window was sized for a 137-bug July, it is the wrong size for a 570-bug one, and this is the vendor doing the finding, not the attacker.

IMPACT 7/10#6 RESEARCH · AGENT SECURITY

StakeBench: 3,168 adversarial runs, and not one scenario consistently blocked

A stakeholder-centric benchmark from researchers at Nanyang Technological University, ST Engineering, IBM Research and UIUC reports that today's AI web agents have no dependable defence against prompt injection: across 3,168 adversarial runs covering 264 benchmark cases on NanoBrowser and BrowserUse, not a single attack scenario was consistently blocked on systems powered by GPT-5 and Gemini (CSO Online). Swapping GPT-5 for Gemini-2.5-Flash raised indirect-injection success by 26.49 points on NanoBrowser and 6.2 points on BrowserUse. The paper's sharper point is methodological: the same agent can look stealthy against user-targeted attacks, exposed against seller-targeted ones and unstable against platform-targeted ones, so a single aggregate attack-success rate hides the risk that matters.

Aggregate ASR is the wrong metric — score your agents per stakeholder, and stop treating a model swap as security-neutral when it moves injection success by 26 points.

IMPACT 9/10D3 POLICY · EU / US

Three days to Aug 1, four to Aug 2 — and Brussels now names cyber as the enforcement priority

WHAT CHANGED Calendar tightens 4→3 days on Aug 1 and 5→4 on Aug 2, and the substance sharpens: the Commission's Action Plan on Cybersecurity and AI (COM(2026) 577, adopted Jul 7 and carried here in edition 028) states that from Aug 2 it will use its AI Act supervisory powers over general-purpose models particularly those posing systemic cybersecurity risks — information requests, evaluations, mandated mitigations, fines to 3% of global turnover. On this week's evidence, that is not a theoretical category.
Two deadlines land inside four days. Aug 1: the classified cyber-benchmarking process for "covered frontier models" is due from Treasury, NSA and CISA under EO 14409, alongside the reported voluntary 30-day pre-release framework with OpenAI, Google and Anthropic. Aug 2: Article 50 transparency plus the Commission's GPAI enforcement and penalty powers switch on — fines to €15M or 3% of global turnover. The obligations have applied since Aug 2 2025; what ends is the grace period on enforcement (EU Digital Strategy, AI Act). The plan also commits €200M from EU programmes and €100M from the EIC Fund toward sovereign AI-cybersecurity capability and an EU third-party evaluation capacity.

If you are a GPAI provider, the cyber chapter is now the sharp end of Aug 2 — have your systemic-risk cyber documentation ready to hand over on request, not merely drafted.
