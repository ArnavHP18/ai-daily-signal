OpenAI publishes 722 maths papers written by a model it hasn't released

What was released. 722 manuscripts grouped into 372 families, Lean formalisations for many (not all) proofs, a formalisation catalogue, ten summaries of the model's reasoning, and statistics on problems attempted and compute used. The repository is openai/math under Apache 2.0.

How it was made. Roughly 4,000 problems were posed; the average result used about three hours of ChatGPT Pro-level thinking compute. Scientific American's report (via Techmeme) says almost all results came from one prompt handed to one AI agent, with some possibly needing several attempts.

How it was shared. OpenAI says it worked out its approach with the Institute for Advanced Study's Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI. On Hacker News (172 points) the debate was about method and attribution, not whether the proofs check.

Watch next. Whether mathematicians confirm the most significant results, how journals treat AI-written submissions, and whether Google DeepMind or Anthropic answer with their own releases. Also watch whether this model is the one held back by OpenAI's training pause.

IMPACT 7/10#1 RESEARCH · AI FOR MATHS · DATED TUE 6 OCT

OpenAI publishes 722 maths manuscripts written by an unreleased internal model, many with Lean-checked proofs, from about 4,000 problems it was given

On Tuesday 6 October OpenAI released new mathematical results from an unreleased internal frontier model: 722 manuscripts in 372 families, posted to a public GitHub repository (openai/math, Apache 2.0) with Lean formalisations for many, but not all, of the proofs and abridged summaries of the model's reasoning. The model was posed roughly 4,000 problems; the average result used about the compute of three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking. Topics span number theory, combinatorics, theoretical computer science and mathematical physics, and OpenAI says it worked out how to share the results with the Institute for Advanced Study's Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI (OpenAI (primary) · Unite.AI · Interesting Engineering · FourWeekMBA). Scientific American's account, via Techmeme, says nearly all the results came from a single prompt handed to a single AI agent, some possibly after several attempts (Techmeme / Scientific American). The post drew 172 points and 119 comments on Hacker News, where methodology and credit were debated (Hacker News AI digest, 7 Oct).

This is the largest batch of machine-generated mathematics a lab has published, and the Lean files mean much of it can be checked by computer rather than taken on trust. The question has moved from 'can a model prove things' to 'who reviews 722 papers, and who gets credit'. It also shows OpenAI has an internal model stronger than anything it sells while its training pause on the most capable models continues (Still Tracking).

IMPACT 7/10#2 MODELS · DECISION MODELS · EMERGING TECH · DATED TUE 6 OCT

OpenAI's Decisions API leaves preview: a public beta on a new gpt-6-luna model that returns typed answers, priced at $0.10 per million input tokens with output free

OpenAI opened its Decisions API (/v1/decisions) as a public beta on 6 October. It returns typed answers to questions the developer defines, over text and image input, rather than generated prose, and runs on a model called gpt-6-luna. Pricing is $0.10 per million input tokens; output tokens and cache reads and writes are not billed, though regional and long-context premiums apply. It supports Zero Data Retention and HIPAA use for eligible customers, with processing in the US or Europe (OpenAI docs (primary) · Unite.AI · AlphaSignal). The docs link reached 76 points on Hacker News (Hacker News AI digest, 7 Oct). For comparison, TypeSafe's Jev, which started this category on 15 September, lists $0.042 per million input tokens with free output (DataCamp on Jev).

Edition 104 to 105 carried the Decisions API as 'preview, still unpriced'. Now the biggest API vendor sells a decision model at about 2.4 times Jev's input price, with the same 'output is free' model. Any team routing, classifying or approving with an LLM call now has a first-party, cheaper, typed alternative from its existing vendor, which is the main threat to Jev's lead. Watch for independent accuracy and calibration tests against Jev and the open models (Decider, Clef, GLiNER2.5-Decide).

IMPACT 7/10#3 ENTERPRISE · AI SECURITY (WEDNESDAY DEEP BEAT) · DATED TUE 6 OCT

Anthropic splits cyber access into three vetted tiers with fewer safety blocks, up to testing of power grids and interbank systems in a tier reviewed with the US government

On 6 October Anthropic expanded its Cyber Verification Program into three tiers that loosen its cyber-misuse classifiers for vetted users, on Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Mythos 5.1 and future models. Defense Access covers SOC work, incident response, malware reverse-engineering and vulnerability analysis, and is open to company, nonprofit, university and government security teams, critical-infrastructure operators, open-source maintainers and individual researchers with a disclosure record (review in "a few days"). Red Team Access adds authorised penetration testing for in-house, government and pen-testing firms only ("a few weeks"); real-time blocks on physical harm or mass disruption stay on. Specialized Access, for testing flight systems, power grids, telecom networks, interbank transfer infrastructure and government networks, is reviewed with the US government. Anthropic says its Project Glasswing partners found at least 129,000 verified vulnerabilities (over 33,000 critical or high) between April and July; Booz Allen and Comcast describe using Mythos (Anthropic (primary) · Bloomberg).

This is the first time a frontier lab has written down graded offensive-security permissions, with the government co-signing the top tier. Security teams get stronger tools, but the same week California and the FTC are investigating agents that attacked real systems (Still Tracking), so expect the vetting criteria, not the models, to draw scrutiny. Other labs will probably copy the tier structure.

IMPACT 6/10#4 POLICY · EU AI ACT · PROVENANCE · DATED MON 5 OCT

OpenAI will watermark ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU with 'textGrain', a hidden statistical signal, to meet the AI Act's labelling rules

OpenAI said on 5 October it will add an invisible watermark to text from ChatGPT and Codex in the European Union, rolling out over the coming weeks to eligible users on all plans, to comply with the EU AI Act transparency rules that took effect on 2 August. The method, textGrain, uses a secret key to nudge which words the model picks, leaving a statistical signature with no hidden characters or unusual punctuation. Detection is limited for now to vetted researchers and specialised institutions. OpenAI cautions that a missing watermark "does not prove human authorship"; in its own tests, replacing 25% of words with synonyms cut detection accuracy to about 17% (TechCrunch · DIGITIMES · Interesting Engineering · Business Today).

This is the first frontier lab to ship text watermarking in production, and only because the law made it. OpenAI's own numbers show light paraphrasing defeats it, so EU schools, publishers and platforms should not treat it as an AI detector. It does set the bar that Google, Anthropic and Mistral will be measured against in the EU.

IMPACT 5/10#6 MODELS · SMALL OPEN CLASSIFIER · EMERGING TECH · SURFACED 6–7 OCT

Musubi's PolicyLM: a small open model that reads a content policy you write and returns one probability per category, with no text generated

Trust-and-safety startup Musubi released PolicyLM, a small open guard classifier. Give it a policy and a piece of text and it returns one probability per policy category; nothing is generated, so there is nothing to parse. It has two modes: a fixed harm taxonomy (NVIDIA's Aegis 2.0 categories) and a custom mode where you type category names and short rules at inference time, with no retraining. It handles single English-first text messages up to 2,048 tokens (Musubi blog (primary) · Baseten model library). Musubi's post calls it 1.7B parameters; Baseten's listing describes a 1B model, and we could not reconcile the two. The launch post appeared on Hacker News on 6–7 October with little discussion (Hacker News AI digest, 7 Oct); its exact release date was not confirmed.

This is the decision-model idea (Jev, Decider, GLiNER2.5-Decide) applied to moderation: write the rule in plain English, get a calibrated score back, at small-model cost. Platforms paying an LLM per moderation call now have an open option to test. No independent benchmarks yet, so treat it as a watch item.

Multiply Labs' robotic clusters for cell and gene therapy manufacturing, which it says cut cost per dose by 74%, are backed by a $75M Series B

Product first: Multiply Labs builds robotic clusters that run the manual steps of making cell and gene therapies and other advanced biologics, working alongside existing pharma equipment. The company says they cut cost per dose by 74% and give up to 100 times the throughput of manual manufacturing (company figures). It raised a $75M Series B from AstraZeneca Ventures, Lingotto, Legend Biotech and Strange Ventures, with Casdin, Lux and Founders Fund returning, taking total funding past $100M (The AI Insider · SiliconANGLE). Cell therapies are very expensive partly because each dose is made by hand. A drugmaker (AstraZeneca) investing suggests robotic manufacturing is moving from pilot to capacity planning. This is an example of physical AI paying off in a regulated industry, not in a demo.
