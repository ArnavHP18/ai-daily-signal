Anthropic prices the biggest IPO ever at $83 a share, raising about $81bn

The Claude maker sold 980 million shares above its $70-78 range, valuing it near $1.5 trillion; trading on Nasdaq as ANT starts Thursday.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceChecked on Anthropic's pricing announcement (opened) for price, share counts, listing date and underwriters; raise size and valuation from Al Jazeera and the BBC (opened); first-day trading not yet available
Anthropic said late on 18 Nov it priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above the $70-78 range it set when the roadshow began. It is selling 980 million Class A shares: 956.4 million new shares and 23.6 million from existing holders, so about $81.3bn in total (Al Jazeera), more than SpaceX raised in June and the largest IPO ever. Underwriters led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley can buy 147 million more shares within 30 days. The SEC declared the registration effective on 18 Nov; shares start trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as ANT on 19 Nov, and the deal closes on 20 Nov. Reports put the valuation at about $1.4-1.5 trillion; the BBC says a further ~$26bn comes from private placements, and Bloomberg reports about $3bn came from Japanese investors (headline). It is the first US company focused only on AI to list.

So what: the first pure-play frontier lab on a public exchange gives banks and investors a daily price for AI demand; how ANT trades will move AI credit, data-centre lending and private AI valuations, and its disclosures become the benchmark for model-vendor risk reviews.

#2Security · NGINX · 18 NovIMPACT 9/10

Critical NGINX flaws found with AI let attackers take over web servers without logging in

F5 and the nginx project released NGINX 1.31.9 (mainline) and 1.30.7 (stable) on 18 Nov, fixing ten CVEs. The two worst are in HTTP/2: CVE-2026-123007, memory corruption in flow control that affects versions back to 1.9.5 (2015), and CVE-2026-123633, a use-after-free. Wiz and F5 rate both 9.2 (CVSS 4.0); nginx.org lists 123007 as 'major'. Both let an unauthenticated attacker run code on servers with HTTP/2 enabled, even ones serving only static files. Wiz, Rapid7, Xint, Ikotas Labs and Metnir found them independently, Wiz with its AI code-scanning harness (validated with Gemini 4 Carbon and ChatGPT 5.6 Sol). Rapid7 published a working exploit; Xint chained the bugs with a Linux kernel flaw for root ('nginx2root') and counts 98.9% of 11.9M version-visible NGINX hosts on affected releases. The exploit needs about 128GB of HTTP/2 traffic, roughly two minutes on a 10-gigabit link (CyberScoop). SANS sees no exploitation yet.

So what: NGINX sits in front of most bank websites, APIs and ingress controllers; with a public exploit, patch internet-facing NGINX (and the copies inside appliances and Kubernetes ingress) this week, or disable HTTP/2 where you cannot.

#8

A tiny 'energy-based' reasoner solves hard puzzles with 345 times less compute

NOTABLEOfficial sourceEmerging · new model types · 18 Nov
A new paper introduces Energy-Based Reasoners (EBRs): looped models that repeat a small block many times, like recent 'looped' reasoners, but also learn an energy function that rates how good the current reasoning state is. Each loop is a step of gradient descent on that energy, so the model can score its own candidate answers, decide when to stop (when the gradient flattens) and pick the lowest-energy answer, with no external verifier. With only 1.8M parameters, an EBR reaches 99.9% on Sudoku-Extreme using 345x less inference compute than the next-best model, and state-of-the-art coverage on N-Queens and graph colouring. Results so far are on constraint puzzles, not language. It drew 298 points on Hacker News (headline says 385x; the paper says 345x).

So what: a built-in 'how sure am I' signal is what agents and decision systems lack; if energy-based checking transfers beyond puzzles, it could give cheaper, self-verifying models for rules-heavy tasks like compliance checks.
