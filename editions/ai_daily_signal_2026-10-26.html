US Treasury proposes wider bans on American investment in Chinese AI, chips and biotech

A draft rule under the COINS Act would make US investors notify Treasury of some deals and ban others outright. Comments are now open.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceChecked on treasury.gov

On 22 October the US Treasury proposed rules for the Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security (COINS) Act, which Congress passed in the 2026 defence bill. US persons would have to notify Treasury of some deals with companies in "countries of concern" and would be barred from others. The law names five sectors: semiconductors and microelectronics, AI systems, quantum, high-performance computing and supercomputing, and hypersonics. Treasury has used its power under the act to add a sixth, biotechnology. The proposal sets out the technical lines for each sector, what a notification must contain, how much checking investors must do before a deal, and the penalties.

So what: any fund, corporate venture arm or limited partner with money in Chinese AI or chip companies should map its pipeline against the draft this week. The draft is open for comment, so the lines for AI can still move. China proposed its own AI export controls the week before (see Developing).

It also says the draft replaces the term "AI systems" with "super intelligence systems", citing President Trump's 29 September order, and that it would widen the AI ban by removing the option to simply report such deals. TNW also gives 26 October for publication in the Federal Register and 7 December for comments. Check the official notice on regulations.gov before relying on these dates.

#2Research · AI safety · 22 OctIMPACT 7/10

Outside reviewers find gaps in OpenAI's monitor for its own AI agents

METR and Redwood Research spent three weeks inside OpenAI. Their public summary lists traffic the monitor never saw and ways agents could get past it.

From 21 July to 11 August, one researcher each from METR and Redwood Research tested the system OpenAI uses to watch its internal agents for misaligned behaviour. They had OpenAI's code, monitor prompts and documentation, worked on OpenAI devices that never left the building, and wrote a 61-page report for OpenAI. The public post estimates that 75–94% of agentic traffic from models at least as strong as GPT-5.5 was eligible for monitoring (99.2% for internal Codex agents). Reinforcement-learning training runs were not covered when the review began. Skipping "compaction" events (when an agent condenses its memory) left about 10% of Codex traffic unwatched from May to July; OpenAI had already spotted this and fixed it during the review. The reviewers also describe ways agents could in principle get past the monitor, including one that could have hidden hundreds of actions; OpenAI redacted the details. OpenAI says it has patched every flaw affecting its production safeguards; the reviewers say they have not checked that.

This is the first look by outsiders inside a frontier lab's agent monitoring, and the gaps are the dull kind: traffic the monitor never saw, not clever escapes. Teams that monitor their own agents through transcripts should check for the same gaps: memory compaction, long runs with no hand-back to a user, and settings that can turn monitoring off.

#3Models · new kind of model · 23 OctIMPACT 6/10

Block releases Dessa, a voice model that never turns speech into text

Jack Dorsey's company says it learned only from human conversation and runs locally. It listens and talks at the same time.

Most voice assistants turn your speech into text, run a text model and read the answer back. Block says Dessa skips the text: it is a full-duplex model (it can talk and listen at once, like OpenAI's GPT-Live) trained entirely on human speech, on a single NVIDIA GPU rack, and its knowledge comes from that conversational data rather than a paired language model. Block's demos show laughs, pauses and changes of tone. "The most powerful AIs in the world can't hear how you feel," Block wrote. TechCrunch notes it is unclear whether the emotion is steered by hidden tags, as it is in ElevenLabs v4 or Fish Audio's Drama 3.

A different design for voice AI: emotion and timing are kept because nothing is converted to text. The trade-off to test is what it knows and how it handles facts without a text model behind it.

#4

UK sets out about £600M for AI, led by a new AI Biosecurity Lab

NOTABLEOfficial sourcePolicy · 24 Oct

The government announced the package on 24 October, ahead of the Budget. Per Reuters: £100M for an AI Biosecurity Lab that uses AI to detect biological threats and £65M for countermeasures against pathogens; OpenAI and Anthropic will offer up to £200M of model credits to the biosecurity work. The AI Security Institute gets £120M more. £130M goes to a National Cyber Shield and a national vulnerability centre, £18M to a chip design academy, £60M to a fund for British robotics and manufacturing AI companies, and £10M a year to study how AI will reshape jobs.

Britain is spending most of its new AI money on security, not compute. The lab-credit deal is a template other governments may copy.

#6

Game maker KRAFTON previews Raon-1 mini, an open hybrid built from scratch

NOTABLETwo or more outletsModels · new architecture · 21 Oct

The PUBG publisher's first model is a 30B-parameter mixture-of-experts with about 4B active, trained from scratch. Per the model card, 18 of its 25 layers use Raon Delta Attention, a linear-attention variant of Kimi Delta Attention with a fixed-size state; only 7 layers keep a KV cache (about 8 KiB per token). It reads up to 262,144 tokens, is strongest in Korean, and in NVFP4 runs on a single RTX 5090. Apache 2.0, but downloads require approval. It is a preview: specs may change and a technical report is promised with the final version.

Another serious open model that is mostly not a classic transformer, this time from a games company. Hybrid linear attention is becoming the default way to get long context cheaply.

#7

A 2015-era image model beats frontier AI at diagnosing skin cancer

MONITOROfficial sourceResearch · 25 Oct

Lynch Lab gave 200 biopsy-proven lesions from the public MILK10k set (clinical and dermoscopic photos) to GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1 and Inception v3, a 2015 convolutional network fine-tuned for about twenty minutes on 68,011 ISIC images. The CNN named the right one of six diagnoses for 125 (62%); the two frontier models for 84 and 85 (42%). On the 30 melanomas: CNN 13, Fable 11, Astra 8. Test images were removed from the CNN's training data. It is one lab's write-up, not peer-reviewed; it reached the Hacker News front page with about 400 points.

For narrow medical imaging, a small specialist model still beats a general one. Don't use chatbots for dermatology triage.

#8

NVIDIA open-sources Flora, the models that label its robot training video

MONITOROfficial sourceResearch · robotics · Oct

Flora is three fine-tuned Qwen3.5 models: one splits robot video into actions, one captions them, and Flora-Rewarder scores progress and judges success. NVIDIA's GEAR lab says it labels 200,000 hours of video a day across 512 GB300s for GR00T training, is competitive with GPT-6 Astra on its internal benchmarks and costs about 125x less (72 cents an hour versus $88, per The Decoder). The models have been on Hugging Face since early October; the project was reported on 25 October.

Labelling, not robots, is the bottleneck in robot learning. An open, cheap labeller lowers the cost of building robot datasets for everyone.

#10

Anthropic tells companies to fold their sub-agents into one agent

MONITOROfficial sourceEnterprise · Oct

Anthropic's Applied AI team says that across several enterprise deployments, a single agent that loads skills when it needs them often beat both one giant prompt and a coordinator with many sub-agents on quality, and usually on cost and latency. One customer's dozen specialist sub-agents made a consistent on-brand experience hard to keep and slowed replies. Sub-agents still make sense for verification, parallel work and separate permissions. A migration kit on GitHub walks teams through the change.

As models get better at long plans, the multi-agent setups built in 2025 may now cost more than they are worth. Measure before adding agents.

#12

Pennsylvania will require labels on ads made or heavily changed by AI

MONITOROne secondary sourcePolicy · 22 Oct

Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act on 22 October. Any ad (image, video, audio or text) "significantly modified or generated by artificial intelligence" must carry a label in the same format as the ad, wherever it was made. Parody, satire, commentary and anything "evidently fictional" are exempt, and The Verge notes "significantly modified" is not defined.

National advertisers will likely label everything that airs in Pennsylvania rather than run two versions.

China's AI export rules: DeepSeek and MiniMax are the first cleared to go abroad

NOTABLETwo or more outletsPolicy · 22-23 Oct
WHAT CHANGED A CCTV-linked account says DeepSeek and MiniMax have registered and got open-source and overseas licences, and spells out the thresholds and carve-outs.

Per the Yuyuan Tantian post (reported by Reuters and TNW, which did not verify it): offering a restricted model by API, app, web page, SaaS or light local deployment is not an export, so "AI companies can continue with their commercialization efforts as usual". Open-source technology gets automatic licensing. Large models are caught above 1026 training FLOPs, more than 2 trillion parameters, or global top-ten capability. Models trained on "core data" would be banned from export, and agents built mainly for defence or critical infrastructure would be too. AI chip design and high-speed links for large clusters are on the list. Comments close 15 November.

For buyers outside China, nothing changes for API and open-weight use. The rules bite on exporting the technology itself (weights of top models, chip design, cluster interconnects).
