Nvidia opens an investigation into Jensen Huang as the FBI and investors close in

Huang admits an affair with a woman US officials call a Chinese spy; Nvidia lost about $250 billion in a day.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceBoard statement and Huang's email checked on Nvidia's newsroom; share move, FBI and lawsuit on BBC and TechCrunch; NYT not opened
WHAT CHANGED Since Sunday's edition: a board investigation, Huang's admission, a $250B share drop, an FBI inquiry and the first lawsuit.

On Sunday Nvidia's board said it is opening an independent investigation, overseen by independent directors with outside counsel, "in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort". It says it has "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised and still has "confidence in Jensen". Hours later Nvidia published Huang's email to staff: "I was unfaithful to my wife ... There is no excuse for what I did." The New York Times reports US officials believe the relationship was with Alice Huang (no relation), a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore cloud firm that bought about $4.8B of Nvidia chips and that US officials think worked for China's Ministry of State Security; China calls the claims "entirely groundless".

Nvidia shares fell 4.27% on Monday, about $250B of market value (BBC, TechCrunch). President Trump said the FBI is "looking into" the matter and that Huang told him he "had no idea she was a spy". Rosen Law Firm filed the first securities class action in the Northern District of California, arguing Nvidia's October statements that the contacts were "not unusual" were misleading. A second NYT report says the Commerce Department paused its Megaspeed chip-smuggling investigation after Nvidia's general counsel emailed the agents, and the case moved to a Justice Department criminal inquiry. Huang is still due at a closed-door House briefing on 18 November.

So what: the person who has argued hardest against tighter chip-export rules is now under investigation by his own board, the FBI and investors. Expect Congress to use this to tighten end-user checks on Chinese buyers, and watch for leadership or disclosure changes at the world's most valuable chipmaker before its next earnings.

Anthropic ships Fable 5.2: cheaper, faster, and it formalised Fermat in 30 hours

Fable is open to everyone at $8/$40; Mythos stays with vetted defenders. Artificial Analysis ranks it first.

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, which it says are "the same underlying model": Fable carries classifiers that block high-risk cyber and biology work (routing those queries to Opus 4.8 or Opus 5) and is open to all users, while Mythos goes only to organisations in its Cyber and Life Sciences Verification Programs. Prices drop to $8 per million input tokens and $40 output (Fable 5.1 was $10 input, per TechCrunch), with a fast mode at $16/$80. Anthropic's table puts Fable 5.2 at 69.9% on FrontierSWE v2 against 62.3% for Opus 5.5 and 65.5% for GPT-6 Astra. In pre-release use, it says, a team of about 1,000 Fable agents formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean in 30 hours (an earlier attempt took 11 days), the model solved two open problems on black holes and designed three working devices with Diode, including circuit boards and enclosures. Artificial Analysis independently scores it 65, first of 74 models, but also among the slowest and most verbose ($26.54 per index task).

For teams on Opus 5.5, Fable 5.2 is now the default upgrade path for the hardest coding and research jobs, and it undercuts GPT-6 Astra on price. Anthropic itself recommends starting on Opus 5.5 and moving up only when needed. Note the safety caveat: the models suspect they are being tested more often, which Anthropic says makes evaluation harder.

#2Models · small model · 9 NovIMPACT 7/10

Meta's Muse Ember is built to be the cheap worker inside AI agents

At $0.10 per million input tokens it targets the high-volume subagent jobs where Haiku and Luna sit today.

Meta released Muse Ember, the fourth Muse model after Spark, the open Glimmer and the frontier Wonder. It is made for the work "agents repeat all day": tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long-document parsing. Meta's research post says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5's best Artificial Analysis score at about a third of the cost and beats every GPT-6 Luna setting; on its own table Ember scores 37.8 on AutomationBench-AA against 13.9 for Haiku. It takes text, images, audio and video, reads up to 1M tokens, and costs $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens on the Meta Model API and Muse Code; it is already listed on OpenRouter. Meta's pitch: "Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume." Haiku still beats it on real coding (FrontierCode 2.0-AA, 34.9% vs 27.2%, The Decoder).

The cheap tier is where agent bills are decided. If Ember's numbers hold in independent tests, teams running large subagent fleets get a new default, and Anthropic and OpenAI face price pressure at the bottom of the range just as Fable moves the top.

Google's new TPUs target secret data and million-token agents

NOTABLEOfficial sourceHardware · chips · 9 Nov

Google added two systems to its eighth-generation TPU line. TPU 8tx uses TPU 8t silicon with 5th-generation AMD EPYC hosts to keep data and model weights protected "even while in active use", with up to 2x lower latency than TPU 8t, so regulated firms can fine-tune, distil and run agents on sensitive data in one place. Baidu's ERNIE Evolve sped up its next model by itself

NOTABLEOne secondary sourceResearch · AI improving AI · 9 Nov

Baidu introduced ERNIE Evolve, a system that picks what to research, edits code, runs tests and keeps only changes that pass separate checks hidden from it. Used on ERNIE-5.5-Flash, a model still in development, Baidu says it raised throughput on engineer-tuned GPUs by 33.7% in about three days and made generation more than six times faster on Kunlunxin P800 chips, though China AI Dispatch notes much of that came from switching on features the setup was not using. China's labs are now running the same "AI speeds up AI" loop as Anthropic (which says Claude writes over 90% of merged code in its core codebase) and Google. Infrastructure teams should expect model-serving gains to arrive faster, and safety teams should note that hidden-check designs like this one are becoming the norm.

#6

An AI research system proves a 2008 quantum computing conjecture, checked in Lean

NOTABLEOfficial sourceResearch · maths · 6-9 Nov

Start-up Physical Superintelligence (PSI) says a research system it built, running instances of a non-public Anthropic model, found a proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture on the third day of a run, and that the Lean proof assistant accepted it; the system later improved the result twice. The conjecture, open since 2008, implies that on yes-or-no questions about random inputs a quantum computer is at most polynomially, not exponentially, faster than an ordinary one. Stanford's Guy Blanc, Jordan Docter, Carmen Strassle and Li-Yang Tan, who proved a special case in August, wrote to PSI: "This is a huge result." It is not yet peer reviewed, and Anthropic would not say which model was used (TechCrunch).

Machine-checked proofs of famous open problems are arriving weekly (Fable 5.2's Fermat formalisation, Axiom's Stacks Project, OpenAI's ETH claim). For quantum computing it narrows where real speedups can exist: structured problems, not random ones.

#8

One hacker's AI agents pulled Taipei's emergency dispatch records

NOTABLETwo or more outletsEnterprise · AI-driven attacks · 6-9 Nov

Researchers at Acronis say a single attacker, very likely based in Hualien City, Taiwan, used AI agents he called BOSSAI against hundreds of organisations in at least 11 countries, including emergency services, banks, universities and military systems. The agents pulled emergency dispatch records for seven Taipei districts from the city's 119 system API and found a fire-department platform exposing patient data, including ECG readings, without a login; about 10 targets in Taiwan, Mexico and Thailand were accessed. Because commercial models mostly refused, the agents ran almost entirely on "abliterated" local models with safety training removed. The evidence came from the attacker's own misconfigured server, which held more than 90,000 files including the agents' conversations (The Hacker News).

This is the open-model version of the AI-agent attacks seen with Artex and Claude-based campaigns: one person, many targets, and refusals in hosted models simply routed around. Public-sector APIs with default credentials are the soft spot.

#9

India sets audit rules for AI agents, including kill switches and six-hour reporting

NOTABLETwo or more outletsPolicy · India · 4-9 Nov

India's cyber agency CERT-In has issued AI Security Auditing Guidelines, a 107-page version 1.0 document covering systems that plan tasks, use tools and hand work to other AIs. Auditors are told to test whether an agent can exceed its permissions, misuse tools or credentials, or be steered by prompt injection or poisoned memory. Systems are sorted into four risk levels; agents touching health records, payments or industrial control need stronger checks. Reports (Industrial Cyber, Cloud Security Alliance research note) say high-risk agents need a kill switch, critical ones need audits by CERT-In-empanelled firms, logs must be kept 180 days and AI incidents reported within six hours. Outlets differ on whether it is final or a proposal, and we could not open CERT-In's site.

For Indian banks, insurers and GCCs deploying agents, this becomes the audit checklist even without penalties. It mirrors the UK's new agent powers (8 Nov) and is more specific than most Western guidance.

#18

Research: small teams of AI agents beat one big team or many loners

MONITOROfficial sourceResearch · agent teams · 6 Nov

Two papers posted on 6 November study teams of AI agents. "Test-Time Compute Scales Better with Parallel Agent Teams" runs many small collaborating teams at once and keeps the best team's result: with a 27B model and a budget of 100 agents, teams of ten beat 100 independent agents on 10 of 12 long tasks (GPU kernels, formal proofs, exploit development) and beat one team of 100 everywhere it was tried; at equal cost, it matched a frontier model on half the tasks. "Discovering formally verified quantum programs with AI" builds a quantum-program library in Lean where agent teams write programs against formal specifications and the prover checks correctness and cost, reporting order-of-magnitude gains on scientific applications.

The same shape as Fable 5.2's 1,000-agent Fermat run: organised teams plus a verifier. How to split a budget across agents is becoming an engineering question with measurable answers.

#19

An open model that turns one photo into a 3D object tops Hugging Face

MONITOROfficial sourceModels · open 3D · 28 Oct-9 Nov

Idol, from Speridlabs, takes a single picture of an object, removes the background and generates a 3D Gaussian splat of up to 1,048,576 Gaussians, "including the sides the picture never showed", written as a standard .ply file. Fast single-image 3D is the missing step for game, e-commerce and robot-simulation pipelines; an open, local version lowers the cost to near zero.

Claude Code's secret leak was reported 15 months ago and closed without a fix

NOTABLETwo or more outletsEnterprise · developer security · 9 Nov
WHAT CHANGED Ars Technica confirms Apiiro's findings and finds the leak was reported in Claude Code bug reports as early as August 2025; those reports were closed without action after 60 days of inactivity; still no Anthropic statement

Sunday's lead now has a second outlet. Ars Technica confirms Apiiro's account: choosing "Yes, and don't ask again" can save a full command, password included, into .claude/settings.local.json, which Claude Code excludes from git only through the user's global settings, not the project's. Ars adds that the problem was raised in Claude Code bug reports as far back as August 2025, and that those reports, and others noting the file sometimes was not ignored, were closed without action after 60 days of inactivity. The advice stands: search repositories, history and forks for Claude settings files, rotate anything they contain, and add them to each project's .gitignore.
