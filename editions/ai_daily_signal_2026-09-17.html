OpenAI put the advertiser inside the answer — Sponsored Agents go live in ChatGPT with named brands attached

On 16 September OpenAI began piloting Sponsored Agents: a ChatGPT ad that, when clicked, opens a labelled, advertiser-run conversation inside ChatGPT rather than a browser tab. The sponsored agent answers follow-ups and can hand off to the business's own site; the exchange is labelled, held distinct from ChatGPT's independent answer, and kept separate from the conversation the user originally started (The Next Web, Search Engine Roundtable). The pilot is running with selected US advertisers and it already has names: Angi announced the same day that it is among the first brands in the test, framing it as an expansion into "emerging AI-powered advertising surfaces" (GlobeNewswire); Wayfair has confirmed it is participating at limited scale. From the same date OpenAI also added campaign-building tools for advertisers and pushed ChatGPT Ads into HubSpot and Shopify. Read it against yesterday's lead-adjacent card: Cloudflare's crawler wall went live on 15 September, defaulting AI training and agent crawlers off ad-supported pages. Within forty-eight hours the open web's ad inventory got harder for AI companies to reach — and the AI company started selling its own.

So what: the answer layer now has a paid tier that talks back, and it is being sold through the CRM and commerce platforms your marketing team already runs — if you own demand generation, the question this quarter stops being "how do we rank in AI answers" and becomes "what does a sponsored conversation cost, and who owns the transcript."

Sponsored Agents is the supply side arriving in between: OpenAI is no longer only the destination that absorbed the click, it is now the seller of the placement. Whatever the open web loses in crawlable ad inventory, the assistant is building on its own land.

IMPACT 8/10#2 HARDWARE · ENERGY

NVIDIA, Google and Emerald AI launch a grid-flexibility alliance — with Anthropic, National Grid and Constellation inside

Announced 16 September: the AI Energy Management Alliance (AEMA), a coalition for data centres that can vary their electricity draw in response to grid conditions rather than simply connecting and pulling (NVIDIA, Unite.AI). 18 launch partners join the three founders, spanning both sides of the meter: Anthropic and Analog Devices on the compute side, National Grid, AES, RWE, Constellation and NRG on the power side. The headline claim is that roughly half of the power system's capacity goes unused across the year and that making data centres moderately flexible could unlock 100GW from existing infrastructure. First work item: common standards for response speed, reliability, performance measurement and operational data sharing (PYMNTS).

Interconnection queues, not chips, are the binding constraint on the 2027 build — if flexibility becomes the price of a faster grid connection, "how fast can this campus shed load" turns into a procurement question, not a sustainability one.

IMPACT 8/10#3 POLICY · EDUCATION

Florida adopts statewide AI rules for schools and colleges: companion AI banned, parental consent required

On 16 September the Florida State Board of Education adopted rules covering K-12 districts and state colleges that prohibit AI designed to simulate companionship or emotional relationships, undisclosed behaviour monitoring, social scoring, psychological profiling, and vendors selling student data or using it to train AI models (CBS Miami, Central Florida Public Media). Districts must notify parents when a student will use an AI product, obtain consent, and offer a non-AI instructional alternative of similar quality to any parent who refuses. The rules do not ban AI outright; they require it to support teachers and parents rather than replace them. Districts have until 1 July 2027 to finalise policies for the 2027-28 school year (News4Jax).

"No training on student data" and "a working non-AI alternative" are contract terms, not principles — if you sell into US K-12 or public higher ed, both now have a date attached, and a second large state has written them into rule rather than guidance.

IMPACT 7/10#9 POLICY · EDUCATION

Microsoft signs a teachers'-union AI standard that districts can make contractually enforceable

Announced 9 September and picked up widely on 15 September: AFT president Randi Weingarten, UFT president Michael Mulgrew and Microsoft vice chair Brad Smith published a National AI Safety & Privacy Standard for schools (Microsoft, AP via TechXplore). The commitments: no using student data to train AI (Microsoft carves a narrow exception for data that could help protect students), no tracking of students, no AI decisions without human oversight, and plain-language transparency for educators and parents. IMPACT 7/10#10 RESEARCH · ARCHITECTURE

A latent-space language model hits OLMo-3-7B's pretraining loss on half the tokens

arXiv:2609.10715, the NCP-ArchPreview technical report, is the week's top-trending paper on Hugging Face and the most substantive architecture result in this scan (arXiv, HF paper page). It pushes autoregressive pretraining past next-token prediction: the model builds a product-quantised concept vocabulary directly from its own hidden states, learns to predict future concepts through a dedicated Concept Module, then feeds those predictions back to the token level to steer generation — next-token and next-concept objectives trained jointly, end to end. Scaled to 8.9B parameters on 5.73T Dolma-3 tokens, described as the largest latent-space language model demonstrated to date. It reaches OLMo-3-7B's final pretraining loss on 51.3% of the training tokens and beats it by 2.45 points on the downstream macro-average, including +5.99 on GSM8K.

If predicting concepts instead of tokens really halves the tokens needed to reach a given loss, the scaling-law arithmetic every capex plan on this page depends on gets re-derived — worth watching for an independent replication before anyone believes the number.

IMPACT 9/10D1 POLICY · GOVERNANCE

OpenAI confirms weeks of safety talks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind — and says no antitrust waiver is needed

WHAT CHANGED Escalated out of Tracking, where it sat as unconfirmed "working-group talks." OpenAI global policy chief Chris Lehane confirmed at a Washington briefing on Tuesday 15 September that the discussions have run for weeks; Bloomberg reported OpenAI saying it is working with Anthropic and Google on safety. Two details are new: the proposal originated with Demis Hassabis in July and envisages a US-led oversight body that vets frontier models and can trigger industry-wide slowdowns; and Lehane's position is that no antitrust waiver is necessary for the three to coordinate.
Three laboratories that between them hold most of the Western frontier have been negotiating a mechanism to pause each other — and have now said so on the record, in Washington, days after the Amodei pacing essay that Altman and Musk endorsed (Bloomberg, Quartz, The AI Insider). The antitrust line is the part to read twice. Coordinated output restriction among the three largest suppliers in a market is the textbook shape of a problem; Lehane's answer is that safety coordination does not need a waiver. Nobody has said which agency agrees. No body has been constituted, no charter published, no membership defined, and no trigger threshold named.

A private slowdown mechanism among three suppliers is a supply-side risk to every downstream roadmap, and it is being designed without a published charter — if your 2027 plan assumes model capability keeps compounding on the current cadence, this is the thread that could invalidate it, and the one where you have no seat.

IMPACT 9/10D2 BUSINESS · FRONTIER

Pacing the frontier, day five: the first named pushback comes from inside Google's own orbit

WHAT CHANGED Carry, with the argument's shape changing rather than its temperature. After Monday's political escalation, the counter-argument acquired a face: Chris Butler, who runs drug discovery at Google-backed Isomorphic Labs, told Semafor on 15 September that pacing calls will not slow his company — "We're able to still progress regardless of what decisions are made elsewhere." Meta separately said new rules are unnecessary. The dissent is no longer only political; it now comes from an applied-AI company inside the DeepMind family whose chair is the person who proposed the oversight body.
Five days in, the pacing argument has produced a political attack (Monday), an on-the-record confirmation of private coordination (D1 above), and now an applied-science rebuttal from a company that would be governed by it (Semafor, Tech Startups). Butler's point is the strongest version of the objection: a drug-discovery programme running on existing models is not obviously the risk surface anyone means by "frontier," and a slowdown negotiated among general-purpose labs would land on it anyway. Nobody in the debate has yet drawn a line between the capability being paced and the applications riding on it.

Watch for the scope definition, not the sentiment — whether a slowdown covers training runs, releases or deployed applications decides whether this is a lab-internal question or a constraint on every company building on top.

IMPACT 7/10D4 POLICY · COPYRIGHT
WHAT CHANGED Escalated, and a five-edition ambiguity is closed. Edition 089 recorded an unresolved conflict between secondary trackers over the Andersen v. Stability AI trial date. It is resolved: the jury trial began on 8 September in the Northern District of California, with 12 jurors seated. It is the first US jury trial on the "model-as-copy" theory, and no verdict has been returned.

The first US jury on whether a model is a copy has been sitting since 8 September

Sarah Andersen, Kelly McKernan and Karla Ortiz's class action against Stability AI, Midjourney and DeviantArt went to a jury on 8 September (Sigma Law Group, CourtListener docket 3:23-cv-00201). The theory on trial is the one the Anthropic settlement never reached: Bartz closed in July for $1.5B across 482,460 works, but it was limited to past acquisition of training data, not to whether the trained model is itself an infringing copy of the works it learned from (Norton Rose Fulbright). A verdict either way is the most consequential number in AI law this quarter, and it can land on any day now.

The settlements so far bought peace on how training data was obtained; this jury is being asked whether the weights themselves infringe — a plaintiff verdict reopens every deal that was priced as if that question were closed.
