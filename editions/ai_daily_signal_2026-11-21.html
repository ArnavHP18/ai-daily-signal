#2Research · mathematics · 20 NovIMPACT 8/10

Anthropic mathematician says Claude helped him prove the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge released a machine-checkable proof that experts have only begun to check; a separate human proof appeared the same day.

In a two-page note posted on 20 Nov, Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, writes: 'I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis', adding that the method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) and that 'Claude and I have also formalized the arguments without assumptions' in Lean, a language computers use to check proofs. The BBC says the work took about six weeks with a non-public Claude model; Scott Aaronson puts the Lean code at 17.4 million lines (the BBC says over 250,000), released as 14 archive files. Terence Tao calls it an 'enormous' Lean-only proof whose strategy 'appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program' and says digesting it 'will take some time'. Hours later, Stanford's Ravi Vakil posted a separate, all-human proposed GRH proof by Alessandro Masullo, which Brian Conrad has been checking for months. Tao also lists AI-assisted, Lean-checked proofs that the 3D Euler and Navier-Stokes equations can blow up in finite time, by Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei Coiculescu.

So what: if it holds, an AI system has helped settle the most famous open problem in mathematics; until the Lean files are independently built and the statement checked, treat it as a strong claim, not a result.

#9

Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini loops its layers to reason better on a laptop

NOTABLEOfficial sourceModels · open weights · 20 Nov

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini with open weights. It is a looped transformer: it runs its middle layers three times for every token, so it can 'reason longer without writing its reasoning down'. It has 26.4B parameters, 3.4B active (5.4B counting the loops), is trained mostly in NVFP4 and needs about 20GB, so it runs offline on a MacBook. The company says it leads open models under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%); Artificial Analysis says it matches Qwen3.8 27B with about 5x fewer active parameters. It trails Qwen3.8-27B on multi-turn chat. A variable-loop research version is released too, because hidden looping can make a model's reasoning harder to monitor.

So what: a frontier-lab, laptop-sized coding model with open weights is a realistic option for banks that must keep code and data on-premises.

#10

Architect Labs says its AI agents designed a new kind of CPU from scratch

NOTABLEOfficial sourceHardware · AI-designed chips · 20 Nov

#11

Researchers show a 27B model improving itself by editing its own weights

NOTABLEOfficial sourceResearch · self-improvement · 18 Nov

A paper on arXiv claims, 'to our knowledge for the first time', that a language model can rewrite its own weights by its own reasoning and get better, with no gradients and no training. An open 27B model, given its weights, graded answers and the ability to run itself, diagnoses its failures and edits the weights: it gains 6.6 points on problems it never saw, keeps improving over eight sessions, and restores a broken copy of itself to over 80% of the original accuracy. Smaller models in the same family either make no edit or break themselves.

So what: a model that changes its own weights is harder to audit; model-risk teams should ask vendors whether deployed weights can change after validation.
