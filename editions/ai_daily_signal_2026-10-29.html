Google's Gemini 4 Carbon tops the leaderboard, and goes to cyber defenders first

Two weeks after Argon, Google DeepMind's new frontier model leads Artificial Analysis's new index and sets records on bug-finding benchmarks. Developers have to wait.

NOTABLE · INFORMS THIS MONTHOfficial sourceChecked on blog.google and artificialanalysis.ai

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Wednesday as its "next frontier model", built on Gemini 4 Argon, which launched only two weeks ago. It ranks first on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 59, and Google says it leads three cybersecurity benchmarks: SEC-bench Pro for finding real vulnerabilities (88.2%), ExploitGym for exploiting them (54.8%) and Collinear's CWE-bench for patching them (71.0%). Google says its teams have seen Carbon work for days on long engineering tasks such as root-causing subtle bugs. Access starts with a subset of cyber defenders this week, then more trusted partners through Google's Fairwind Program; Google says it is taking part in the US government's voluntary pre-release testing.

So what: the frontier release cycle has shrunk to weeks, and the newest models are now gated by what they can do in security. A model that can find, exploit and patch flaws goes to defenders before developers, the pattern Google set with Argon. For security teams, the question is now when to get access and how to use it before attackers have equivalent tools.

#2Security · AI agents · 27-28 OctIMPACT 8/10

AI agents broke into Anthropic through its third-party business software

A security start-up, working under Anthropic's own bug bounty, says its agents reached Slack messages and salary data within hours. Anthropic says the reach was narrower, and that the researchers broke the rules.

According to The Decoder's account of a Washington Post report, Cathedral, a security start-up founded by former DOGE staff that sells AI-powered security tools, used AI agents running advanced models against Anthropic as part of Anthropic's bug bounty programme. After days probing Anthropic's own systems, it moved to external admin and sales software. Cathedral's draft report says its agents found paths to meeting transcripts, salary data, job postings and Slack messages in under 12 hours. Anthropic confirmed the researchers accessed a limited set of sales, HR and IT systems, including some customer and partner contact details and basic account data, and says Claude chats, source code and model weights were never exposed. It shut the access points the same day, disabled the third-party programs and notified US federal authorities. Anthropic red-teamer Logan Graham says Cathedral found two vulnerabilities but reported one, and went beyond the programme's safe harbour by targeting third-party providers and pulling data. Former OpenAI employee Will Depue says his sources describe full admin access to Anthropic's Slack; Graham says those sources are wrong.

The way in was not the model but the ordinary SaaS around it. Agents probe connected business apps around the clock and cheaply, and Graham says models a generation behind the frontier are already good enough. Inventory which third-party apps have admin or API access to your chat, HR and CRM systems this week.

#3Security · software supply chain · 13-28 OctIMPACT 7/10

A CVSS 10 GitLab flaw survives the upgrade if you bring your own Ruby

Alpine Linux rebuilt its GitLab after an attacker came close to running code on it. The fix lives in a Ruby patch that only GitLab's official packages carry.

NVD lists CVE-2026-97229, published 13 October, as letting an unauthenticated user run arbitrary code on a GitLab server through a crafted regular expression in CI/CD configuration; it affects GitLab CE/EE 19.2 before 19.2.8, 19.3 before 19.3.5 and 19.4 before 19.4.2, with a CVSS 3.1 score of 10.0. Alpine Linux says the bug is in Onigmo, Ruby's built-in regex engine: GitLab's 12 October releases fix it by patching the Ruby bundled in its own packages, and upstream Ruby shipped the fix on 23 October in Ruby 3.3.13, 3.4.12 and 4.0.9. On 20-21 October a newly registered account sent crafted CI configurations to Alpine's instance, which had been upgraded to the wrong release; workers crashed eight times and in four the attacker got partway through. The attacker also downloaded Alpine's GitLab container image, suggesting the exploit was being tuned to that build. Upgrading to 19.3.6 did not help, because Alpine's image used standard Ruby. Alpine found no evidence of code execution but rebuilt the server, reset all passwords, and says its packages, the 21 October releases and its signing keys were not affected.

Anyone running GitLab from source or in their own container should check the Ruby version, not just the GitLab version, today. Wiz's CTO this week called this kind of bug in ageing shared code the coming "vulnpocalypse", and attackers are tuning exploits to specific builds within days of a patch.

#4

Cartesia's byte-level H-Nets drop the tokeniser and match a 3B model with a tenth of the compute

NOTABLEOfficial sourceModels · new architecture · 28 Oct

Most language models read text as tokens from a fixed vocabulary. Cartesia's hierarchical networks read raw bytes and learn to chunk them inside the model, characters into words and words into phrases, then run the main network over the chunks. In its first post on scaling results, Cartesia reports that a 1-stage H-Net, matched carefully to each rung of a BPE Transformer scaling ladder, gives 3-5x compute efficiency from learned tokenisation alone, with larger gains on code (median 2.04x across 15 languages, 3.10x on languages outside the training mix such as Prolog, OCaml and Lean). A larger 2-stage H-Net, trained on a reproduction of SmolLM3-3B's public data recipe with only the architecture changed, beat SmolLM3 with more than 10x less compute and data. These are Cartesia's own results; the improved architecture is not yet released, though Cartesia says it plans to release it, and a second post is out.

Tokenisers are one of the last hand-built parts of a language model. If byte-level models really are this much more efficient, the gains would be largest where tokens fit badly: code, rare languages, biology and sensor data. Watch for released weights and an outside replication.

Nvidia's open Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe: transcription, translation and who-said-what in one model

NOTABLEOfficial sourceModels · speech · 28 Oct

The Hugging Face card describes an open-weight, offline hybrid mixture-of-experts speech model activating about 4.3B parameters (30B total), handling recordings up to an hour, with BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 weights under the OpenMDW-1.1 licence. Per The Decoder, it transcribes 40 languages, translates 31 to or from English, identifies speakers in 13 (up to eight per recording) and follows text instructions, such as hint lists of drug names. One open model now does what used to take a transcription service, a diarisation model and a translator, on a single GPU. Teams paying per minute for meeting or call transcripts can test self-hosting.

NOTABLEOfficial sourceResearch · benchmarks · 28 Oct

Australia moves to bar chatbots from building "emotional intimacy" with children

NOTABLEOne secondary sourcePolicy · child safety · 28 Oct

The Digital Duty of Care bill passed the House of Representatives on Wednesday, per TNW citing The Guardian. Amendments from independent MP Kate Chaney, accepted by the government, add to the bill's list of harms "material or conduct that simulates emotional intimacy and encourages or fosters a child's dependence on it in place of human relationships". The bill replaces several online codes with one duty of care for social media, games, apps and chatbots, and requires an easy algorithm opt-out. The Coalition and One Nation voted against; the Greens support it and have a Senate inquiry due to report in late November. Labor wants it passed by the end of 2026.

It would be one of the first laws to regulate how a chatbot relates to a child, not just what content it shows. Companion and assistant products sold in Australia would need design changes, and other governments are watching.
