Euro area picks Spain's Hernández de Cos to succeed Lagarde at the ECB

Boone and Brunnermeier join the board package; EU leaders are expected to make it formal in December

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceChecked on António Costa's own post (opened via its public feed); Xinhua and JBizNews (opened) report the Dutch switch and the December timeline

European Council President António Costa said on 26 Nov that euro area member states have agreed to recommend Pablo Hernández de Cos as President of the ECB, and Laurence Boone (France) and Markus Brunnermeier (Germany) as members of its Executive Board. Costa will propose that the European Council consult the European Parliament and the ECB's Governing Council, 'paving the way for the formal appointment of the new ECB leadership' at the December summit. The Netherlands switched its backing from former Dutch central bank governor Klaas Knot, clearing the main obstacle.

Hernández de Cos has run the Bank for International Settlements since July 2025, after six years as Bank of Spain governor and on the ECB Governing Council, and chaired the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision from 2019 to 2024. Christine Lagarde has said she will step down by early February 2027.

So what: the euro area's next central banker is the official who led global bank-capital standards through the Basel III endgame; expect continuity on supervision and capital, and watch his first comments on rates, the digital euro and stablecoins.

MONITORTwo or more outletsWorld · US weather · 26 Nov

A powerful storm hit the eastern US on Thanksgiving with winds up to 70mph, torrential rain and major coastal flooding. More than 920,000 households were without power on Thursday evening (PowerOutage.us), including about 226,000 in Pennsylvania, 170,000 in Maryland and 145,000 in Virginia. So what: Black Friday store traffic in the north-east starts on the back foot; utilities' storm costs follow.

UK-EU reset treaties published, with a costly 10-year exit clause

MONITOROne secondary sourceWorld · UK-EU · 26 Nov

The government and the EU published two of the three legal texts agreed last week, covering food exports (an SPS deal) and linking emissions trading schemes, with a cost-cutting bill to enact them and an impact assessment. The food deal has a 10-year termination clause: a party that ends it must pay 'reasonable and proportionate costs' of rebuilding border-check infrastructure. So what: exporters get lower friction from next year, but the exit clause turns the reset into an election issue.
