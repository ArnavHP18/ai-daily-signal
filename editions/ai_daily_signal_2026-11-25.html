#3Security · firewalls · 23 NovIMPACT 8/10

Attackers using AI tools broke into Check Point firewall management servers at 22 firms

Threat-intelligence firm GreyNoise says an unattributed actor it calls GT13 broke into Check Point Security Management Servers, which control firewall policy, at at least 22 organisations in 17 countries (UK, Ireland, France, Germany, India, South Korea, Hong Kong, Australia, the US and others) between 17 Oct and 2 Nov. Victims include financial services, energy, government and transport. The actor used 99 IP addresses and a compromised relay server that held data stolen from 24 management servers, and tried exploits including CVE-2026-16232 (Check Point SmartConsole authentication bypass, patched in July), Citrix NetScaler, Ivanti and Windows NetLogon flaws. GreyNoise says it saw hands-on-keyboard activity but believes the actor used LLMs and agents for scanning, exploitation and tool-building, and that the campaign is ongoing. It is notifying victims through CERTs.

So what: whoever owns a firewall's management server owns the firewall; banks running Check Point should confirm the July hotfix is on every management, log and SmartEvent server and hunt for the indicators GreyNoise published.

#4

OpenAI says GPT-6 can design working proteins without specialised biology models

NOTABLEOfficial sourceResearch · AI for science · 24 Nov

OpenAI published a preprint, "GPT-6 as a Universal Foundation Model for Protein Biology", saying its GPT-6 family can design new protein shapes and amino-acid sequences by reasoning and writing code, without calling specialised tools such as RFdiffusion or ProteinMPNN. An internal research model matched or beat specialised models on many design tasks. In lab tests with partner Onava, at least one small protein based on GPT-6.2's 3D designs showed binding for each of five targets, and many redesigned minibinders and antibodies kept binding. OpenAI is candid about limits: its hit rates were below the original RFdiffusion study, and specialised models (Boltz-2, ESMFold2) supplied sequences and filtered candidates, so the work "does not yet establish" a fully general-model pipeline.

So what: general models are starting to replace the specialist tools in drug discovery; biotech lenders and pharma investors should watch whether design costs and timelines actually fall.

Free coding agent found a financial firm's access key in under six minutes

NOTABLEOne secondary sourceSecurity · AI attacks · 24 Nov

Tel Aviv security startup Jesta told The Register that on 16 Nov someone in Bucharest pointed OpenCode, a free open-source coding agent that works with 200+ model providers, at a major financial-services customer's website. Using only the agent, the Mac's built-in curl and Chrome, it tried several ways in and found an access key (a planted decoy) in under six minutes, at no cost to the operator. #10

South Korea's draft law hands won stablecoins to bank-led groups

NOTABLEOne secondary sourceCrypto · stablecoins · 24 Nov

South Korea's Financial Services Commission published a draft bill on Tuesday under which won-denominated stablecoins could at first be issued only by groups in which banks collectively own more than half, though a fintech or tech firm could be the largest single shareholder. Issuers would need FSC authorisation, at least 5bn won (about $3.7m) of capital, 100% reserves and redemption at face value without delay. Crypto exchanges would need dealing, brokerage and custody licences, and any shareholder would be capped at 20% (34% with approval). The bill would also lift the 2017 ban on domestic coin offerings. It settles a long fight: the central bank wanted banks in control; the FSC had favoured non-banks too. A Korean report says the won-stablecoin rollout may slip to 2028.

So what: another major economy is putting stablecoins inside the banking system rather than beside it; Korean banks become the gatekeepers, and foreign issuers face a bank-partnered route.
