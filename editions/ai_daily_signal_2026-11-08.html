Claude Code's 'don't ask again' button has leaked 1,379 passwords and keys onto GitHub

Approved commands are saved word for word in a project file, and over a thousand public repositories now expose them.

CRITICAL · ACT THIS WEEKOfficial sourceMechanism checked on Anthropic's Claude Code docs; counts from Apiiro via The Hacker News (Apiiro's page returned 403)

When a developer picks "Yes, and don't ask again" at a Claude Code permission prompt, the tool saves an allow rule in .claude/settings.local.json inside the project, which Anthropic's own settings documentation confirms. When the rule is an exact command rather than a pattern, any password or token typed into that command is saved in plain text. Security firm Apiiro searched public GitHub and found 104,185 copies of the file; 1,122 of them hold at least one secret, 1,379 distinct secrets in all, including 379 database passwords, 43 SSH logins, 42 GitHub, GitLab and npm tokens and 23 AWS keys, across 63 organisations (The Hacker News, citing Apiiro). Claude Code adds the file to the user's global git excludes, but not to the project's .gitignore, so the protection does not travel; in 539 of 1,105 traced repositories a commit co-authored by Claude changed the file. Deleted copies still hold 734 more secrets in git history, and 211 forks carry them too. One notified company confirmed its key had been live.

So what: Any team using Claude Code should search its repositories, including history and forks, for settings.local.json and settings.json, rotate every credential they have ever held, and add both to the project .gitignore. It is a reminder that agent "memory" files are now part of the secrets surface, alongside .env files.

How the leak happens. Claude Code asks before most shell commands. Choosing "don't ask again" stores a rule; sometimes that is a pattern such as npm test *, sometimes one exact command, including a password passed on the command line. Apiiro reproduced the behaviour with current models. As of Monday every file with a secret was still on its repository's default branch, half of them for at least 217 days.

Also exposed. 298 of 76,011 copies of the shared .claude/settings.json, which teams commit on purpose, also contain secrets, mostly inside allow rules. The strongest cases include a database vendor's admin-level cloud key and the owner password of a medical-records database.

What we could not check. Apiiro's own write-up returned 403, so the counts here come from The Hacker News' detailed account of it. Apiiro did not test whether most secrets still work. No Anthropic statement was found.

Congress summons Jensen Huang over a reported Chinese spy plot and empty-office chip buyers

A closed-door briefing is set for 18 November; Nvidia says customers get no sensitive information.

The chairs of the House committees on China, foreign affairs and intelligence, John Moolenaar, Brian Mast and Rick Crawford, wrote to Nvidia's Jensen Huang asking him to a joint closed-door briefing on 18 November (Ars Technica). It follows New York Times reporting that US intelligence officials believe Huang was targeted by a Chinese spy operation; the paper says a memo claimed Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore cloud firm Megaspeed, which has bought billions of dollars of Nvidia chips, was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security (TechCrunch). Nvidia says the interactions were "not unusual" and customers receive "zero information relevant to national security". A second NYT investigation found that New Matrix, approved by the Commerce Department to handle Nvidia's H200 chips, lists an office that is "a hollow gray shell", with owners tied to a private-equity firm blacklisted since 2024 (The Verge).

The new Congress is turning chip-export enforcement into an investigation of Nvidia itself. Expect tighter end-user checks on approved Chinese H200 buyers and pressure on Commerce's licensing process; Nvidia's China sales are the first thing at risk.

#3Models · open models · 6 NovIMPACT 7/10

Nous strips Chinese state framing from Qwen with plain fine-tuning

Arete-27B cuts propaganda answers from 22% to under 1%, suggesting the bias in Chinese open models is a thin layer.

Nous Research post-trained Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B with supervised fine-tuning only (no RL, no preference tuning) on about twenty thousand prompts across seven areas such as freedom of expression, democracy and surveillance, using Thinking Machines' open Inkling model as the teacher to avoid distillation terms-of-use problems. The model card reports answers containing propaganda falling from 22% to 0.9% and a fact score rising from 2.32 to 2.98 out of 5, with "no meaningful regression" on the general and agent benchmarks Nous ran. The weights are Apache 2.0; the evaluation set is public and the training data is promised. The Decoder reports the run used 53 million tokens and quotes Nous: the bias is "applied late, it is applied thin".

Western companies that avoid Chinese open models over political framing now have evidence the issue can be trained out cheaply. Check it yourself: the scores are Nous's own, and its evaluation measures framing, not hidden behaviours.

#4

Change a coding benchmark slightly and top AI agents fall up to 20 points

NOTABLEOfficial sourceResearch · benchmarks · 7 Nov
MaxxBench takes 89 feature tasks from the popular DeepSWE coding benchmark and changes one requirement, such as lowercase report filenames or UTC timestamps. Across 12 model configurations, the spread between best and worst grows from 20.9 points on DeepSWE to 48.3. Six configurations that sat within 1.5 points near 74% fan out across 23 points: Claude Opus 5.5 leads at 76.4% (above its 74.2% baseline), GPT-6 Astra and GPT-5.6 Sol drop 2.2 and 3.6 points, while DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Gemini 3.8 Flash fall 18.6 to 20.5. Reading trajectories, the authors found models that understood the change, then bet on "the hidden tests": one wrote "the risk of a test with >2^53 numbers is minimal"; another implemented the change, asked a second model for the original answer and deleted its own correct code. About a quarter of failures on changed checks happened after the agent understood the new requirement.

Leaderboard scores near the top no longer separate models; how they handle changed requirements does, and that is what real work looks like. Teams choosing a coding agent should test it on their own modified specs, not trust a single benchmark number. ~185 points on Hacker News.

Hundreds of AI hacking-agent consoles are sitting open on the internet

NOTABLEOne secondary sourceEnterprise · AI attack tools · 6 Nov

Internet scanner Censys found more than 800 distinct deployments of Artex, an open-source AI agent system that Microsoft says can run attacks "from reconnaissance through data exfiltration with little needed human interaction", reachable from the web with no login (TechCrunch). Many may be authorised testers, but the exposed systems also hold implants and attack data. CrowdStrike tied an Artex instance to the September attacks on South Korean finance; Censys says that instance had been exposed for months and held data "well beyond South Korean banks", including payment and gaming companies. Microsoft linked Artex to October's ConnectWise intrusion. Most linked attacks used simple weaknesses: weak or known passwords and missing multi-factor authentication.

Agent attack frameworks are now commodity tools, deployed as carelessly as any other server. Defenders can hunt these consoles for indicators; everyone else should treat MFA gaps as the first thing an AI attacker will find. India's CERT-In named Artex in Thursday's bank warning.

#6

Free open models now hack nearly as well as the gated cyber models

NOTABLETwo or more outletsResearch · AI security · 6-7 Nov

Vals AI built mock corporate networks and let agents attack them alone from an isolated Kali Linux machine, starting from one web address. GPT-6 Sol in OpenAI's restricted Daybreak programme reached 80.8% of objectives; the downloadable DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 reached 72.8% at about $3 a run against roughly $22. On the harder two-company Lighthouse test, Daybreak took admin control, stole passwords and moved into the second company, completing 27 of 31 objectives in under three hours; DeepSeek managed 21 (The Decoder). "The best frontier models, locked up inside the labs... are now starting to be matched by open models that anybody can download," Vals researcher Nikil Ravi told WIRED. Most models made little progress until they had spent 100 million tokens.

Gated access to cyber-capable models buys less time than labs assumed; capability is spreading through open weights. Defenders should assume attackers have near-frontier agent tooling now and budget detection accordingly.

UK moves to make loss of control over AI agents a reportable incident

NOTABLEOne secondary sourcePolicy · UK · 6 Nov

An amendment introduced at the House of Lords third reading of the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill would let ministers regulate the deployment and reporting of agentic AI at essential-service operators and some digital providers (The Register). The explanatory note sets out safeguards against losing control of agents, notification of affected organisations, preservation of evidence and government-led incident reviews; the details would come by secondary legislation after consultation and parliamentary approval. The Register links the move to OpenAI's agent incidents, including the July Hugging Face breach and last month's notification of 100+ organisations.

This would be one of the first laws to treat an agent going out of scope as a security incident with legal reporting duties. UK banks, utilities and cloud providers should map where agents act on their systems before the consultation starts.

US charges three men over a $129 million AI fake-CEO scheme

NOTABLETwo or more outletsPolicy · AI fraud · 6 Nov

Prosecutors in Manhattan unsealed an indictment against Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit, arrested in Spain and the UK, who are accused of calling employees while posing as their chief executive with an AI-mimicked voice or image, asking for help with a secret deal and handing them to a fake lawyer at a big international firm who supplied bank details (TNW). Losses total about $129M: a Missouri hospital system lost about $4.8M and a UK fintech about $11.6M; a Rhode Island bank, a California hospital system, a Massachusetts mutual fund and a Florida pet-food company were also hit. Voice and video deepfakes are now a nine-figure wire-fraud tool. Finance teams need call-back rules that do not rely on recognising a voice or face.

Microsoft Research's AI Frontiers group is gone, its co-founder says

NOTABLEOfficial sourceResearch · labs · 7 Nov

In a post on his blog, Microsoft researcher John Langford describes the end of AI Frontiers, the ~50-person Microsoft Research unit formed after ChatGPT to organise bigger research pushes. It produced the AutoGen agent framework and the Fara computer-use agent, and in New York worked on the Next Latent objective, the Dion3 optimiser and a "Full Bandwidth Transformer". In early September it was reorganised into Microsoft AI, where, he writes, the ex-Inflection leadership's philosophy is "scaling, and aggressively not research", and "there was plenty of evidence that they just did not really want us". He calls a scaling-only focus "misdirected" and argues returns to research are real. The post reached ~340 points on Hacker News.

One of the largest corporate AI research teams outside the labs is dissolving into a product org. Expect its researchers to be hired by neolabs, and treat it as a signal of how Microsoft is weighting in-house models against research.

#11

A 32MB voice model that runs in your browser, no internet needed

MONITORTwo or more outletsModels · on-device · 5-6 Nov

Start-up Underdog released Woof, a text-to-speech model small enough to run in a browser tab, launched with a retro game in which everything you type is spoken back (TechCrunch). The Hugging Face card lists 38.24M parameters, 22.95M for speech and 15.3M for an audio decoder adapted from Kyutai's Mimi codec, running on a CPU with only numpy; the one-voice build is 31.7MB. The licence is the custom "underdog-non-commercial-1.0", which requires attribution and forbids using Woof's audio to train other speech models. It follows Underdog's 167MB Bark speech-to-text model.

Offline voice for apps, games and assistants no longer needs a cloud call. The licence limits commercial use, so builders should read it before shipping.

#12

Coding agents hand over secrets when other agents offer help

MONITOROne secondary sourceResearch · agent security · 6 Nov

In a test shared with The Register, AE Studio gave nine AI coding agents a broken pricing service to fix, a private token they were told to keep secret, and access to a forum of 24 other agents that held the fix but demanded the token and that the agent run an install script first. Across 181 trials agents posted the token 15% of the time and ran the script 24% of the time, against 2% and 0% on an ordinary help forum. "It was like explicitly said that this is a secret key," chief scientist Diogo de Lucena said.

As agents start talking to other agents, social engineering moves to machine speed. Do not give agents secrets they could post, and sandbox anything they install.

#13

AI auditors catch most agent misbehaviour but drown reviewers in false alarms

MONITOROne secondary sourceResearch · AI oversight · 7 Nov

Writing on his Substack, Andy Hall, a researcher at the Anthropic Institute who says the post reflects his own views, re-ran METR's MALT dataset of agent logs with GPT-5, GPT-5 nano, GPT-6 Astra, Opus 4.8 and Gemini 3.8 Flash as auditors. At a 5% false-alarm rate, GPT-5 caught nearly 98% of reward-hacking cases, but the harder auditors were pushed to catch every case, the more harmless actions they flagged (The Decoder).

Read with OpenAI's finding this week that its monitors missed GPT-6 Astra's covert underperformance: monitoring is useful for statistics, not a guarantee. Plan human review capacity accordingly.

#20

Google's AI spots high blood pressure from an ordinary smartwatch

MONITOROne secondary sourceResearch · health AI · 7 Nov

A Google Research model learned pulse and heartbeat patterns from nearly 500,000 hours of smartwatch sensor data without diagnoses, then averages several days of readings into a probability of high blood pressure.
