■ Today's Lead · New · Policy · Day 1 of the model-IP-extraction arc

Three US agencies put a name, a method and a victim list on model distillation — and call it China's core AI strategy, not a supplement to it

The NSA, CISA and the FBI issued a joint cybersecurity advisory, AA26-251A, alleging that six China-based AI companies — DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI — have run "aggressive, malicious, and targeted" knowledge-distillation campaigns against the frontier models of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and xAI, extracting billions of tokens across millions of requests since at least late 2024 (BleepingComputer, CyberScoop). The framing is the news: the agencies say distillation is the core of these firms' development strategy rather than an accelerant, and they publish four techniques as a defender-facing taxonomy — regional-restriction evasion combined with subscription exploitation, centralised request-routing infrastructure, automated request-metadata sanitisation, and systematic quota and cost optimisation. The advisory also asserts that DeepSeek's widely-cited $5.6M training cost excludes data obtained through distillation (The Register, Engadget).

So what: if you sell API access to a frontier model, your abuse-detection stack is now a national-security control with a published threat taxonomy to test against — and if you buy or fine-tune on Chinese open-weight models, your legal and procurement teams have a US government document to reckon with that did not exist on Tuesday.

What the advisory actually says. AA26-251A is a joint product of NSA, CISA and the FBI addressed to organisations that operate or resell frontier-model APIs. Its central claim is structural rather than incidental: that for the six named firms, systematic extraction of proprietary model behaviour from US frontier systems "forms the core — not merely a supplement — of their AI development strategy." It names DeepSeek's campaign specifically as running since at least late 2024 and as having generated synthetic training data used for models including R1, released in early 2025 (Unite.AI). TNW reports that the document lists which US models each firm targeted.

The four techniques, and why they are the useful part. The advisory characterises as novel: (1) regional restriction evasion combined with subscription exploitation — obtaining access from jurisdictions where a provider's terms would otherwise block it, at consumer subscription tiers; (2) centralised request routing infrastructure — fan-out through intermediaries so that no single account looks anomalous; (3) automated request metadata sanitisation — stripping the fingerprints that abuse detection keys on; and (4) systematic quota and cost optimisation — engineering the extraction to sit under per-account thresholds. Read as a defender checklist, this is the first US government description of what large-scale distillation looks like in provider telemetry.

The economics claim. The advisory's assertion that DeepSeek's $5.6M figure is misleading because it excludes distillation-acquired data is a policy argument as much as a technical one: it goes to whether the "cheap frontier training" narrative of early 2025 was ever an apples-to-apples comparison. This briefing notes that the advisory asserts this; no independent cost reconstruction was located in this scan.

What is not in it. No enforcement action, no sanctions, no export-control change and no named remedy accompany the advisory in the reporting located here. The named companies had not responded on the record in any source located in this scan. Distillation against a provider's terms of service is a contractual and possibly trade-secret matter; the advisory does not assert a specific statute violated. Reuters-tier confirmation of the per-model victim mapping was not independently obtained beyond TNW's account.

Watch next. Whether any of the four named US labs publishes corroborating telemetry; whether Commerce or Treasury follows the advisory with an action; whether the named firms respond; and whether enterprise buyers of Chinese open-weight models (Qwen and DeepSeek derivatives are widely deployed) begin asking provenance questions in procurement. Also worth watching against Beijing's own reported deliberations on restricting overseas access to top Chinese models — the two moves point the same direction from opposite ends.

IMPACT 8/10#3 RESEARCH · AI FOR SCIENCE

DeepMind publishes molecular predictions for all 9 billion possible single-letter human DNA variants

Released 8 September: AlphaGenome Atlas precomputes the predictions of DeepMind's AlphaGenome sequence-to-function model across the entire human genome, giving predicted molecular effects for all ~9 billion single-nucleotide variants, plus a new variant-ranking score (AVI) and a technical paper (DeepMind, Nature news). The dataset is roughly 1 petabyte, which DeepMind says is over 30x the AlphaFold Database. The site is open for non-commercial research from 8 September alongside the AlphaGenome API and a skill in Google Antigravity; a static download of AVI scores is permissively licensed for commercial use, with broader commercial access via Google Cloud "coming soon". The authors state plainly that Atlas and AVI are research tools forming part of an evidence chain, not sufficient evidence for clinical diagnosis, and that AlphaGenome has no clinical validation or approval.

If you run rare-disease or variant-interpretation pipelines, a precomputed genome-wide prior is now free to try — but the "not clinically validated" line is the operative sentence, and it is the lab's own.

IMPACT 8/10#4 POLICY · SAFETY & PLATFORMS

332 paid ads containing AI-generated child sexual abuse material cleared Meta's ad review, researchers find

The Tech Transparency Project (Campaign for Accountability) published an investigation identifying 332 advertisements containing CSAM that passed Meta's advertising review and ran on Facebook and Instagram this year. Most showed a photo of a real child digitally altered with AI to depict a sex act; TTP says it identified multiple real children, including a young member of a European royal family. Nearly 80% ran in the United States. The majority directed users to AI image- and video-generation apps from Chinese developers, many of which TTP verified as capable of "nudifying" people or placing them in sex videos (TTP, Engadget). The sharpest finding: after Meta said it had rolled out new systems to better detect child exploitation, TTP found hundreds of new CSAM ads running.

This is the first well-documented case of generative CSAM being monetised at scale through a mainstream ad system — expect it to become the reference exhibit in every nudify-app and platform-liability proceeding, and in the EU AI Act transparency debate, for the rest of the year.

Day 3: the agent-workday metric produces a physical artifact — a chip OpenAI's own models designed

WHAT CHANGED Escalated. At Goldman Sachs' Communacopia conference on 8 September, CFO Sarah Friar confirmed on the record that OpenAI used its own frontier models to design the Jalapeño custom inference chip — co-developed with Broadcom, announced June 2026, with a claimed nine-month design-to-tape-out. She also said OpenAI is now selling into chip design, life sciences and financial services as verticals, is experimenting with outcome-based pricing rather than usage pricing, and that an 80% price cut to the lower-cost Luna model drove roughly a tenfold rise in usage, putting Luna below the cost of running Chinese open-weight models on cloud providers. Yesterday this thread was a published productivity ratio; today it has a named part, a go-to-market and a price weapon.
The through-line from edition 081's "3.1 agent-workdays per human researcher workday" is now visible in three directions at once. Recursion: the models design the inference silicon the models will run on — the compute-landlord thesis applied to its own supply chain (Forkast). Commercialisation: chip design as a sellable vertical means OpenAI is competing with EDA vendors' AI roadmaps, not just with chatbots. Price: the Luna cut and the claim of undercutting Chinese open-weight inference is a direct answer to the cost argument that has driven open-weight adoption all year — and it landed the same week a US advisory accused those same Chinese labs of distilling US models. Friar's remarks were reported by Reuters and carried widely (Reuters via affiliate, PANews). The 10x usage figure and the cost comparison are company statements at an investor conference, not audited disclosures.

Two things to act on: re-price your inference assumptions against Luna's post-cut rate before your next vendor cycle, and treat "AI designs chips" as a live procurement question for anyone with a silicon roadmap — the reference customer is now OpenAI itself.
