■ Today's Lead · New on the Wire · Broke Mon 12 Oct · Day 17 of the frontier-pacing arc

Meta releases Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, its first models to sit near the frontier, but only the smaller one is open to developers

On Monday Meta released two models from Meta Superintelligence Labs. Muse Wonder is available through the API. Muse Wonder Full, the stronger one, is listed only inside Meta's own Muse Code product. On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, Wonder Full scores 56: level with Claude Sonnet 5.5, behind Opus 5.5 (58), and ahead of GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon and Fable 5.1 (53 each). Wonder scores 52. Meta's own charts show its biggest leads on long agentic coding and legal work. It trails Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (VentureBeat · ProPakistani · BenchLM).

So what: a fourth lab is now within two points of the top of an independent index. Meta is keeping its best checkpoint inside its own coding product, so developers get Wonder and Meta's products get Wonder Full. That reverses Meta's open-weights strategy of the Llama years.

IMPACT 6/10#2 HARDWARE · CUSTOM SILICON · INFERENCE · BROKE MON 12 OCT

Microsoft unveils Maia 300, its third-generation AI chip, built for inference, and says 2027 deployment could exceed 1 million units

A multi-chiplet design: the compute chiplet is on TSMC 2nm, the data-movement chiplets on 3nm, with 288 GB of HBM4 at 18 TB/s per accelerator. Microsoft claims about 40% more useful compute per megawatt than leading GPU systems (no named comparison) and up to 7x Maia 200's rack-scale token throughput. Racks hold 96 chips at ~167 kW (vs 142 kW for a 72-GPU GB300 rack). The chips mainly serve Microsoft's own AI services, and Nvidia and AMD still handle training (Quartz · SBS · Rallies · TheStreet (Reuters)).

The million-unit figure is "potentially": earlier reporting had TSMC capacity talks for ~300,000 units. Even so, hyperscalers are now moving inference, the larger and growing workload, onto their own chips.

IMPACT 5/10#3 MODELS · EMERGING TECH · DECISION MODELS · SPARSE ATTENTION · BROKE MON 12 OCT

Subquadratic launches SubQ Snap, a multimodal decision model with a 384K-token context (12x Jev's), built on its sparse-attention architecture

Snap targets structured steps in enterprise agent workflows, such as routing, filtering and scoring over long, document-heavy inputs. It uses Subquadratic Sparse Attention (SSA), which computes only the token relationships it judges relevant. On Subquadratic's own run of the Decision Index public suite it scores 61.7 vs Jev's 57.9. Access is limited early access, with 100M free tokens for the first 10,000 users. Subquadratic launched in May with SubQ, a 12M-token LLM; one report said it reused open Qwen weights (press release · subq.ai · FourWeekMBA · SiliconANGLE (May)).

Watchlist movement. Until now decision models (Jev, Clef, Microsoft-Decision-1) worked on short inputs. Snap is the first to aim at whole case files and long agent traces. Benchmarks are vendor-only; price is not yet published.

IMPACT 5/10#4 RESEARCH · OPEN MODELS DEEP BEAT · AGENT RL · ANNOUNCED 11–12 OCT

Alibaba's Qwen team details QwenGyre, an "elastic" RL framework that trains coding agents on rollouts lasting hours and approaching 1M tokens, through unmodified black-box harnesses

An elastic scheduler shifts GPUs between rollout and training based on unfinished rollout work, while keeping in-flight agent calls served. A trajectory processor rebuilds branching histories, scores partial progress and removes duplicate paths. On the flagship Qwen 3.8 (2.4T) model the authors report NL2RepoBench 52.5% → 58.5% in 48 steps, with 1.85x and 1.78x speed-ups over colocated and async baselines. The paper does not say the code will be released (arXiv 2609.33848 · MarkTechPost · FourWeekMBA).

Training on real harnesses with hours-long runs, instead of short synthetic tasks, is how labs are pushing agent stamina. Paired with Xiaomi's open RL environments (Tracking), the open-model ecosystem now has published recipes, if not code.

IMPACT 6/10D1 RESEARCH · AI FOR SCIENCE · AUTOMATED MATHEMATICS

OpenAI's 722-manuscript math release meets its first scrutiny: three papers withdrawn after one sign error, 14 repaired, and a public backlash from mathematicians

WHAT CHANGED Tracking said "no mathematician verification surfaced". In fact, on 7 Oct, a day after release, OpenAI withdrew three manuscripts and repaired 14. The 8–12 Oct scans missed this. Since then: the catalogue lists 719 manuscripts, ~42% of top-line results have machine-checked Lean proofs, and organised criticism has followed. Promoted from Tracking; Monitor → Notable.
A sign error (+1 where OpenAI's own conventions required −1) broke the argument in "Algebraicity of Weil classes on split abelian eightfolds" and the two Hodge-conjecture papers built on it. Cornell's Alex Townsend expects more errors. The Association for Human Mathematics called the release "not a demonstration of scholarship" but "a demonstration of power"; Terence Tao reportedly endorsed the statement. Clay Institute president Martin Bridson reportedly called it "breathtaking". A preprint finds that parts of the Lean code for the Navier-Stokes blow-up claim differ from the written paper (Retraction Watch · Implicator · Futurism · Scientific American).

Lean checks the logic of a proof, not whether a written paper matches its formal code, or whether a result matters. The bottleneck in AI-generated mathematics has moved from producing proofs to verifying them, and the model is unreleased, so outsiders cannot rerun it.
