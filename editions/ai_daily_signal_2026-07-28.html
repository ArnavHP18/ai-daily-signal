The chip supplier is now in talks to guarantee the customer's rent

Nvidia is in early discussions to provide up to $250B in financing guarantees so that OpenAI can lease capacity at a planned 10-gigawatt data-centre campus in Ohio — a project whose full cost, chips included, could exceed $500B, making it the largest data-centre development announced to date. The guarantee would cover lease and construction financing only; the chips sit outside it, and a separate discussion of roughly $350B covers financing the Nvidia silicon that would fill the site (CNBC, Tom's Hardware). The site is a decommissioned uranium-enrichment facility roughly 50 miles south of Columbus, with SoftBank involved on the development side; the first 800MW phase is targeted for 2028 (Al Jazeera). The stated reason a guarantee is needed at all is plain: OpenAI does not hold an investment-grade credit rating, and without a backstop the lease would not price favourably. The story was broken by the Wall Street Journal on Jul 26 and carried by Bloomberg, CNBC, Reuters and Al Jazeera through Jul 27; both companies describe the talks as ongoing and subject to change.

So what: your compute supplier, your compute landlord and your compute customer are converging into one credit exposure — if you model AI-infrastructure counterparty risk at all, the diversification you thought you had between Nvidia and OpenAI is thinner than the org chart suggests.

What is actually being discussed. Not an investment and not a purchase — a guarantee. Nvidia would stand behind up to $250B of the lease and construction financing for the Ohio campus, which improves the terms on which OpenAI can commit to capacity it cannot otherwise underwrite. A second, separate line of discussion covers financing for the chips themselves, reported at up to a further $350B. Neither is signed. Every outlet in this scan describes the talks as early and liable to change, and neither company has confirmed terms (CNBC, Quartz).

Why it is the lead rather than the capex thread. This briefing has carried the megacap capex argument for eleven days as a question about whether revenue is growing into spending (Developing D3, and two earnings prints away). The backstop is a different question: not whether the spending is affordable, but who carries the risk if it is not. A vendor guaranteeing its customer's obligations moves the exposure from the buyer's balance sheet to the seller's — which is why Michael Burry's public reaction to the report was "around and around we go" (Yahoo Finance).

The precedent it extends. Nvidia and OpenAI announced a letter of intent in 2025 to deploy at least 10GW of Nvidia systems, with Nvidia intending to invest up to $100B in OpenAI progressively as each gigawatt was deployed (Nvidia newsroom). That structure — supplier capital released against supplier hardware deployed — is the same shape, one order of magnitude larger and now extended to real estate.

What is not established. The exact instrument (guarantee, residual-value support, take-or-pay), the fee Nvidia would charge, the counterparties on the debt, and whether SoftBank's role is developer, landlord or both. Reporting also varies on whether the $350B chip line is incremental to or inclusive of existing commitments. Treat every number here as a reported figure from talks in progress, not a term sheet.

Watch next. Whether either company addresses it on this week's calls — Microsoft and Meta report Jul 29 and the sell-side will ask; whether the structure appears in an Nvidia filing; and whether the Ohio phase-one 2028 date survives contact with the interconnection rules FERC has just ordered rewritten (New #11).

IMPACT 8/10#2 ENTERPRISE · MODEL PROCUREMENT

Chinese open models now carry roughly 60% of US-company token usage on OpenRouter — and the switch is being made on price

The day after Kimi K3's weights went live, the more consequential number is not the parameter count but the routing. Data from the API marketplace OpenRouter puts Chinese models at approximately 60% of token usage by US companies, with six of the top ten models — and all of the top five — coming from Chinese labs including Tencent, Xiaomi, DeepSeek, MiniMax, Moonshot and Z.ai (Fortune, eWeek). Named adopters, on the record: Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong says the company halved its AI spending by moving employees onto Kimi and Z.ai's GLM models; DoorDash CTO Andy Fang says it pushes "lower-level work" to Kimi for "better quality, cheaper cost" while reserving Fable for higher-level functions; Cursor has said Kimi provided the foundation for Composer 2. The arithmetic behind it is blunt — $15 per million output tokens on K3 against $50 on Fable.

Model selection has stopped being an ML decision and become a procurement and data-jurisdiction one — if nobody in your organisation owns the question of which weights serve which workload, the answer is already being set by whoever writes the routing config.

IMPACT 7/10#3 MODELS · OPEN INFRASTRUCTURE

Tuesday deep beat: Moonshot open-sourced the training stack under K3, not only the weights

Alongside the weights and a technical report, Moonshot published the infrastructure that produced them: MoonEP, a high-performance communication library for extremely large fine-grained MoE models; FlashKDA attention kernels, reported at a 1.72–2.22× prefill speedup on H20 GPUs; and AgentEnv, released with kvcache-ai, a distributed platform for running agent environments at scale that powered K3's agentic reinforcement-learning training (tech report, MarkTechPost). FlashKDA was already public; MoonEP and AgentEnv are new. The architectural claim in the report is efficiency rather than scale — roughly 2.5× the intelligence per unit of compute attributed to Kimi Delta Attention, Attention Residuals and MoonEP together, not to parameter count (Geopolitechs). vLLM published a production-scale K3 support preview on Jul 22 (vLLM).

Weights let you run someone else's model; an agentic-RL environment platform lets you train your own — this is the more transferable half of the release, and it is the first time a harness at this scale has been handed over publicly.

IMPACT 7/10#4 POLICY · AI SECURITY

Prompt injection is now running as a live fraud campaign: 4 of 26 models made the payment

Zscaler's ThreatLabz documented two in-the-wild campaigns that plant instructions inside content an AI agent reads. Both used SEO poisoning to rank the malicious page highly, then buried prompt-style instructions where a human never looks — CSS pushing text off-screen, and JSON-LD structured metadata that machines read as trusted context. Campaign one impersonated documentation for a non-existent Python library, requests-secure-v2, and told visiting agents to make a payment to obtain an API key. Campaign two typosquatted the DeFi portfolio tracker DeBank and asserted to agents that the impostor was the legitimate domain. ThreatLabz then ran its own autonomous agent against the sites across 26 LLMs: four executed the fraudulent crypto payment — including versions of Meta's Llama and Google's Gemini — and two failed to classify the DeBank impostor correctly (Zscaler ThreatLabz, Infosecurity).

The trust boundary for a browsing agent is the web page, not the model — if your agents can browse and can move money, those two capabilities need to be separated by something stronger than a system prompt.

IMPACT 7/10#5 POLICY · US STATES

Illinois became the first US state to require independent third-party safety audits of frontier AI

Governor JB Pritzker signed SB 315, the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, on Jul 6. Developers of the largest advanced AI systems must publicly disclose safety practices, report significant safety incidents and maintain compliance processes; the Act also creates confidential reporting channels and whistleblower protections for employees raising safety concerns. The distinguishing provision is the audit: Illinois is the first state to require regular independent third-party safety audits by qualified experts without financial conflicts of interest (Governor's office, WTTW). It passed with bipartisan support. Date caveat: the state's own materials and general coverage give an effective date of Jan 1, 2027, while a Crowell & Moring client alert frames what frontier developers must do before January 2028 — the phase-in should be checked against the enrolled text rather than the summaries (Crowell).

While Washington negotiates a voluntary pre-release framework due Aug 1, a state has made third-party audit mandatory — the US compliance surface is now federal-optional stacked on state-compulsory, and the states are moving faster.

IMPACT 6/10#6 RESEARCH · AI FOR MATHEMATICS

The 87-year-old Jacobian conjecture fell to a counterexample found with Claude Fable 5

At roughly 02:19 UTC on Jul 20, Levent Alpöge posted a Fable-5-assisted counterexample: a polynomial map C³ → C³ with constant Jacobian determinant −2 that is nonetheless not invertible, sending three distinct input points to the same output. The conjecture had been open since 1939. The arithmetic has been independently checked and written up in a verification preprint, and the Secret Blogging Seminar published a worked account the same day (Secret Blogging Seminar). Status, precisely: false for n ≥ 3; the n = 2 case remains open; not journal peer-reviewed. Timothy Gowers called it the first time an LLM had solved a well-known problem he had heard of outside his own area, while noting it is a counterexample rather than the end of mathematics; Terence Tao circulated an alternate factorisation worked through with ChatGPT (The Conversation).

The honest reading is narrow and real: models are now credible at the search-for-a-counterexample end of mathematics — the part that is expensive to find and cheap to verify. That asymmetry, not "AI does maths", is what transfers to your domain.

IMPACT 5/10#11 HARDWARE · GRID POLICY

FERC gave all six US grid operators 60 days to justify or rewrite how data centres connect

On Jun 18, at the Department of Energy's direction, FERC issued six Federal Power Act Section 206 show-cause orders — one per jurisdictional RTO/ISO and its transmission owners — requiring each to explain why its open-access transmission tariff remains just and reasonable for large-load interconnection, or to file revisions. FERC defined large loads as peak demand above 50MW interconnecting to lines above 69kV. Informational reports on how operators will ensure sufficient generation for new and existing large loads were due within 30 days; the tariff filings are due within 60 days — mid-August (McGuireWoods, White & Case). Running alongside it: Virginia's $0.011/kWh data-centre electricity consumption tax took effect Jul 1, estimated at roughly $600M a year.

Developers are routing around the interconnection queue behind the meter while regulators rewrite it — if a 2028 capacity plan assumes a grid connection, mid-August is when the rules governing it get filed.

Four days to the US deliverables, five to EU enforcement — and a state got there first

WHAT CHANGED Escalated: Aug 1 at four days, Aug 2 at five. New this window is a precision that changes who should be worried — the EU GPAI obligations have applied since Aug 2, 2025; what switches on next Sunday is the enforcement machinery that was deliberately held back for a year, and the exposed cohort is mid-market fine-tuners and rebranders who may be legal "providers" without knowing it.

The distinction that matters for planning: the GPAI obligations have applied since Aug 2, 2025; what was held back for a year was the supervision and enforcement machinery, and that grace period is what expires (AI Act explorer). The most exposed are not the frontier labs, which have been engaged with the AI Office for a year, but mid-market firms that fine-tune or rebrand GPAI models and have not asked whether they are, in law, providers.

Two voluntary or supervisory regimes land this week while Illinois has already made third-party audit compulsory (New #5) — if your compliance plan is built around the federal timetable, the binding obligation may arrive from a statehouse instead.
